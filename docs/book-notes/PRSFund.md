## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Forehead subunits: central, glabella, lateral, suprabrow, temporal (Fig. 48.4) [PRSFund p.483]. Golden-proportion subunits: forehead unit = 1a central, 1b temple, 1c eyebrow subunit (Fig. 62.2) [PRSFund p.601].
  - Upper-third reference lines: trichion and glabella [PRSFund p.600].
  - **Layer stack (forehead, superficial→deep), 8 layers**: skin, superficial subdermal fat layer, suprafrontalis fascia, frontalis muscle, retrofrontalis fat, subfrontalis fascia, loose areolar tissue, periosteum [PRSFund p.484].
  - Superficial fat compartments of forehead (Rohrich/Pessa list reproduced): central, middle temporal, lateral temporal [PRSFund p.704].
  - Vascular: supratrochlear + supraorbital (internal carotid via ophthalmic) and frontal branch of superficial temporal artery (external carotid); intersystem anastomoses toward midline [PRSFund p.484, p.625].
  - Sensory: supratrochlear nerve = midline to about 2–2.5 cm lateral; supraorbital nerve from there to lateral brow; auriculotemporal + zygomaticotemporal for temporal forehead [PRSFund p.484].
  - Supraorbital foramen: mean distance from facial midline "ranging from 21.7 to 32.02" (mm implied; varies by population/race/gender); more often a notch than a foramen [PRSFund p.625].
  - Supratrochlear artery is "deeper (supraperiosteal) when it emerges from the supraorbital rim" [PRSFund p.486].

## Brow

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Brow in thirds: inner third starts on vertical of medial canthus and nasal alae; middle third slight curve, highest point coinciding with the sclero-corneal limbus; lateral third straight and progressively thinner [PRSFund p.623]. Tail of brow on vertical of lateral canthus, "or not exceed by more than 3 mm" [PRSFund p.623].
  - Arch peak over lateral canthus (Table 62.1) [PRSFund p.604]; younger women peak at lateral canthus, older can be more medial [PRSFund p.627].
  - **Position**: female middle third 2–5 mm above orbital rim; male at level of orbital rim [PRSFund p.604, p.624, p.727]. Female inter-brow distance 6–7 cm [PRSFund p.604, p.624]. Blepharoplasty chapter: lateral brow should arch above rim in women and "should be 10 mm above orbital rim at mid pupillary line" [PRSFund p.635].
  - Muscles: frontalis (only elevator) vs corrugator supercilii, depressor supercilii, orbicularis oculi, procerus (Table 65.1) [PRSFund p.625].
  - Sentinel vein: 1 cm lateral and superior to the lateral corner of the orbit; frontal branch of CN VII 1 cm lateral and superior to the sentinel vein [PRSFund p.626].
  - Pitanguy line: point 0.5 cm below tragus to point 1.5 cm lateral to tail of brow; frontal branch runs superficial to deep temporal fascia over the zygomatic arch (Seckel's second danger zone) [PRSFund p.596]; facelift chapter: mid-tragus to 1.5 cm superolateral to lateral brow [PRSFund p.707].
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book on fillers; non-surgical brow options listed = botulinum toxin, fillers, microfocused US, RF [PRSFund p.626])
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product/rheology**: (no data in this book)
- **Expected surface response & longevity**
  - Botulinum toxin lateral brow lift: 4–5 mm [PRSFund p.626].
  - 2.5 cm scalp excision → 1 cm brow lift ("strictly mechanical") [PRSFund p.626].
  - Gliding Brow Lift: 2–3.2 mm incisions; hemostatic net removed at 48 h; result shown at 6 months [PRSFund p.623, p.627].
  - Perceptive threshold for brow-position asymmetry: 3.5 mm [PRSFund p.603].
- **Danger zones**: subcutaneous lateral temporal lift — avoid dissection within ~2 cm of the orbit (supraorbital nerve) [PRSFund p.628]; sentinel vein / frontal branch as above.
- **Variation**: Brow falls "approximately 2.5 mm per 20 years" [PRSFund p.607]. Male brow flatter, at rim, denser hair; female medial limit at or below rim, sits higher when young [PRSFund p.627].

## Upper eyelid & periorbital

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Lamellae: anterior = skin + orbicularis; posterior = tarsus + conjunctiva [PRSFund p.630].
  - Orbicularis: orbital (over bony orbit, superficial to corrugators/procerus), preseptal, pretarsal [PRSFund p.630].
  - Upper tarsal plate 10 mm height [PRSFund p.632].
  - Levator inserts onto dermis 8–10 mm above superior tarsal edge; 10–15 mm of lid elevation; Müller's muscle 2–3 mm of lift (alpha agonists give 2–3 mm) [PRSFund p.634–635].
  - Supratarsal crease 7–10 mm from palpebral margin (Table 62.1) [PRSFund p.604]; ideal fold 8–10 mm above lash line with 3–6 mm visible pretarsal skin [PRSFund p.636]; upper bleph incision 6–10 mm above lash line [PRSFund p.637].
  - Preseptal fat: ROOF (upper lid), SOOF (lower lid) — between septum and orbicularis; deep periorbital compartments = SOOF and ROOF below orbicularis [PRSFund p.595, p.633]. Superficial periorbital compartments: superior, inferior, lateral [PRSFund p.595].
  - Orbital (postseptal) fat: upper lid 2 compartments (nasal, middle) separated by superior oblique; no lateral compartment because of lacrimal gland; lower lid 3 (nasal, central, lateral), inferior oblique between nasal and central [PRSFund p.633].
  - Canthal tilt positive: 1°–2° male, 2°–4° female [PRSFund p.604].
  - Asian eyelid: absent/short supratarsal crease, shorter tarsus, descending preaponeurotic fat [PRSFund p.637].

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (deep/superficial fat compartments)

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Cheek golden-proportion subunits: 4a medial, 4b zygomatic, 4c buccal, 4d lateral [PRSFund p.601].
  - **Superficial cheek fat compartments**: nasolabial, medial, middle, lateral (temporal-cheek) [PRSFund p.595, p.703–704]. Medial-malar + nasolabial pads "make up Malar fat pad, important in the definition of 'youthful' cheek" [PRSFund p.703].
  - **Deep compartments**: deep medial cheek compartment and buccal ("fat mouth pad" / Bichat's) [PRSFund p.595, p.705]. Fig. 73.3 reproduces Rohrich & Pessa 2007 fat-compartment map [PRSFund p.706].
  - **Zygomatic retaining ligaments**: originate from periosteum of zygoma body, pass through malar fat pad, insert in dermis; biomechanically the strongest facial retaining ligament, "elongating by a mere 9 mm" [PRSFund p.595]. McGregor's patch = zygomatic cutaneous ligament [PRSFund p.595]. Tendinous attachments from zygomaticus major/minor through malar fat to skin reach the nasolabial fold over maxilla medially = "premaxillary space" [PRSFund p.595].
  - Five-layer model (Fig. 73.2, from Fitzgerald 2018): 1 skin, 2 subcutaneous, 3 musculo-aponeurotic (SMAS), 4 retaining ligaments and spaces (sub-SMAS plane), 5 periosteum and deep fascia; true ligaments = zygomatic, masseteric, mandibular [PRSFund p.705].
  - Medial facial skin supplied by myocutaneous perforators; lateral by fasciocutaneous perforators (divided during facelift) [PRSFund p.706].
  - Facial nerve branches lie below SMAS medial to the parotid [PRSFund p.595]; muscles innervated on superficial surface: levator anguli oris, buccinator, mentalis [PRSFund p.710]; buccal branch = most commonly injured in facelift [PRSFund p.596, p.711].
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: HA safety: slow injection, small amounts, bolus or threading; cannula not a guarantee of safety [PRSFund p.618].
- **Volumes**: Case: 3 cc HA (Restylane Lyft) to cheeks + temples in a 65-year-old [PRSFund p.615]. Otherwise (no data).
- **Product/rheology**: see General.
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: (no region-specific data). "Deep fat atrophies and superficial fat tend to reposition or hypertrophy"; repositioning pulls skin in an inferomedial vector [PRSFund p.606].
- **Danger zones**: Infraorbital artery; facial artery/angular artery (nasolabial region) [PRSFund p.619].
- **Variation**: Middle-third aging: loss of cheek projection, malar bone resorption, attenuation of retaining ligaments, nasolabial folds [PRSFund p.606]. Facial skeleton: bone resorption predominantly middle and lower thirds → recession of overlying soft tissue [PRSFund p.605]; aging skeleton: decreased midface/lower-face height, increased zygomatic-arch prominence, increased facial depth, fat-pad atrophy [PRSFund p.712]. Triangle of youth inverts with age [PRSFund p.605].

## Nose

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Nasal soft-tissue envelope 5 layers: skin, superficial areolar fatty layer, SMAS, deep areolar fatty layer, perichondrium-periosteum; subcutaneous fat concentrated at supratip, radix, lateral nasal wall; thickness varies cephalic→caudal and between patients [PRSFund p.648]. Lower-third skin thicker/sebaceous; upper third thinner [PRSFund p.~656].
  - Blood supply: dorsal nasal artery (ICA/ophthalmic); facial artery → superior labial → columellar artery; **angular artery → lateral nasal branch 2–3 mm above alar groove** (primary tip supply); infraorbital artery → dorsal/lateral skin [PRSFund p.642, p.503].
  - Analysis: ideal tip projection 0.67 × ideal nasal length; 60% of tip anterior to vertical line at upper lip [PRSFund p.651]; supratip breakpoint 2–3 mm above tip-defining points in females [PRSFund p.651]; boxy tip: angle of divergence >30°, domal arc >4 mm [PRSFund p.651]; alar base width = intercanthal distance / 70% of nasal height / one-fifth face width [PRSFund p.651]; tip suture adds max 1–2 mm projection [PRSFund p.~657]; nasal tip deviation perceptive threshold 4 mm [PRSFund p.603].
- **Layer thickness data**: qualitative only — Table 70.1/70.2 ethnic SSTE: leptorrhine (Caucasian/Mediterranean) thin; platyrrhine (African) thick; mesorrhine (Latino/Asian) intermediate; Asian thick with abundant fibrofatty tissue; Hispanic moderate-thick sebaceous; Middle Eastern thick sebaceous [PRSFund p.668–669].
- **Injection plane & technique**: Non-surgical dorsum/tip augmentation with Radiesse shown (Fig. 70.2) [PRSFund p.670]. Dorsal augmentation tiers (surgical grafts): minimal 2–3 mm, moderate 3–5 mm, significant >5 mm [PRSFund p.670].
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product/rheology**: HA indicated for "folds, lips, nose, facial contouring" [PRSFund p.617].
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones**: Nasal region = dorsal nasal, angular, lateral nasal, inferior alar, marginal, facial, columellar arteries (Table 64.3) [PRSFund p.619]. Visual-loss protocol Fig. 64.4 [PRSFund p.620].
- **Variation**: tip droop with age: pyriform/maxillary recession, decreased glabellar angle, loss of internal nasal ligament tone, cartilage density, skin laxity [PRSFund p.607]. Ethnic tables above.

## Lips & perioral

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: upper lip subunits philtrum/lateral/mucosal; lower lip central/mucosal [PRSFund p.601]. Ideal: lip width 40% of lower-face width; upper lip protrusion 3.0–3.5 mm, lower 2.0–2.5 mm; both beyond subnasale–pogonion line; golden ratio ~1:1.618 [PRSFund p.604, p.727]. Female: upper lip 3–5 mm ahead of vertical reference plane, lower lip 2–3 mm behind upper [PRSFund p.729]. Subnasale–stomion = half of stomion–submentale [PRSFund p.728]. Oral commissure deviation threshold 3 mm [PRSFund p.603].
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book) — HA indicated for lips [PRSFund p.617].
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product/rheology**: (no data in this book)
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: HSV-1 reactivation after lip filler 24–48 h, incidence <1.45%; valacyclovir 1 week [PRSFund p.620].
- **Danger zones**: Perioral region = facial artery, superior and inferior labial arteries (Table 64.3) [PRSFund p.619]. Intravascular algorithm (Fig. 64.2): local heat + massage 10 min → hyaluronidase 200–500 U every 60–90 min until resolution, up to 1500 U → ASA 500 mg daily 7 d or clopidogrel 75 mg daily 7 d, sildenafil 100 mg daily until resolution, dexamethasone 8 mg/2 mL IM, pentoxifylline 400 mg q8h 7–10 d, radiofrequency; 24 h surveillance [PRSFund p.619].
- **Variation**: aged lips: loss of fullness/volume, fine lines, "barcode" lines from orbicularis tone loss, thinning subcutaneous fat [PRSFund p.606–607]. Marginal mandibular injury → cannot evert lower lip; cervical branch injury → smile asymmetry with preserved lower-lip wrinkling [PRSFund p.596].

## Chin

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Mentalis (only lower-lip elevator; origin incisive fossa, inserts chin skin), depressor labii inferioris, DAO; mental nerve [PRSFund p.725]. Mental foramen on axis of 2nd premolar (63% of cases), midway between basilar margin and alveolar ridge; accessory foramen 3–7% [PRSFund p.726].
  - Labiomental crease depth ≈4 mm women, 6 mm men [PRSFund p.728]. Riedel's line (upper lip–lower lip tangent should touch pogonion) [PRSFund p.728]. Mentocervical angle 80°–95° [PRSFund p.728]. Chin deviation perceptive threshold 6 mm [PRSFund p.603].
  - Table 75.2 chin harmony: Female—chin 2–3 mm behind chin–lip plane; chin 1–5 mm behind vertical; mentolabial groove 2 mm behind vertical of Sn; Pg posterior to Sn vertical. Male—chin at chin–lip plane; mentolabial groove 1 mm behind Sn vertical; Pg 0.5 mm more posterior than women [PRSFund p.729].
  - Correct only chin protrusion >6 mm or retrusion >10 mm [PRSFund p.604, p.727].
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: chin implant placed "central supraperiosteal, lateral subperiosteal" [PRSFund p.731]. Fillers/fat = non-invasive alternative, office-based, HA reversible; not for chin excess [PRSFund p.730].
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product/rheology**: (no data in this book)
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: Selection by deficit magnitude (Table 75.3 + Q11–12): filler or fat grafting when chin–lip plane deficit <7 mm; fat grafting NOT for >7 mm; surgical augmentation genioplasty when >10 mm under-projection [PRSFund p.730]. Rohrich: fat grafting to chin corrects shape, asymmetry, mild microgenia, labiomental sulcus [PRSFund p.726].
- **Danger zones**: mental nerve [PRSFund p.725]. Jawline region = facial artery [PRSFund p.619].
- **Variation**: aging: loss of chin height, width, projection; dentoalveolar resorption begins in the 30s, increases mandibular angle and decreases chin height [PRSFund p.606–607]; "increased prominence of the chin" in aging skeleton list [PRSFund p.712]. Female triangular chin, male square [PRSFund p.604].

Cervical branch branching point: 15 ± 2 mm along perpendicular from mandibular angle to mastoid-mentum line [PRSFund p.720].

## Neck

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Platysma: origin deltopectoral fascia at clavicle; continuous with SMAS superiorly; covered by superficial cervical fascia; innervated by cervical branch CN VII; supplied by submental branch of facial artery and suprascapular branch of thyrocervical trunk [PRSFund p.719].
  - **De Castro platysma types**: Type I (75%) fibers interdigitate only 1–2 cm below chin; Type II (15%) interdigitate down to thyroid cartilage; Type III (10%) separate throughout submental region (Table 74.2) [PRSFund p.720].
  - **Three neck fat compartments**: supraplatysmal (skin–platysma), subplatysmal (platysma–anterior digastrics), deep (deep to anterior digastric and submandibular glands) [PRSFund p.721].
  - **Six neck retaining ligaments**: lateral SCM-cutaneous, mandibular osteocutaneous (MOCL), mastoid-cutaneous, platysma-auricular/earlobe, submental, platysma-mandibular [PRSFund p.721]. Cervical retaining ligaments (Ch 61): along posterior border of platysma at SCM junction, anterior-inferior parotid, posterior mandibular body; extend 1.5 cm medial to anterior SCM border [PRSFund p.595–596].
  - GAN: Erb's point, McKinney's point 6.5 cm from lower edge of tragus over mid-SCM [PRSFund p.720]; 6–7 cm inferior to tragus on posterior SCM border, parallel to EJV [PRSFund p.711].
  - Submental incision 1.0–1.5 cm posterior to submental crease, 3–3.5 cm long [PRSFund p.723]. Pre- vs subplatysmal fat test: grasp posterior to crease and activate platysma; fat rising = subplatysmal [PRSFund p.723].
  - Neck skin laxity grades I (none) – IV (severe) [PRSFund p.721].

## General & cross-region

### Layer-stack models
- Five critical layers of the face: skin, subcutaneous tissue, SMAS–muscle layer, fascia, facial nerve branches (Fig. 61.2): layer 1 skin+subcutaneous, layer 2 SMAS (continuous with temporoparietal fascia/galea above, platysma below), layer 3 parotidomasseteric fascia (continuous with deep temporal fascia) [PRSFund p.593–594].
- Facelift chapter (Fig. 73.2): 1 skin, 2 subcutaneous (fat compartments), 3 musculo-aponeurotic (SMAS / mimetic muscles / parotidomasseteric fascia), 4 retaining ligaments and spaces, 5 periosteum and deep fascia; plus investing fascia of parotid, masseteric fascia, deep temporal fascia; neurovascular bundles (facial nerve/vessels, parotid duct, buccal fat pads) [PRSFund p.703–705].
- SMAS = thin fascial layer investing mimetic muscles, deep to subcutaneous, superficial to muscles of mastication; continuous with temporoparietal fascia, galea, superficial cervical fascia, platysma [PRSFund p.710]. SMAS has "more lasting retention ability" than skin and "less delayed relaxation following lifting" (rationale for HIFU targeting SMAS) [PRSFund p.613].
- Forehead 8 layers / temple 13 layers (see Forehead/Temple) [PRSFund p.484].
- Skin: dermis mean thickness 1–3 mm; hypodermis thickness "highly variable depending on location, body weight, sex, or age" [PRSFund p.~5].

### Retaining ligaments (history & mechanics)
- Term coined by McGregor 1959; Kaye named McGregor's patch; Furnas 1989 confirmed skin–skeleton attachments [PRSFund p.595].
- True ligaments (Fig. 73.2): zygomatic, masseteric, mandibular; adhesions: temporal adhesion, lateral orbital thickening; septa: superior/inferior temporal [PRSFund p.705].
- Zygomaticocutaneous = strongest, elongates only 9 mm [PRSFund p.595]. Osteocutaneous ligaments do not lose adherence with age, so surrounding laxity/hypotrophy creates the surface features of aging [PRSFund p.605].

### Fat compartments (complete list as given)
- Superficial: nasolabial; cheek—medial (malar), middle, lateral temporal; forehead—central, middle temporal, lateral temporal; orbital; jowl. Deep: deep medial cheek, buccal ("fat mouth pad"); periorbital deep = SOOF, ROOF [PRSFund p.595, p.703–706].
- Aging: "Deep fat atrophies and superficial fat tend to reposition or hypertrophy"; skin pulled inferomedially [PRSFund p.606]. Muscles lose volume/tone but relative pull on skin/fat increases (hyperdynamic lines) [PRSFund p.606].

### Filler physics / rheology (Table 64.1) [PRSFund p.617]
| Property | Definition |
|---|---|
| Cohesion | Force between particles holding them together; depends on HA concentration and crosslinking |
| Elastic modulus G′ | Ability to rebound to original shape under dynamic forces |
| Viscous modulus G″ | Resistance to dynamic forces; higher G″ = thicker gel, more extrusion force |
| Phase angle / tan δ | G″/G′; elastic gels tan δ ≈0, viscous ≈1 |
| Complex viscosity η* (G*) | Resistance to deformation; for most gels G* ≈ G′ |
| Strength | Elastic resistance to static deformation (volumizers) |
| Stretch | Ability to deform/adapt to tissue stretching (superficial use) |
| LVER | Range of stress over which viscoelastic properties stay constant and recover |
| Swelling factor (SwF) | Fluid uptake while staying single-phase |
| HA concentration | mg HA per 1 mL finished product |
- Crosslinking gives mechanical strength and longevity; more crosslinking → higher G′, lower swelling factor; a gel below hydration equilibrium takes up water after injection, one at saturation does not swell appreciably [PRSFund p.616].
- NOTE: no numeric G′ values, no product-by-region table in this book.

### Filler types and longevity (Table 64.2) [PRSFund p.617]
| Component | Duration | Indication |
|---|---|---|
| Collagen | 3–4 months (temporary) | folds, acne scars, dermal contour |
| Hyaluronic acid | 6–12 months (temporary) | folds, lips, nose, facial contouring |
| Polycaprolactone | 1–2 years (semi-permanent) | depressed scars, wrinkles, dermal atrophy |
| Calcium hydroxyapatite | 1–2 years (semi-permanent) | moderate–severe folds, soft-tissue loss |
| Poly-L-lactic acid | 1–2 years (semi-permanent) | facial contouring, volumetric augmentation |
| Autologous fat | 1–2 years (semi-permanent) | facial contouring, folds, scars |
| Polyalkylimide | permanent | replacing facial volume, contour |
| PMMA | permanent | deep defects |

### Fat grafting
- Only qualitative: autologous fat = "1–2 years (semi-permanent)" filler [PRSFund p.617]; in facial palsy, most frequent recipient areas: temporal, periorbital, zygomatic, nasolabial fold, upper/lower lip, chin [PRSFund p.566]; chin fat grafting limited to <7 mm chin–lip plane deficit [PRSFund p.730]; smoking/diabetes → poor graft survival [PRSFund p.727]. NO retention percentages anywhere in the book (breast/gluteal chapters are also non-quantitative on retention).

### Filler complications & safety numbers
- Early: pain, ecchymosis, erythema, edema, type-I hypersensitivity edema, malar edema, Tyndall, hyperpigmentation. Delayed: type-IV hypersensitivity, granulomas, abscess, herpes, biofilm, nodules, atypical mycobacteria, paresthesia [PRSFund p.620].
- HSV-1 reactivation <1.45%, 24–48 h post-injection [PRSFund p.620].
- Intravascular algorithm: hyaluronidase 200–500 U every 60–90 min up to 1500 U; ASA 500 mg/d 7 d or clopidogrel 75 mg/d 7 d; sildenafil 100 mg/d; dexamethasone 8 mg/2 mL IM; pentoxifylline 400 mg q8h 7–10 d [PRSFund p.619].
- Vascular danger zones table (Table 64.3) [PRSFund p.619]: glabellar (supratrochlear/supraorbital/dorsal nasal anastomoses), temporal (STA, middle temporal vein), perioral (facial, sup/inf labial), nasolabial (facial, superior labial, lateral nasal), nasal (dorsal nasal, angular, lateral nasal, inferior alar, marginal, facial, columellar), infraorbital (infraorbital a.), jawline (facial a.).
- Facial arteries list Fig. 73.4 (18 named vessels) [PRSFund p.707].

### Botulinum toxin (Table 64.4) [PRSFund p.621]
- Botox 900 kDa complex, 5 ng/100 U, 2–8 °C 36 mo; Dysport 4.35 ng/500 U, 1:2–1:3 vs Botox; Xeomin 150 kDa, 0.44 ng/100 U, RT 36 mo, 1:1; Myobloc (type B) 55 ng/2500 U, 1:40–1:50; Daxxify 150 kDa, 0.18 ng/40 U, RT 72 mo, 1:2. Lateral brow lift 4–5 mm [PRSFund p.626]. Case total 40 U across five muscles [PRSFund p.615].

### Energy devices (non-invasive) [PRSFund p.610–613]
- RF collagen remodeling target 40–45 °C, 8–12 sessions at 7–10-day intervals; lower frequency = deeper penetration; bipolar depth ∝ electrode spacing [PRSFund p.611].
- MFUS/HIFU: microcoagulation from deep dermis to SMAS; mid–deep reticular dermis up to SMAS; adverse events include fat atrophy (rare) [PRSFund p.613].
- Cryolipolysis: +7 to −15 °C applicator; PAH 0.25–1% [PRSFund p.611–612].

### Facial analysis / proportions / thresholds (relevant to 3D landmarking)
- Golden ratio φ = 1.618 [PRSFund p.600]. Horizontal thirds: trichion–glabella, glabella–subnasale, subnasale–menton [PRSFund p.600–602]. Vertical fifths = eye width / intercanthal distance; nose in middle fifth; Caucasian vs Asian eye/nose widths differ vs intercanthal distance [PRSFund p.602–603]. Longest face: male, Afro-American [PRSFund p.603]. Most ethnically variable subunits: orbital and nasal [PRSFund p.604].
- **Perceptive thresholds of asymmetry**: eyelid position at rest 2 mm; oral commissure 3 mm; brow 3.5 mm; nasal tip 4 mm; chin 6 mm [PRSFund p.603]. Dynamic: lid during blink 33 ms; lid depression/smiling 67 ms [PRSFund p.605].
- Aesthetic-unit map (Fig. 62.2) with 9 units and subunits: forehead (central, temple, eyebrow), nasal, eyelids (lower, upper, lateral canthal, medial canthal), cheek (medial, zygomatic, buccal, lateral), upper lip (philtrum, lateral, mucosal), lower lip (central, mucosal), mental, auricular, neck — defined by "surface topography and three-dimensional qualities based on underlying anatomic structures, superficial and deep facial fat compartments, skin thickness, facial muscle activity, and curvature (flat, convex, concave)" [PRSFund p.601]. Useful as a region-segmentation scheme.
- Preoperative facelift assessment lists "Volumetric facial analysis" as an item but gives no method [PRSFund p.712]. Sources cite Wu et al. 2022 "Quantitative analysis of facial symmetry by using three-dimensional technology" (BMC Oral Health 22:277) [PRSFund p.607] — not summarized in text.

### Aging summary (Table 62.2 + text) [PRSFund p.605–607, p.712]
- Upper third: wrinkles, nasofrontal angle ↑, craniofacial convexity ↑, bi-temporal width ↓, fat-pad hypotrophy/repositioning, brow ptosis (≈2.5 mm/20 yr), superior orbital rim recession.
- Middle third: orbicularis tone ↑, orbital rim recession, senile enophthalmos, lid laxity, eyelash length/thickness ↓, superomedial orbital fat hypotrophy, lateral canthus descent, tarsal laxity, nasojugal groove/tear trough, palpebral-malar junction support loss, crow's feet, cheek projection loss, malar bone resorption, ligament attenuation, NLF.
- Lower third: lip definition/fullness loss, barcode lines, orbicularis oris hypotrophy, maxilla/mandible resorption, dentoalveolar resorption (from the 30s), ligament attenuation, nasomandibular folds, chin height/width/projection loss.
- Skeleton: decreased midface & lower-face height; increased chin and zygomatic-arch prominence; increased facial depth [PRSFund p.712].
- Extrinsic: UVA/UVB → AP-1 → ↓collagen I/III; MMP degrades collagen [PRSFund p.605].

### Surgical numbers (context only)
- Facelift hematoma 7% (1–8%); male 6–8% vs female 3–4% [PRSFund p.597]; alt. 0.2–8%, 70% of all complications; overall men 3.1% vs women 1.3% [PRSFund p.713]. Pre-infiltration 1:400,000 epinephrine, 150–200 cc per side; hemostatic net 5-0, removed 24–72 h [PRSFund p.598]. GAN injury 3–5% [PRSFund p.596]. Nerve recovery case: TENS 5×/day 10–15 min for 60 days [PRSFund p.593].

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch
- Anatomy/boundaries: Transverse facial artery (TFA) runs across lateral cheek; may form loops (Fig. 6, p7).
- Layer thickness data: (no data)
- Injection plane & technique: (no data)

### High-risk zones and their vessels (p2)
- Glabella: supratrochlear a., supraorbital a., cutaneous branches of ophthalmic a.
- Nasolabial fold, nasal tip, alar triangle: facial a., angular a., lateral nasal a.

### Delphi consensus on where ultrasound is required (Velthuis 2025 [25], cited p9)
- HRUD MANDATORY: forehead, glabella, temple, nose, pyriform fossa.
- HRUD RECOMMENDED: lips, lower eyelids, zygomatic arch, chin, pre-auricular regions.
- Authors add: zygomatic & malar regions (transverse facial artery) [32, 33].

### Table 1 — Strategies to mitigate VO risk (p2; adapted from Murray 2021 [12] and Kroumpouzos 2023 [13])
Knowledge
- Know safest injection techniques; 3D anatomy of danger zones, usual vessel paths, main variations.
- Anatomy may be altered by surgery, trauma, scars.
- Be versed in HRUD.
Injection technique
- Use reversible (HA) fillers.
- Avoid epinephrine (masks blanching of VO).
- Cannula bore 27 G or LARGER.
- HRUD before injection or HRUD-guided injection in high-risk / altered-anatomy areas.
- Small linear threads safer than boluses; RETROGRADE safer than anterograde.
- Bolus size < 0.2 mL in any one region, injected slowly, low pressure, correct depth and tissue plane.
- Keep needle/cannula tip in constant motion; revisit area with another pass rather than depositing more.
- Aspiration is not failsafe even when done correctly.
- Targeted digital pressure to compress arterial pathways (prevents retrograde filler movement/embolization).
Observation
- Watch for pallor/mottling in treated area or surroundings, pain/discomfort, visual changes.
- Capillary refill > 3 s = vascular compromise.
Patient instructions (p2): pain, visual changes, pallor, livedo, purple/black discoloration of skin or mucosa,
rashes, skin breakdown -> contact office promptly.

### Table 2 — Indications for HRUD in high-risk areas (p3)
1. Total vascular mapping (patient/provider interest; prior procedures that may have altered vascular anatomy)
2. Clinical suspicion of anatomical variations
3. Before filler procedures in postsurgical patients
4. Focal vascular mapping before filler injection in every patient

### HRUD technique essentials (p3)
- Grayscale probe frequency > 15 MHz plus Doppler.
- Power Doppler: single color, flow intensity not direction, best for small capillaries. Color Doppler:
  red/blue direction coding (blue = toward probe when blue on top).
- Arterial waveform pulsatile with systolic peak; venous undulating with respiration, interrupted by Valsalva.
- Image each structure in two planes (axial/transverse and longitudinal).
- Insonation angle ~60 degrees to vessel course for velocity measurement.
- Planes in which vessels are located and reported: subdermal, hypodermal, intramuscular, SMAS [22-24].
- Report per artery: diameter, depth, location plane, spectral curve.
- Ultrasound can also detect prior filler and estimate degradation and quantity [13].
- Limitations: cost, availability, equipment sensitivity, operator training/skill; mitigates but does not
  eliminate risk.

### Mechanisms of VO (p1-2)
Intravascular embolism, extravascular compression, vascular spasm [4, 5]. CaHA case (Fig. 6) illustrates
compression mechanism; resolved with 300 U hyaluronidase under US guidance even though product was CaHA.

### Anatomical variants catalogued (p4-6, p9)
- Facial artery loops (single/multiple) in nasogenian groove; facial artery superficial to SMAS / more
  external course.
- Persistent-caliber labial artery (upper > lower lip).
- Intercantal vein on nasal dorsum.
- Median perforating artery of mandible (submental <-> sublingual anastomosis).
- Supratrochlear artery anastomosis + medial, superficial (hypodermal) course.
- Transverse facial artery loops; TFA substituting for hypoplastic facial artery -> angular -> dorsal nasal.
- Post-rhinoplasty and post-tumor-resection altered vasculature; scars fix arteries in place.

### Product/rheology usage mentioned (case captions only)
- Supraperiosteal frontal volumization: medium G' HA (Vycross) (Fig. 2).
- Supraperiosteal malar/jaw boluses: high G' HA (Fig. 8).
- SOOF (infraorbital): medium G' HA (Vycross) (Fig. 8).
- Temple: HA at hypodermal plane by cannula, or deep (one-up one-over) near deep temporal artery (Figs. 9-10).

## Assessment
- Quantitativeness: LOW. This is a level-V narrative review with case vignettes. All hard anatomy numbers
  (4 vessel depths, 2 vessel diameters, 2 vessel-to-bone distances) come from single-patient figure
  captions, some of them explicitly anatomical VARIANTS (superficial facial artery, intercantal vein), so
  they should not be used as population norms — only as "this vessel can be this shallow" examples.
- No layer-thickness tables, no volumes per region, no rheology values (only "medium G'" / "high G'"
  qualitative labels tied to plane: medium G' for supraperiosteal forehead and SOOF; high G' for
  supraperiosteal malar/jaw boluses), no surface-response or longevity data, no age/sex/ethnicity data.
- Best-covered regions: temple (planes + one full depth stack skin/deep temporal artery/bone), midface/
  lateral cheek (transverse facial artery hazard and mechanism), nose (epidemiology + post-rhinoplasty
  rule), lips (persistent-caliber labial artery), chin (median perforating artery hazard), forehead
  (supratrochlear variants).
- Directly usable rule-set content: Table 1 safety rules (bolus < 0.2 mL, 27 G+ cannula, retrograde
  threads, no epinephrine, capillary refill > 3 s), the Delphi mandatory/recommended ultrasound region
  list, and the sonographic plane vocabulary (subdermal / hypodermal / intramuscular / SMAS /
  supraperiosteal) for tagging each region's injection plane.
- Follow-up leads with real numbers: [24] Cotofana 2021 (forehead artery plane change), [22] Phumyoo 2020
  (glabella), [14] Velthuis 2021 (standard Doppler positions), [17] Sigrist 2024 (upper third), [29] Wu
  2025 (lip sonoanatomy), [16] Gonzalez 2025 (lip cannula vs needle by US), [35] Khorasanizadeh 2023
  (Doppler variant survey), [40] Toure 2021 (TFA).

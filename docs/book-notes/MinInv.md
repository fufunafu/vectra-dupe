## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: supplied by supratrochlear a. (runs on vertical line from medial canthus, pierces orbicularis and travels on muscle surface), supraorbital a. (pierces frontalis "about 3 cm lateral to the medial canthus and about 2 cm above the supraorbital rim", anastomoses medially with supratrochlear, laterally with frontal branch of STA), frontal branch of STA (MinInv p.3). Supratrochlear artery lies in the subcutaneous fat layer on ultrasound (Fig 8.1, MinInv p.108).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: TSST places "filler with good projection capacity" in the **subgaleal space at the forehead** via 25G × 40/50 mm cannula (MinInv p.15). Ch8: Doppler vascular mapping recommended before forehead filler; "the safest technique involves creating an entry point above the eyebrow" (MinInv p.108). Biostimulators (Table 3.3, p.35): linear threading / cross-fanning with 22–25G cannula, **supraperiosteal (preferred), subdermal**; but PLLA, PDLLA, CaHA are "Not recommended" in forehead (vascular occlusion + nodule risk), PCL "Safe" (Table 3.2, p.34).
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product/rheology**: TSST: "Filler with good projection capacity with relative moldability … deep supra-periosteal injections … areas with thin skin, e.g., periorbital area, perioral area, temple, and forehead" (MinInv p.14).
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: (no data in this book). FOS sign: "overprojected forehead" (MinInv p.148).
- **Danger zones**: supratrochlear a. has direct connection to ophthalmic a. → retrograde embolization/blindness (MinInv p.108). Botox note: hypertrophied corrugator/glabellar muscles ("bulky medial to the eyebrow, creating an illusion of hollowness in the forehead above the brow") increase **filler migration after forehead filler**; pre-treat with toxin (MinInv p.177).
- **Mobility/dynamic**: frontalis extends slightly lateral to temporal crest; frontalis toxin 12–18 U over 6 points (MinInv p.176).
- **Variation**: (no data)

## Glabella

- **Anatomy**: supratrochlear + supraorbital + dorsal nasal (ophthalmic branches) anastomose with facial-artery branches in glabella/dorsum (MinInv p.3). Intercanthal vein connecting both angular veins at medial canthus "reported to exist in about 70% of the population", crosses over procerus, connects to cavernous sinus (MinInv p.5–6).
- **Layer thickness**: (no data)
- **Technique**: filler in glabella "carry a risk of blindness"; corrugator often hypertrophied → toxin first, filler for residual lines later (MinInv p.177). Corrugator originates superomedial orbital rim, runs superolaterally "at an approximate 30-degree angle", inserts dermis of medial brow (p.177). Toxin: 3–4 U/site (3 U women, 4 U men); if muscle thick/bulging above brow, shorten interval "every 4 months instead of 6" (p.177).
- **Volumes / Product / Longevity**: (no data)
- **Danger zones**: as above; toxin ptosis incidence ~1–5% (trial of 264 subjects: 5.4% transient), meta-analysis 1.8%, onset 2–10 days, full recovery 2–3 months (MinInv p.180).

## Brow

- (no dedicated data). Supraorbital a. exit point and the zygomaticoorbital branch of STA "ascends toward the lateral eyebrow end" (MinInv p.3). Cannula entry for forehead filler placed above the eyebrow (p.108).

## Temple

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: STA divides into anterior/posterior branches "at a point about 17.7 mm anterior and 37 mm superior to the tragus near the zygomatic arch", and travels **in the subcutaneous fat**; anterior branch runs under superficial temporal fascia toward forehead (MinInv p.3). Middle temporal vein "about 2 cm above the zygomatic arch between the superficial and deep layers of the deep temporal fascia", diameter reported **0.5 mm to 9.1 mm**, continuous via superficial temporal → supraorbital → superior ophthalmic vein to cavernous sinus (MinInv p.6). Ultrasound layer stack (Fig 8.10, p.113): skin, subcutaneous fat, STF, DTF housing superficial temporal fat pad, temporalis muscle, temporal bone. STA in subcutaneous fat; middle temporal artery in the superficial temporal fat pad (Fig 8.11, p.114). Deep temporal artery (maxillary branch) "may sometimes be in direct contact with the periosteum" (p.112–113); anastomoses between deep temporal and ophthalmic arteries implicated in blindness (ref Sheth 2025, p.110).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no numeric thickness). Toxin-induced temporalis reduction: "approximate 55% reduction in temporalis muscle thickness and a 1.1 cm decrease in head circumference" (MinInv p.183).
- **Injection plane & technique**: three planes listed — subcutaneous fat; **interfascial space (between STF and DTF)**; deep periosteal (needle to bone) (MinInv p.109, p.112). TSST: "Interfascial space at the temple" via cannula with projection-type filler (p.15). Biostimulators (Table 3.3): deep supraperiosteal, interfascial, or subdermal (p.35); all deep biostimulators "Safe" in temple (Table 3.2, p.34).
- **Volumes**: (no data)
- **Product/rheology**: "medium-viscosity" HA; author uses **Lorient No. 4** (G' 338 Pa, G'' 95 Pa, complex viscosity 2,795,776, tan δ 0.28, cohesiveness 0.4237 N) (Table 8.1, MinInv p.111).
- **Expected response/longevity**: (no data)
- **Danger zones**: deep temporal artery at periosteum makes bone-contact needle technique "inherently risky"; MTV "Venous Danger Zone"; cannula in interfascial space may drift into subcutaneous fat or superficial temporal fat pad (MTA) (p.113).
- **Variation**: (no data)

## Upper eyelid & periorbital

- Eyelid skin "thinnest in the human body"; "average skin thickness of the lower eyelid is 0.37 mm to over 2 mm" (Chopra 2015) (MinInv p.41).
- Orbicularis oculi "average thickness of about 1.56 mm and is located 0.88–2.8 mm below the skin surface" (crow's feet context; MinInv p.177). OOM thins with age (loss of fibres, contractility) (p.43–44).
- Crow's feet toxin: 3 sites/side, 2–3 U/site, intradermal or just subdermal (p.177).
- Ptosis after toxin: see Glabella. Apraclonidine 0.5% gives ~1–2 mm lift; 1–2 U into pretarsal orbicularis gives ~1–2 mm elevation (p.181).

## Tear trough & infraorbital

- **Anatomy/boundaries** (Ch4 pp.41–48; Ch10 pp.139–141):
  - Groove subtypes: nasojugal groove, tear trough deformity, palpebromalar groove; caused by atrophy of skin/subcutaneous fat, orbital OOM contraction, malar bone resorption (p.140). Assessment categories (Table 4.4, p.47): preseptal hollow; medial (tear trough groove); lateral (palpebromalar groove); midfacial (negative vector, mid-cheek groove, loss of ogee); fluid/oedema (pitting); "push test to estimate gel volume replacement".
  - OOM: orbital + palpebral (preseptal, pretarsal). "The inferior border of the orbicularis oculi corresponds to the nasojugal groove medially, whilst the tear trough is located between the orbital and palpebral parts of the OOM" (p.43).
  - True tear trough has only three layers: "skin, muscle and bone"; cadaver studies show filler deposited intramuscularly there (p.51).
  - Lower-lid orbital fat: medial, central, lateral compartments; protrusion mostly nasal and central (p.45).
  - Superficial fat: minimal eyelid subcutaneous fat, confined just below orbital rim; superficial malar fat pad supports lower lid. Deep fat below OOM: medial SOOF, lateral SOOF, deep medial cheek fat (DMCF). SOOF split into superior/inferior compartments by the **malar septum** ("impermeable membrane" bounding malar mounds/oedema/festoons) (p.45).
  - Bone: infraorbital foramen "about 6–10 mm below the inferior orbital rim", ~mid-pupillary line; zygomaticofacial foramen "approx. 6 mm beneath the infraorbital rim at the inferior lateral orbital notch" (p.47). Age: orbital floor descends, maxilla rotates/recedes → negative vector, longer lower lid (p.47).
  - Ligaments: ORL defines superior border of SOOF; zygomatic cutaneous ligament its inferior edge; medial ORL = tear trough ligament (p.47). ORL "maintains the lid-cheek junction" (p.11).
  - Vessels: angular a., zygomaticofacial a., infraorbital a. "In the majority of cases, there are no major arteries directly on the infraorbital rim"; detouring angular artery "observed in 30% of Asians", shifted laterally close to the rim; infraorbital–angular anastomoses exist (p.47–48). Small vertical veins over OOM. Inferior palpebral vein: usual course lateral canthus → inferomedial to angular vein; variants 22% undeveloped, 12% beneath inferior IPV, 8% origin beneath both lateral+inferior OOM (p.48). Angular artery "frequently passes through the tear trough area" on Doppler (p.111).
  - Lymphatics: superficial system above preseptal OOM; deep system below it; fewer valves → obstruction-prone (p.48–49).
- **Layer thickness data**: lower-eyelid skin 0.37 mm to >2 mm (p.41); OOM ~1.56 mm thick at 0.88–2.8 mm depth (p.177). Asians: "thick skin and more subcutaneous fat" delays rhytids (p.42).
- **Injection plane & technique** (Table 4.6, p.50; text p.50–52):
  1. Direct tear trough: only thick skin, no/mild bag; 25G cannula; **supraperiosteal**; inject lower part of groove, do not run cannula to medial canthus (angular a./v.).
  2. Preseptal hollow: microdroplet subdermal HA (Restylane Skinboosters Vital Light) with fine needle; high Tyndall/swelling risk; advanced injectors only; all patients had small bumps for first few days (p.51).
  3. Direct infraorbital (palpebromalar groove/SOOF loss): small boluses **in front of orbital septum at supraperiosteal level, between ORL and zygomatic cutaneous ligament**; entry **3 cm below the lateral canthus**, inject below ORL in pre-zygomatic space; beware backflow depositing HA above malar septum (p.52).
  4. Indirect: negative vector → fill SOOF + DMCF (p.52).
  - Ch10 (Landau) for **bulges/fat herniation**: low-G' "soft filler", 0.2 mL transferred to 0.3 mL insulin syringes, 31G 6 mm needle; small aliquots **subdermal** along palpebral-malar transition, skin pulled up away from bone; start medially, first entry 4–6 mm from medial border of herniation; needle visible through skin; lateral part may go deeper to bone (p.141). Kato classification (Japanese): Type 1 hollow w/o negative vector → fill tear trough + palpebromalar; Type 2 (negative vector, no bag on smile) → add mid-cheek; Type 3 (negative vector + bag on smile) → add superficial filler in front of bags (p.140).
  - Cannula 22–25G; needles vs cannulas: no significant difference in safety/outcome per systematic review, but needles bruise more (p.51, p.58). Patient seated, eyes open (p.49–50).
- **Volumes**:
  - Tear trough: "Expert groups do not recommend >0.5 ml volume in each tear trough"; recent publication average **0.26 (0.1) cc [range 0.08–0.32]** (Bernardini 2025) (p.51).
  - Palpebromalar groove: not >1 ml; **0.5–1 ml initially**, larger needs spread over visits with less each time; "volume injected is thought to be a more important factor affecting swelling compared to the brand of filler" (p.52).
  - Overall IO: "Under correct rather than overcorrect the IO area (volumes between 0.2 and 1 ml)" (p.58).
  - Ch10 bulges: **0.6–1.0 ml for both eyes**; aim ≤80% correction; touch-up at 8 weeks usually ≤0.2 ml (p.141).
- **Product/rheology**: "low to moderately cross-linked HA (Belotero Balance, Restylane, Volbella, Redensity2)"; FDA-approved: Restylane Eyelight (20 mg/ml), Juvéderm Volbella XC (VYC-15L, 15 mg/ml); "lower elasticity and firmness is recommended" (p.49); "low-viscosity instead of high-viscosity HA, which are less hydrophilic" (p.58). Landau: relatively low G' soft filler (p.141). TSST: SOOF superficial layer gets "fillers with good tissue integration" (high cohesivity), deep lateral orbital floor gets projection-type filler in 0.05–0.1 cc aliquots (p.15). Table 5.3 values for cited products: Volbella G' 99 Pa; Belotero Balance 21.7 Pa; Restylane 349 Pa (p.70–71).
- **Expected surface response & longevity**:
  - HA is hydrophilic — "complete product 'saturation' with the in-tissue water" takes ~2 months; follow-up scheduled at 8 weeks, rarely earlier (p.141). Cross-linked HA in IO "usually provides long-lasting (more than 12 months) correction" (p.141).
  - Patient satisfaction 83.6% short term, 76.7% long term (systematic review) (p.58).
  - Delayed complications (systematic review 2022): swelling 42.3%, lumps/nodules 25%, xanthelasma-like 17.3%, migration 7.7%, discolouration 3%; **mean onset 16.8 months** (xanthelasma soonest mean 10 months; discolouration latest mean 52 months) (p.56).
  - Migration mechanism: OOM gradually pushes superficially placed/overfilled filler; supraperiosteal HA "can lie within the muscle fibres" and be displaced deep→superficial through gaps in OOM → periorbital oedema (p.57). Swim/ski goggles can push product up (p.144, p.54).
  - Acute swelling/malar oedema common, "generally improves over a few weeks" (p.54). Preseptal microdroplets: small bumps for first few days (p.51).
- **Danger zones**: infraorbital foramen (avoid directly over it); angular a./v. at medial canthus; infraorbital neurovascular bundle deep on bone → superficial injections with tissue retracted are "relatively safe" (p.144); "few arteries run over the periosteum in this region" (p.58); post-septal filler if cannula enters behind septum (keep finger on rim, p.50). Malar septum: filler above it → chronic oedema (p.57). Filler between bilaminar ORL layers → oblong mass (p.57).
- **Variation**: Caucasians fine rhytids/sagging; Asians thick skin, more fat; thin-skin preseptal hollows common in SE Asians, Hispanics; haemosiderin risk Fitzpatrick 4–6 (p.42, p.56). 30% Asians lateral-shifted angular artery (p.48). IO hollows second most requested area in 30–34 y age group (p.41).

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (deep/superficial fat compartments)

- **Anatomy**: superficial midface fat = nasolabial, medial, middle, lateral pads above OOM; deep = medial SOOF, lateral SOOF, DMCF below OOM (p.45). Ch1: superficial fat above SMAS / deep fat below; deep compartment adipocytes smaller; deep compartments lose volume with age, rate varies by region and time (Fig 1.16) (p.9–10). Zygomatic retaining ligament relevant to malar augmentation (p.11). Ristow's space "between the DMCF and the periosteum" (p.110). Parotid duct line: tragus → midpoint between ala and commissure (p.65).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no numeric data)
- **Injection plane & technique**: TSST "X-point" entry between lateral canthus and oral commissure; 25G × 40–50 mm cannula; projection-type filler in **0.05–0.1 cc** aliquots at V-frame deformity (deep fat pad), lateral orbital floor along lateral orbital rim, canine fossa via superficial nasolabial fat pad; then tissue-integration filler superficially in SOOF; all from one entry point (p.15). Ch8 midface: entry "vertically at the lateral canthus level", cannula to tear trough, anterior malar, lateral cheek compartments; identify angular, infraorbital, zygomaticofacial arteries (p.111). Biostimulators: subdermal / supraperiosteal, 27–30G needle serial puncture/depot/microdroplet or 22–25G cannula (Table 3.3, p.35); all deep biostimulators "Safe" in midface (Table 3.2, p.34).
- **Volumes**: only the 0.05–0.1 cc aliquot size (p.15). (no per-region totals)
- **Product/rheology**: TSST deep = projection/moldability; superficial = high cohesivity integration type (p.14). FOS: "Low-viscosity high cohesivity HA fillers are preferred for superficial regions… high projection capacity products suit deep volumizing" (p.149).
- **Expected response/longevity**: MRI review (Master 2024, 33 MRI studies): HA in the mid-face "persist for years", not 6–12 months (p.148). Midface volumization has indirect lifting effect on lower face/jawline; "midface volumization should be prioritized" in some lower-face cases (p.65, p.79).
- **Danger zones**: infraorbital a., angular a., zygomaticofacial a. (p.111).
- **Variation**: TSST effective in Asian and Caucasian but sites adapted (p.23).

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch

- TSST "lateral canthus" placement of integration-type filler laterally (Fig 2.12, p.18); lateral cheek fat compartment treated via lateral-canthus-level entry (p.111). Zygomaticoorbital artery toward lateral canthus (p.3). (no volumes/thickness)

## Preauricular

- Parotid gland superior/posterior to masseter; cannula can drift into parotid during jawline injection → nodules, infection (p.65, p.115). Toxin parotid reduction 20–30 U/side (up to 50 U men), 5 U × 4–6 points; ultrasound-measured thickness decrease at 6 months; effect ~3–4 months (p.183–184). (no filler data)

## Nose

- **Anatomy**: dorsal nasal a. (terminal ophthalmic) emerges at medial canthus, along dorsum, connects to angular a.; retrograde flow → blindness; occlusion → nasal skin necrosis (p.3). Nasal septal artery from superior labial near philtrum, superficial branch → columellar a. (p.5).
- **Technique**: TSST uses 25G × 40/50 mm cannula for nose bridge with "good elasticity" filler (p.15); ultrasound confirms dorsal nasal artery, cannula tracked and dorsum height adjusted in real time (p.108, p.114–115).
- **Volumes/thickness/longevity**: (no data). FOS sign "nasal broadening" (p.147).
- Bunny lines toxin 2–3 U × 3 points (p.178).

## Nasolabial fold

- **Anatomy**: NLF from sagging nasolabial fat compartment, DMCF loss (pseudoptosis), reduced dermal elasticity; lip elevators insert into dermis around NLF (p.110). Facial artery "often courses within the subcutaneous layer" but may be "in direct contact with the bone"; course differs between sides of same face (p.110, p.114). Facial artery runs deep in buccal fat pad, superficial near nasolabial sulcus → angular (p.64). Facial vein ~1.5 cm posterior to facial artery (p.5).
- **Technique**: HA into **Ristow's space (DMCF–periosteum)** "a critical step"; cannula or needle-to-bone chosen by ultrasound (p.110–111). TSST canine fossa via superficial nasolabial fat (p.15). Biostimulators: supraperiosteal upper one-third, subdermis over lower two-thirds (Table 3.3, p.35); all deep biostimulators "Safe" for NLF (Table 3.2).
- **Volumes / longevity**: (no data). Lateral mouth-corner line is an NLF extension (see Perioral).

## Lips & perioral

- **Anatomy**: superior labial a. branches ~1 cm above commissure, runs "0.5–1.2 cm above the vermillion border", deeper than orbicularis oris, close to wet mucosa (p.5). Inferior labial a. along lower-lip vermilion, deep to OOM near wet mucosa, becomes more superficial medially ascending toward dry mucosa (p.5). SLA lies between mucosa and orbicularis — separate from the subdermal target layer (p.104). Ideal lip ratio 1:1.5 upper:lower (p.111).
- **Wrinkle physiology**: upper-lip vertical lines from orbicularis puckering ("accordion-like central compression"); visible generally after age 45 (eye lines in 30s); philtrum sebaceous/vascular density delays them; men fewer (p.94). Multistage wrinkling theory (Fig 7.3, p.95).
- **Injection plane & technique**: goal "thin and broad filler cushion" in **subcutaneous layer just beneath the dermis** replacing atrophied dermal fat (p.99). 23G cannula from mouth corner fanning toward centre (25G too flexible); or needle retrograde along lines from vermilion border, or serial punctures with short needle (microneedling effect) (p.99). Nerve block/topical anaesthesia. Biostimulators must be in/near dermis; PLLA diluted to 6–7 cc perioral (5 cc maximises collagen but more nodules) (p.100); PLLA/CaHA "Not recommended" for lips/marionette, PDLLA caution, PCL safe (Table 3.2, p.34); Table 3.3 perioral plane subdermal (p.35).
- **Volumes**: upper lip lines "about 1 cc of filler per session" (p.99). Mouth-corner lines 0.2–0.4 cc/side (p.99); case: 0.3 cc each side, 1-year follow-up (Fig 7.10, p.100); case: 0.6 cc mouth-corner + 1.2 cc marionette (p.103).
- **Product/rheology**: upper lip needs G' **150–220 Pa (0.1 Hz)** — very soft neck/infraorbital fillers give suboptimal/short-lived results (p.99). Mouth corners/marionette: G' **250–350 Pa (0.1 Hz)** "longer-lasting", thicker skin, stronger compression (p.99). TSST: perioral gets integration-type superficially (p.14). FOS: avoid high volumes in lips ("pillowing", "sausage-like") (p.147, p.149).
- **Expected response/longevity**: biostimulator collagen begins 1.5–2 months post-injection (p.100); ECM supplements (collagen/HA skin boosters) 3–4 sessions at 2-week intervals (p.102). Combination (filler + toxin + booster) recommended over 40s (p.102).
- **Toxin (mobility)**: orbicularis oris 8–10 points along a line 5 mm above vermilion, 1–1.5 U/pt, total ≈12 ± 4 U (p.101); mouth corners 0.5 U × 2–3 pts/side deep dermis (p.101).
- **Danger zones**: dermal plexus just beneath deep dermis (bruising with cannula); SLA separate from target layer; risk "minimal" if just below deep dermis (p.104). Nodule risk near dermis; biostimulator nodules: non-cross-linked HA + RF, multiple sessions (p.104).
- **Variation**: female > male upper-lip wrinkles (p.94); protruded mouths more lateral commissure lines (p.96).

## Marionette & oral commissure

- **Anatomy**: marionette line from modiolus along medial DAO border to mandibular border; upper 1/3 (commissure→modiolus) from modiolus descent (muscle support loss, fat atrophy); lower 2/3 = DAO medial-border dermal insertion (p.96). Labiomandibular fold/cheek-chin crease prominence rises with tissue-thickness difference across it; lateral labiomandibular skin loosely attached → ptosis, medial firmer (p.64). Mandibular ligament at inferior margin of labiomandibular sulcus, anterior to jowl; weakens with age → jowl adjacent to insertion; fold extends to mandibular border → prejowl sulcus (p.64). Contributors (Table 5.1, p.64): maxillary/mandibular resorption, gravitational descent, superficial fat loss/redistribution (jowl), deep labiomandibular fat atrophy, depressor hyperactivity, mandibular ligament tethering. Lateral lower-lip fat compartment atrophy accentuates marionette (p.63). Marionette lies medial to facial artery → low occlusion risk (p.104).
- **Layer thickness**: skin thicker than upper lip (p.99). (no mm)
- **Technique**: subdermal release with cannula before injection (wrinkles adherent to dermis and muscle); do not put large volumes outside the line (adds heaviness/sag) (p.99). Ch5: "soft filler", restore support to oral commissure; combined supraperiosteal + subcutaneous in prejowl (p.73). Botox DAO 2–3 U at 1 cm lateral + 1 cm inferior to commissure, or 0.5 cm lateral to line, on DAO surface; also mentalis (p.101); Ch13 DAO 3–4 U at mid-marionette point on "ee" (p.179).
- **Volumes**: marionette **0.5–1 cc** (p.99); cases 1.2 cc and 0.8 cc marionette (p.103).
- **Product/rheology**: Ch5 guide: marionette → **G' < 200 Pa (0.1 Hz)**, "softer filler with high viscosity" (p.69); Ch7: 250–350 Pa firmer for commissure/marionette (p.99) — the two chapters disagree; note both.
- **Longevity/response**: case 1-year (Fig 7.10); combined filler + threads/HIFU 1-year photos (Figs 5.17–5.18, p.76). After DAO toxin only lower 2/3 corrects (Fig 7.7, p.98).
- **Danger**: DAO too medial → DLI impairment (p.101, p.179).

## Chin

- **Anatomy**: skin thickest over chin (thin posteriorly along mandible, thickens anteriorly) (p.63). Deep mental fat + deep labiomandibular fat compartments (p.64). Mental artery via mental foramen; submental a. along mylohyoid (p.64). Mentalis originates near alveolar bone, descends inferolaterally to chin dermis (p.179). Ideal chin width: women = medial intercanthal distance; men = mouth width. Projection: men chin aligned with lower lip; women lower lip 1–2 mm beyond chin (p.72–73). Aging: mandibular length decrease, chin recession/protrusion, tooth loss (p.62).
- **Layer thickness**: (no mm)
- **Technique**: retrogenia → supraperiosteal (p.72). Layering: deep supraperiosteal first, then superficial fine-tuning (p.72). Cannula entry laterally at prejowl ~5 mm above jawline; pinch to deliver **beneath mentalis**; projection → pogonion; lengthening → menton; even midline/lateral distribution; needle for direct supraperiosteal (p.73). Mentalis toxin adjunct 4–5 U/site × 2 (p.73, p.179); hypertrophied mentalis → more filler migration, augmented chin length shortens faster (p.179). TSST: chin gets "good elasticity" filler in **0.02 cc** aliquots, 27G needle, walk-on-the-bone (p.15). Biostimulators supraperiosteal/subdermal, all deep types "Safe" (Table 3.2–3.3).
- **Volumes**: **0.2–0.5 mL per side** deep; ~**0.1 mL per side** retro-injected subcutaneously for softer contour (p.73). Manual molding after (p.73).
- **Product/rheology**: **G' 200–400 Pa (0.1 Hz)**, "moderate elasticity and viscosity"; excessively firm → unnatural (p.69).
- **Longevity/response**: case photos 3 days / 3 months (Fig 5.14, p.74); 6 months (Figs 5.7–5.9); 1.5 years after touch-up (Fig 5.12, p.72). Contours "exaggerated immediately post-injection but typically normalize over the next 1–2 weeks"; final results "usually visible within 2–4 weeks"; avoid massage 2 weeks (p.77). Females: overfilling prejowl → broader chin appearance (p.73).
- **Danger zones**: mental foramen/artery/nerve (p.64–65).

## Jawline & prejowl

- **Anatomy**: subcutaneous fat compartments: superior + inferior mandibular fat pads (atrophy with age), fat over parotid-masseteric fascia, submandibular compartment, lateral lower-lip compartment (p.63). Posterior to labiomandibular sulcus: platysma beneath subcutaneous fat, parotid-masseteric fascia deep; anterior: DAO, platysma, DLI, mentalis (p.63). Marginal mandibular nerve: anterior to masseter runs above border; posterior to masseter anterior edge "frequently courses **1–2 cm inferior to the mandibular border**" (p.65). "The subdermal plane directly overlying the platysma along the jawline is typically devoid of major neurovascular structures" → relatively safe (p.65). Facial artery crosses inferior border just anterior to masseter insertion; facial vein lateral and deeper, deep to platysma (p.64). Bone: mandibular angle flattens, length decreases with age (p.62).
- **Layer thickness**: (no mm)
- **Technique**: supraperiosteal for structure; target zone from posterior to jowl toward mandibular angle, "parallel to the inferior border of the mandible, within a range of approximately **1.5 cm**" (p.69). Cannula 20G/22G, 40–50 mm, single entry at prejowl point on inferior border, retrograde linear threading (p.72). Needle for direct supraperiosteal (p.72). Prejowl: combined supraperiosteal + subcutaneous fat plane (submuscular + superficial) (p.73). ## Masseter & mandibular angle

- Masseter divided into superficial and deep layers by deep inferior tendon (DIT); toxin into deep layer only → superficial compensatory bulge, fix with 10–15 U (p.183).
- Toxin dose 25–30 U/side, up to 50 U men; 24 U/side (48 U) most cost-efficient; start 50 U total, up to 60 U; sites 1–2 cm above angle, 3–4 points ~1 cm apart, needle to bone then withdraw slightly (p.182). Lower-face max width reduction ~4–8 weeks post (p.182). Onset 2–3 days, peak 1–2 weeks, duration 3–4 months; cumulative reduction >6 months, up to 1–2 years with repeats (p.182). Especially Asians (p.182).
- Submandibular gland toxin 20 U/side (2 × 10 U), gland posterior to facial vein (p.184).
- Filler at angle: see Jawline (needle within masseter fibers onto bone, p.115; TSST 0.02 cc aliquots, p.15).

## General & cross-region

### Layer-stack / compartment models
- Superficial fat above SMAS, deep fat below; compartments vary by region; midface has clear superficial/deep separation, upper/lower face differ; deep-compartment adipocytes smaller; deep compartments lose volume with age, "rate of fat absorption also varies by region and over time" (Fig 1.16); wrinkles localise to specific compartments (MinInv p.9–10). Retaining ligaments: periosteum/deep fascia → dermis; ORL (lid-cheek junction), zygomatic retaining ligament (p.11).
- Facial nerve runs deep to expression muscles under SMAS; temporal branch crosses zygomatic arch anterosuperiorly (p.8).
- Ultrasound: SIBUS-IN finger probe at **15 MHz**, Doppler (CFM) for vessels, B-mode for layers (p.112–113).

### Filler physics / rheology (Table 5.2, MinInv p.69)
- G' = storage modulus (Pa), G'' = loss modulus (higher → more extrusion force), tan δ = G''/G' (gelatin-like ≈0, honey-like ≈1), G* = sqrt(G'^2 + G''^2), "Most HA filler G' > G''", "G* is near G'". Cohesion = resistance to dispersion. Concentration mg/mL; cross-linked HA holds shape, pendant/free HA degrades faster. Low G* → superficial; high G* → deep volume (p.68).
- Site guide (p.69): Jawline G' > 400 Pa; Chin 200–400 Pa; Marionette < 200 Pa (all 0.1 Hz). Ch7 (p.99): upper lip 150–220 Pa; mouth corners/marionette 250–350 Pa.
- TSST three-class system (p.14): (1) projection + moldability → deep supraperiosteal in thin-skin areas (periorbital, perioral, temple, forehead); (2) high elasticity → nose, chin, mandibular angle; (3) high cohesivity/tissue integration → superficial mobile areas (periorbital, perioral) "without migration concerns". Aliquots 0.05–0.1 cc (cannula, midface) and 0.02 cc (needle, T-hollow/angle/chin) (p.15). Tools: 25G × 40/50 mm cannula multilayer; 27G × 40 mm needle pre-periosteal (p.15).

### Product longevity / degradation
- HA: "once assumed to degrade within 6–12 months, persist for years, as shown by MRI studies" (33-MRI-study review of mid-face) (p.148). Infraorbital cross-linked HA >12 months (p.141). HA water saturation complete ≈2 months (p.141). Filler stabilises in tissue over first 2 weeks; final look 2–4 weeks; immediate contours exaggerated, normalise 1–2 weeks (p.77). Non-HA fillers form "permanent fibrotic collagen networks" (p.148).
- Cross-linking chemistry (Ch6): BDDE forms A (conjugated), B (pendant), C (free BDPE), D (raw BDDE); NMR metrics MoD, CrR, CrD = CrR × MoD (p.85). Table 6.1 (p.86) MoD(%,1H)/MoD(%,13C)/CrR/CrD for 13 anonymised fillers — range MoD(1H) 2.239 (Lorient) to 17.065 (5E); CrR 0.014 (8H) to 0.394 (1A); CrD 0.146 (11K) to 4.034 (9I). FDA limit: ≤600 particles >25 μm and ≤6000 >10 μm per syringe (p.86). Delayed inflammatory reaction defined as inflammatory signs 2–3 weeks after HA injection (p.83).
- Biostimulators (Ch3): PLLA type I collagen significant increase by 3–6 months; new type III detectable ~4 months (p.27); PDLLA total collagen by week 4, abundant type I by 8–12 weeks (animal) (p.27); CaHA collagen deposition within 1 month, increase 4–78 weeks (canine), dermal density up ≥1 year; particles 25–45 μm (p.28); PCL persists longest, breaks down over 2–4 years, clinical results ~3 years+ (p.29); PCL vs CaHA equal collagen at 2 and 4 months, PCL more fibroblasts at 4 months (p.29). Collagen synthesis begins 1.5–2 months (p.100). Intermediate-onset papules 1–3 mm, resolve within 3 months; late-onset >2 years may persist for years (p.36). Sessions minimum 30-day interval; mild 1 session, moderate 2–3, severe ≥3 (Table 3.3, p.35). Table 3.1 (p.31–33) dilution: Sculptra 150 mg PLLA + 90 mg CMC + 127.5 mg mannitol, 40–63 μm, 5 mL SWFI (old) or 8 mL SWFI + 1 mL lidocaine (9 mL total), hydrate ≥2 h up to 72 h; Aesthefill 154 mg PDLLA + 46 mg CMC, 30–70 μm, 7–8 mL SWFI; Juvelook 42.5 mg PDLLA + 7.5 mg HA, 10–40 μm, 8 mL; Lenisna 170 mg PDLLA + 30 mg HA, 40–80 μm, face 16 mL / body 20 mL; Radiesse 30% CaHA/70% gel, 25–45 μm, optional 1:2–4 dilution; Ellansé 30% PCL, 25–50 μm; Gouri 21% (210 mg/ml) solubilised PCL; PN 10–20 mg/mL ready-to-use.
- Botulinum toxin: effects diminish ~3–4 months, some 4–6 months (p.171); onset 2–3 days, peak 1–2 weeks (p.182). Reconstitution 100 U in 2 mL → 0.02 mL = 1 U; 31G 8 mm insulin needle (p.174–175). Toxin then filler when combined, "typically around 12–30 U" for combined face treatments (p.175).

- Table 9.1 (p.120): Canfield Vectra H2 + Visia (face reveal, RBX; spots/wrinkles/texture/pore; simulate 3D); QuantifiCare LifeViz Mini; Miravex Antera 3D (full 3D; wrinkle/texture/pore/volume); FotoFinder ATBM; Aram Huvis AI Scalp Grader. ### Complications & safety (cross-region)
- Vascular occlusion protocol: hyaluronidase high-dose repeated, warm compress/vasodilators, HBOT, aspirin/nitroglycerin/LMWH (p.78). Hyaluronidase for FOS 1500 IU in lidocaine via 30G, repeat sessions for cross-linked HA (p.149). Do not inject within 2 weeks of vaccines/dental work (p.53). Bruising: pulsed-dye laser within 24–48 h (p.53). Malar oedema: oral antihistamines/steroids, low-dose intralesional triamcinolone (p.53–54).
- Facial artery course variable and asymmetric; needle-on-bone only when artery confirmed off bone (p.114).

### Aging volume-loss numbers
- No quantitative fat-compartment volume-loss data in this book (Gierloff CT studies cited only by reference, p.12, p.80). Qualitative only: superior/inferior mandibular fat pads atrophy; deep labiomandibular/deep mental fat atrophy; SOOF/DMCF loss → tear trough, palpebromalar, mid-cheek grooves (p.45, p.63–64).

## Assessment

Quantitativeness: moderate. This is a technique/complication book, not an anatomy atlas. Its strongest numeric content is (1) the 50-product HA rheology table (Table 5.3, G'/G''/G*/tan δ/viscosity/cohesiveness/particle size/injection force at 0.02 Hz), (2) region-specific G' targets at 0.1 Hz (jawline >400, chin 200–400, marionette <200, upper lip 150–220, commissure/marionette 250–350 Pa), (3) infraorbital volume ceilings (≤0.5 mL/tear trough, mean 0.26 cc, ≤1 mL palpebromalar, 0.2–1 mL overall, 0.6–1.0 mL both eyes subdermal) and delayed-complication statistics, (4) chin/perioral per-side volumes (chin 0.2–0.5 mL + 0.1 mL; commissure 0.2–0.4 cc; marionette 0.5–1 cc; upper lip ~1 cc), (5) botulinum toxin doses per muscle with a few muscle-thickness numbers (OOM 1.56 mm at 0.88–2.8 mm; temporalis −55%), and (6) biostimulator dilution and collagen-timeline tables. Soft-tissue layer thickness in mm is almost absent (only lower-lid skin 0.37–>2 mm and OOM). No fat-compartment volume-loss quantities, no 3D-imaging outcome numbers, and no per-mL surface-lift data. HA longevity: only qualitative ("persist for years" by MRI; IO >12 months; water saturation ~2 months; swelling normalises 1–2 weeks). Best-covered regions: tear trough/infraorbital (Ch4, Ch10), lower face — chin/jawline/prejowl/marionette (Ch5), perioral (Ch7), temple layering (Ch8). Weakest: forehead, brow, nose, lateral cheek, neck (largely vessel-safety prose only).

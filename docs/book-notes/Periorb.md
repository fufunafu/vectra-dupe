## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: forehead = hairline to superior orbital rim; bony landmarks frontal bone centrally, zygomatic + temporal bones laterally (Periorb p189, Ch19). Forehead height (top of eyebrows to frontal hairline) should ideally be one-third of face height; distance top-of-eyebrow to frontal hairline generally 5–6 cm; eyebrow to corneal light reflex 2.5 cm (Periorb p44, Ch4). Lateral canthus to temporal hairline typically 4.5 cm in a youthful face (Periorb p44, Ch4).
- **Layer thickness data**: "The depth of the frontalis muscle can vary from 2 to 7 mm, but in most subjects, it lies about 3.5 mm to 5 mm underneath the skin [4]" (Periorb p189, Ch19). Frontalis enveloped by galea aponeurotica; fibers terminate at or before the temporal ridge; frontalis does not extend temporally (lateral brow ptosis more prominent) (Periorb p189-190, Ch19). Cadaver study (Choi You-Jin): lateral border of frontalis lies along the "L2 line" ≈ temporal crest; in a few patients fibers extend lateral to temporal crest (Periorb p73-75, Ch6).
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no filler data in this book for forehead). Botulinum: see Glabella/Brow. MFU above eyebrow: shots in 1–3 layers at depths 4.5 mm, 3 mm, 1.5 mm targeting "orbital ligament, frontalis fascia, dermis"; 25–50 shots/side (Periorb p142, Ch13). Endoscopic lift note: "the deeper the plane of dissection, the more effective and durable the lift effect"; superficial forehead dissection ↑ neuropathy/bleeding (Periorb p183, Ch18).
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product/rheology**: (no data)
- **Surface response & longevity**: (no data)
- **Danger zones**: Supraorbital nerve exits ≈ 27 mm from midline (at supraorbital notch); supratrochlear nerve ≈ 17 mm from midline (Periorb p190, Ch19; p193, Ch19). Deep branch of supraorbital nerve runs along deep galea ≈ 0.5–1 cm parallel to superior temporal line (Periorb p8, Ch1). Supraorbital notch on medial third of superior rim; may calcify into a foramen (Periorb p1, Ch1). Supraorbital a. (from ophthalmic) exits notch/foramen, supplies lateral forehead; supratrochlear a. (terminal branch of ophthalmic) supplies medial forehead, anastomoses with contralateral + ipsilateral supraorbital (Periorb p7, Ch1). "Retrograde injection of cosmetic fillers, especially in supraorbital or supratrochlear artery, can reach ophthalmic artery and even cause obstructions in central retinal artery. Due to abundant anastomoses in this region, injections even in superficial temporal sub-branches can lead to the same outcome" (Periorb p7, Ch1). Frontal branch of facial nerve: line from 0.5 cm inferior to tragus to 1.5 cm above lateral eyebrow (Pitanguy line); ~2 cm safe zone from lateral canthal angle; typically courses 2 cm above the brow (Periorb p180, Ch18; p190, Ch19).
- **Variation**: Men: frontal bone more vertically oriented, more prominent supraorbital rim; frontal bone enlarges/broadens with age, glabellar angle flattens, superomedial and inferolateral orbital rim thin/flatten (Periorb p15 Ch2; p180 Ch18; p190 Ch19). Superior orbital rim most projected due to frontal sinus expansion esp. adult males (Periorb p1, Ch1).

## Glabella

- **Anatomy**: Procerus from nasal bone → skin above glabella (horizontal lines); corrugator from nasofrontal suture → skin above superciliary arch (vertical "11" lines); depressor supercilii; supratrochlear nerve pierces corrugator (Periorb p3-4, p8, Ch1). Glabellar complex = corrugator, procerus, depressor supercilii (Periorb p189, Ch19).
- **Layer thickness**: (no data)
- **Injection plane/technique**: Filler: none. Endoscopic lift adjunct: "subperiosteal glabellar fat grafting" after corrugator disinsertion to give glabellar fullness and prevent reinsertion; fat harvested from excised temporal scalp subcutis (Periorb p184, Ch18). Corrugator excision/incision may cause "esthetically unpleasant glabellar depression" (Periorb p184, Ch18).
- **Volumes / Product / Longevity**: (no data)
- **Danger zones**: supratrochlear artery/nerve; retrograde embolism to ophthalmic a. (Periorb p7, Ch1). Dorsal nasal artery emerges between supraorbital notch and medial canthal ligament (Periorb p1, Ch1).
- **Dynamic factors**: Males have more procerus activity → "U pattern" glabellar wrinkle; females "11 pattern" (Periorb p13-14, Ch2). Botulinum: 5 U Dysport upper medial point (depressor supercilii) for medial lift; upper central 1–2 points 2–6 U Dysport (Fig 6.3, Periorb p72, Ch6). Toxin onset ≈72 h, duration 3–8 months (Periorb p34, Ch3).

## Brow

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: Eyebrows span 5–5.5 cm horizontally, 1.3–1.5 cm vertically; medially start at supraorbital rim on line lateral nose–medial canthus, end laterally on line nasal ala–lateral canthus; both ends on same horizontal (Periorb p4, Ch1; p44, Ch4). Females: brow 0.5–1 mm above superior rim (Ch1 p4) / "1 cm above the supraorbital rim" (Ch4 p44) / peak "0.5–1 cm above the supraorbital rim at junction of middle and lateral third" (Ch18 p180); peak between lateral limbus and lateral canthus, 3 mm above orbit (Periorb p4, Ch1); lateral tail 1–2 mm above medial head (Periorb p180, Ch18). Males: flatter, at rim level (Periorb p4 Ch1; p44 Ch4; p180 Ch18; p189 Ch19). Brow ptosis = <10 mm brow-to-eyelid (Periorb p4, Ch1). Vertical skin distance upper lid margin → eyebrow = 20 mm (with MRD1 ≈5 mm) (Periorb p44, Ch4). Lid crease centre → lower brow margin should be 3–5× the tarsal platform show (Periorb p46, Ch4).
- **Fat/ligaments**: ROOF (retro-orbicularis oculi fat) = "upper galea fat pad lies just under the skin, covered by deep galea", more noticeable laterally; with age moves anteriorly and inferiorly along with pretarsal fat → tension on orbital ligaments/deep galea → frontalis contracture + temporal lid fullness (Periorb p5, Ch1). Sub-brow fat pad = extension of ROOF, gliding cushion between mimetic muscles and periosteum; its gravitational/senescent descent compounds dermatochalasis (Periorb p179-181, Ch18). Lateral upper-lid bulge usually ROOF excess/descent or lacrimal gland prolapse rather than preaponeurotic fat; >10% of upper bleph patients had clinically significant lacrimal gland prolapse, half missed preop (Periorb p47, Ch4). Conjoint tendon: "5 mm wide band of tight adhesion" over temporal crest (Periorb p180, Ch18). Orbicularis retaining ligament over lateral orbital rim must be released for temple/brow lift (Periorb p184, Ch18).
- **Layer thickness**: frontalis depth 2–7 mm (usually 3.5–5 mm) — see Forehead (Periorb p189, Ch19). No skin/fat mm for brow.
- **Injection plane & technique (fat graft, Ch8)**: "a person only needs 1–3 milliliters (mL) of fat to be placed on the periosteum in the upper part of the eyebrow, deep to the orbicularis oculi muscle" (Periorb p91, Ch8). Technique: "to add volume to each eyebrow, 1–2 mL fat is slowly injected on the periosteum and below the orbicularis in the lateral half of the eyebrow" via entry point (a) along the lateral canthus (Periorb p92, Ch8). Blunt cannula. Filler HA for post-browpexy dimples (Periorb p241, Ch23). MFU: 25–50 shots/side, 1–3 layers at 4.5/3/1.5 mm (Periorb p142, Ch13).
- **Volumes**: fat 1–3 mL per brow (p91) / 1–2 mL lateral half (p92), Ch8.
- **Product/rheology**: (no data)
- **Expected response / longevity**: Fat: "fat cells seem to last for 7–10 years [13]"; inject less than seems adequate; revision/refill session expected; "fat remains wherever it is injected"; edema resolves by end of a week, ecchymosis up to 3 weeks (Periorb p90-92, Ch8). Surgical brow lift: brow "can fall a few millimeters in the weeks following surgery" (Periorb p194, Ch19). Endoscopic lift outcomes stable ≥5 years (Periorb p185-186, Ch18).
- **Danger zones**: supraorbital n./a. at notch 27 mm from midline; frontal branch CN VII 1.5 cm above lateral brow; sentinel vein near "swirl" of deep temporal fascia split marks proximity of frontal branch (Periorb p184, Ch18).
- **Variation**: Asian: thicker brow skin, brows slightly higher (Periorb p5, Ch1; p181, Ch18). Aging: lateral brow descends most; senile receding of superior orbital rim aggravates lateral hooding; some individuals' medial/central brow rises with age (Periorb p45 Ch4; p181 Ch18). Loss of temporal fat causes lateral brow droop (Periorb p91, Ch8).

## Temple

- **Anatomy**: Deep temporal fascia fuses with frontal periosteum at temporal ridge; splits inferiorly into intermediate + deep layers around Yassergil's (interfascial) fat pad 1–2 cm above superior orbital rim, merging at zygomatic arch; superficial temporal fascia (= SMAS/galea continuum) carries frontal branch CN VII (Periorb p180, p184, Ch18). Sentinel vein lateral to the fascial "swirl"; zygomaticotemporal sensory nerve may run a similar path (Periorb p184, Ch18).
- **Layer thickness**: (no mm data)
- **Injection plane & technique**: Fat: "loss of fat in the temporal region causes drooping of the lateral brow and can be corrected by placing the fat on the superficial temporal fascia" (Periorb p91, Ch8); "The temporal area will need 2–3 mL fat to be placed on the temporalis fascia" via lateral-canthus entry point (Periorb p92, Ch8). (Note book says "superficial temporal fascia" on p91 and "temporalis fascia" on p92.)
- **Volumes**: 2–3 mL fat per temple (Periorb p92, Ch8).
- **Product / rheology / longevity**: (no filler data). Fat 7–10 yr cell lifespan (p90).
- **Danger zones**: frontal branch CN VII within superficial temporal fascia crossing arch; sentinel vein; "injections even in superficial temporal sub-branches can lead to" retinal artery occlusion (Periorb p7, Ch1). Frontal branch of superficial temporal artery supplies part of upper eyelid (Periorb p7, Ch1).
- **Variation**: (no data)

## Upper eyelid & periorbital (superior sulcus, crow's feet, lateral canthus)

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: Interpalpebral fissure 30–31 mm wide × 10–11 mm high, decreasing to ~8–10 mm in older patients (Periorb p4, Ch1). Upper lid margin 0.5–1 mm below upper limbus; MRD1 normal 4–5 mm; MRD2 5–6 mm; vertical palpebral aperture 10–11 mm (Periorb p5 Ch1; p46 Ch4). Ptosis usually MRD1 <3 mm (p46, Ch4). Levator function: ≥13 mm excellent, 8–12 good, 5–7 moderate, ≤4 poor (Periorb p46, Ch4); Ch17 grading: normal ≥15, excellent 12–14, good 9–11, fair 5–9, poor <4 mm (Periorb p171, Ch17). Levator muscle ≈36 mm, aponeurosis 14–20 mm (Periorb p170, Ch17). Upper tarsus 25–30 mm wide × 10–12 mm high; lower tarsus 3–5 mm high (Periorb p6, Ch1); Asian tarsus 4 vs 10 mm (Periorb p45, Ch4). Upper lid crease 8–10 mm above margin in Caucasian females, 6–8 mm in males (eyes closed) (Periorb p45 Ch4; p146 Ch14); tarsal platform show 3–6 mm in relaxed forward gaze (Periorb p45-46, Ch4). Leave ≥20 mm skin between brow cilia and crease in blepharoplasty (Periorb p146, Ch14; p193, Ch19). Eyelid skin "lacks subcutaneous fat" — "the skin below the eye is the thinnest in the body" (Periorb p79, p82, Ch7); "covered by a cutaneous epidermis without any subcutaneous fat layer" (Periorb p4, Ch1).
- **Fat pads**: Upper lid: 3 fat pads — preaponeurotic (behind septum), pretarsal, ROOF (Periorb p5, Ch1). Aging: "upper lid nasal fat pad becomes more prominent with age, and the middle fat pad atrophies resulting in relative bulging medially and hollowing superiorly [24]" (Periorb p15, Ch2). A-frame deformity = hollow superior sulcus (Periorb p47, Ch4). Deep superior sulcus (DSS) post-bleph treated with HA filler ("immediate results and can be adjusted") or fat grafting (Periorb p237-238, Ch23).
- **Lateral canthus**: Lateral Canthal Tendon ≈1 mm thick × 3 mm wide × 7 mm long; Whitnall's tubercle ≈2.9–0.8 mm inside orbital rim of zygoma; lateral canthus 1–2 mm higher than medial, <1 cm from lateral orbital rim; canthal angle ≈6° to horizontal, ≈60° total span (Periorb p153-154, Ch16). Intercanthal distance ≈ horizontal palpebral fissure length (Periorb p49, Ch4). Ageing: aperture becomes more circular, lateral canthus migrates medially/downward (Periorb p154, Ch16).
- **Layer thickness**: (no mm data beyond "no subcutaneous fat")
- **Injection plane & technique**: Fat graft upper sulcus: "the most difficult area to correct, is carefully performed with 1–2 mL fat behind the septum" (Periorb p92, Ch8); only fill sulcus if patient had fuller sulcus when young (Periorb p91, Ch8). Filler: HA with "low viscosity, low concentration, and high malleability is recommended for periorbital area"; "products with a higher elastic modulus (G') are not recommended in thin areas such as periorbital region" (Periorb p81, p84, Ch7). Botulinum crow's feet: 3–5 superficial points ≈1 cm from lateral orbital rim or 1.5 cm from lateral canthus; 10–30 U Dysport/side (= 4–12 U Botox); above Frankfurt line (Periorb p73, Ch6). Fig 6.3 dose map (Dysport): upper medial 5 U; upper central 2–6 U; upper lateral 6–8 U; lateral (classic) 15–30 U; lower lateral 2–3 U (risk lymph stasis, not below Frankfurt line); lower central 2–3 U (high risk, sagging); medial 1–2 U superficial; pretarsal limbus line 1–2 U; mid-pupillary pretarsal 2 U (Periorb p72, Ch6). Lateral brow elevation via 1.25–2.5 U Botox 2 cm above orbital rim laterally (Periorb p35, Ch3). MFU under eye: 15–30 shots/side, 1–2 layers targeting septum/orbicularis fascia/dermis (Periorb p142, Ch13).
- **Volumes**: fat 1–2 mL per upper sulcus (Periorb p92, Ch8).
- **Surface response & longevity**: toxin 3–8 months (p34 Ch3); MFU swelling subsides within 72 h (p143 Ch13).
- **Danger zones**: Frontal branch of superficial temporal artery to upper lid — "one of the most vulnerable arteries during blepharoplasty" (Periorb p7, Ch1); marginal arcades (superior/inferior) from medial + lateral palpebral arteries "very susceptible to bleeding" (Periorb p6, Ch1). Lacrimal artery lateral. Toxin diffusion to levator → ptosis (Periorb p3, Ch1).
- **Variation**: Asians: no upper crease in ~50%; septum-levator fusion below superior tarsal margin; pretarsal & suborbicularis fat more prominent → fuller, puffier lids; epicanthal fold types (Periorb p5 Ch1; p45 Ch4; p181 Ch18). Males more pronounced periorbital changes, thicker skin at all ages, increased lower-lid sagging (Periorb p12, Ch2). Asian rhytid onset delayed ~10 years vs Caucasian but more prominent periocular rhytids; darker skin → thicker dermis, midface volume loss as first aging sign (Periorb p13, Ch2). Bell's phenomenon absent in 25% of population (Periorb p47, Ch4).

## Tear trough & infraorbital (lower lid, lid-cheek junction)

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: Lower lid: 3 fat pockets (lateral, medial, central), separated from malar fat by SMAS; inferior oblique between medial and central pads (Periorb p5 Ch1; p150 Ch15; p200 Ch20). Lower-lid layers ≥5 mm from margin: skin, orbicularis, orbital septum, orbital fat, retractors, conjunctiva; within 5 mm of margin: skin, orbicularis, tarsus, conjunctiva (Periorb p199, Ch20). Septum originates at arcus marginalis; in lower lid it joins capsulopalpebral fascia 5 mm inferior to tarsal plate (Periorb p5 Ch1; p200 Ch20). Lower lid covers inferior limbus or 1 mm of cornea; lower scleral show normal variant in up to 10% (Periorb p47, Ch4). Distraction test: normal lid cannot be pulled >7 mm from globe; canthal tendons allow <2 mm displacement (Periorb p48, Ch4).
- **Ligaments**: Double-convex deformity = superior bulge of prolapsed orbital fat + inferior descended midface/cheek soft tissue "separated by the firm attachments of the orbicularis oculi muscle to the orbital rim periosteum along the tear trough ligament and orbicularis retaining (orbitomalar) ligament" (Periorb p48, Ch4). ORL (orbitomalar ligament): "osseo-cutaneous structure originates from periosteum near the orbital septum and encircles the lower orbital rim"; "there is descent and deflation, yet the ORL will maintain its connection and become increasingly visible as grooves of the eyelid-cheek interface" (Periorb p200, Ch20). ORL is disinserted in midface lift to connect preseptal and preperiosteal planes (Periorb p203, Ch20). Orbital septum weakness + fat atrophy → steatoblepharon, lacrimal gland prolapse (Periorb p15, Ch2).
- **Bony**: Orbital + midface bone resorption deepens the tear trough (Periorb p15, Ch2). Negative orbital vector: cornea anterior to vertical line touching infraorbital rim = "relative hypoplasia of maxillary bone at the inferior orbital rim" (inherent or senile) → ↑ lower-lid retraction risk after surgery (Periorb p49, Ch4; p150, Ch15). Orbital volume 24.5 ± 3.08 cc (slightly larger in males/right side); globe ≈7.2 cc, diameter 24 mm (Periorb p1, Ch1).
- **Layer thickness data**: only qualitative: "thin eyelid that lacks subcutaneous fat, making irregularities more visible" (Periorb p79, Ch7); "the skin below the eye is the thinnest in the body and lacks subcutaneous fat" (Periorb p82, Ch7). Very thin lower-lid skin → dark circles via visible orbicularis/vessels/melanin/hemosiderin (Periorb p44, Ch4).
- **Injection plane & technique (HA filler, Ch7)**:
  - Patient marked seated (tear trough + lid-cheek junction). Topical anesthetic 20–30 min; ice; chlorhexidine 2–4% or 70% IPA (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - "HA is then injected deep in the juxtaperiosteal plane, in order to reduce visibility of the filler ... this is the only area where it is advisable to inject deep under the muscular plane in the juxtaperiosteal plane" (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - Key points: "injected at the supraperiosteal level and underneath the orbicularis oculi muscle, along the orbital rim"; "Blunt-tipped cannulas are preferred as they are safer and less painful while produces less edema"; care at infraorbital foramen; avoid overcorrection; avoid strong massage (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - Needle/cannula placed perpendicular to skin, advanced to top of nasojugal fold; "Parallel threads of the filler are injected cephalad and caudal to the tear trough"; direction changed if trough deep; retrograde deposition on withdrawal; light massage with cotton tip (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - Low injection pressure, small volumes per pass; "Inject 0.1–0.2 mL of product" per pass; aspirate; inject slowly; stop on resistance (Periorb p81, p83, Ch7).
  - MD Codes (de Maio) three-point technique, all supraperiosteal: Tt1 central infraorbital, Tt2 lateral infraorbital, Tt3 medial infraorbital (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - Follow-up at 15 days; touch-ups possible; ice 24 h; no strenuous activity 24 h (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - Fat graft: entry point (b) at intersection of lateral canthus line and nasal ala for tear trough + palpebromalar groove; blunt cannula on periosteum (Periorb p92, Ch8, Fig 8.3/8.4).
  - PRP plasma gel: supraperiosteal "1 cm under the orbital rim" via serial puncture 0.2 cc/point or push technique with 22-G needle; liquid PRP intradermal 1.5–2 mm depth at 1 cm intervals (Periorb p96, Ch9).
  - Mesogel: intradermal, 30–32 G, 4–8 sessions (Periorb p86, Ch7).
  - Surgical alternative (minced orbital fat grafts to tear trough during transconjunctival bleph; fat repositioning over rim with subperiosteal pocket ≈2 cm down face of maxilla) (Periorb p10 ref 13 Ch1; p150 Ch15).
- **Volumes**:
  - "Most patients require 0.2–0.3 mL per eyelid" (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - MD codes: Tt1 0.2 mL, Tt2 0.2 mL, Tt3 0.1 mL per side (total 0.5 mL/side); "the amount could be doubled based on the judgment of the physician" (Periorb p82, Ch7).
  - Fat: "Depending on the hollowness of the tear trough and the palpebromalar grooves, 2–3 mL fat can be injected on the periosteum for each area" (Periorb p92, Ch8).
  - Plasma gel: 0.2 cc per injection point (Periorb p96, Ch9).
- **Product/rheology**: HA is "the most suitable filler for treating periorbital region"; recommend "hyaluronic acid with low viscosity, low concentration, and high malleability"; higher G' products "not recommended in thin areas such as periorbital region"; definitions: G' elasticity, G'' viscosity, G* viscoelastic modulus (higher G* = more lift); cohesivity = capacity not to dissociate, higher cohesivity gels "uniformly infiltrate the tissues and are not fractionated by movements" (Periorb p81, p84, Ch7). Product named in MD-codes ref: VYC-15L (Volbella) (Periorb p87 ref 12, Ch7). No numeric G' values.
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: No mL→mm data. Complications listed: ecchymosis/swelling, irregularities, Tyndall effect (too superficial; treat with hyaluronidase, firm massage, or stab excision), nodules, puffiness "of the lower lid extending into the malar region ... compromised lymphatic drainage" (malar edema; ref: periorbital venous stasis on duplex ultrasound), asymmetry, blindness, necrosis (Periorb p82-84, Ch7). Mesogel effect duration 6–8 months, recovery 1–4 days (Periorb p86, Ch7). Fat: 7–10 yr cell lifespan; edema resolves ~1 week; ecchymosis up to 3 weeks (Periorb p90-92, Ch8). Hyaluronidase for necrosis "as early as possible especially within 24 hours" (Periorb p84, Ch7). Fig 3.6: filler improvement of infraorbital hollow photographed at 3 weeks (Periorb p34, Ch3).
- **Danger zones**: Infraorbital foramen: "above the zygomatic process of the maxilla, approximately 2–3 cm lateral to the vertical midline of the face. It is typically 0.7–1 cm inferior to the infraorbital margin" (Periorb p201, Ch20). "deeper filler procedures close to the foramen should be avoided because of the blood supply net in this area. The facial artery and infraorbital artery are key arteries supplying the periorbital area" (Periorb p79, Ch7). Infraorbital a. branches anastomose with angular, dorsal nasal, facial arteries (Periorb p7, Ch1). Medial palpebral arteries from dorsal nasal a. after crossing medial canthal ligament (Periorb p6, Ch1). Zygomaticofacial/zygomaticotemporal nerves exit lateral inferior rim (Periorb p201, Ch20). Contraindications: hyperpigmentation without hollowness; skin laxity without hollowing; fat herniation → surgery instead (Periorb p80, Ch7).
- **Variation**: "the eyelid-cheek junction is situated in a lower position in men than in women over time [24]" (Periorb p16, Ch2). Males: increased sagging severity of lower eyelid (Periorb p12, Ch2). Darker skin: midface volume loss is initial aging sign (Periorb p13, Ch2).

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (SOOF, malar mound, deep/superficial compartments)

- **Anatomy/boundaries**: Facial layers superficial→deep: "skin, subcutaneous fat, superficial musculo-aponeurotic system (SMAS), deep fat, and periosteum [5]" (Periorb p200, Ch20). "The sub-orbicularis oculi fat (SOOF) is superficial to the deep fascia in the lateral half of the infraorbital rim"; "SOOF resembles the ROOF and is located between the lower eyelid and cheek" (Periorb p200 Ch20; p5 Ch1). "Midface suborbicularis oculi fat (SOOF) descends during the lifetime"; SOOF/midface lift simulated by digital upward-lateral traction (Periorb p49, Ch4). Descent of malar fat pads deepens nasojugal and nasolabial folds (Periorb p199, Ch20). Malar mounds/festoons: descent of orbicularis + midfacial soft tissue with fluid in "shelved pockets"; located below inferior orbital rim, partially improve with orbicularis squeeze, unchanged on upgaze (vs orbital fat) (Periorb p49, Ch4); treated with sclerosing agents / direct excision (Periorb p151-152, Ch15).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no mm data)
- **Injection plane & technique**: Fat graft to cheeks via lateral-canthus entry point (a) (Periorb p92, Ch8). Filler: HA usable for malar augmentation "if bony evidence is minimal ... not a permanent solution ... volume is restricted" (Periorb p210, Ch21). Midface lift: preperiosteal dissection ≈1 cm inferior to inferior orbital rim (further if more mobilization needed), ORL disinserted (Periorb p202-203, Ch20).
- **Volumes**: (no data for cheek filler)
- **Product/rheology**: (no data)
- **Surface response & longevity**: HA filler for malar "does require periodic renewal" (Periorb p210, Ch21). Malar edema after periorbital filler via lymphatic compromise (Periorb p84, Ch7).
- **Danger zones**: infraorbital foramen 2–3 cm lateral of midline, 0.7–1 cm below rim (p201 Ch20); zygomaticofacial nerve; facial artery crossing maxilla deep to zygomaticus major/levator labii (Periorb p79, Ch7).
- **Variation**: Malar hypoplasia impacts lid position (Periorb p246, Ch23).

Otherwise (no filler data).

## General & cross-region

- **Filler physics definitions** (Periorb p81, Ch7): viscoelasticity = G' (elasticity, shape retention under force) + G'' (viscosity, resistance to gradual shear deformation) → G* viscoelastic modulus; higher G* = more resistance to deformation = "major lifting effect". Cohesivity = capacity to not dissociate; high-cohesivity gels infiltrate uniformly and are not fractionated by movement. Ideal filler list (adds volume, no migration, reversible, durable, etc.) (Periorb p81, Ch7). HA fillers "differ in their degree of crosslinking, affecting durability and biocompatibility"; used periorbitally since 2004 (Periorb p79, Ch7). No G' numeric table.
- **Product-longevity data**: Mesogel (HA mesotherapy) 6–8 months, 4–8 sessions (Periorb p86, Ch7). Botulinum 3–8 months, onset ~72 h (Periorb p34, Ch3). Fat graft cells 7–10 years (Periorb p90, Ch8). Malar HA "periodic renewal" (Periorb p210, Ch21). No HA filler duration in months given.
- **Edema/bruising timelines**: HA: ice 24 h, follow-up 15 days (p82 Ch7). Fat: acute edema first 2 days, most edema gone by 1 week, ecchymosis up to 3 weeks (p92 Ch8). MFU: swelling ≤72 h; tingling 2 days–2 weeks (p143 Ch13). Surgical: early wound dehiscence mean 9.2 days (6–15) (p238 Ch23).
- **Imaging / 3D**: No Vectra/3D volumetric outcome data. Photography advice for fat grafting: lighting from above the head to show shadows; avoid direct flash (Periorb p91, Ch8). Ch21: CT-based virtual surgical planning; "Current imaging technology makes it challenging to capture soft tissue ... deformational forces and involuntary muscle motion can cause inaccurate measurements" (Periorb p213, Ch21). Ultrasound only as MFU guidance (depths 1.5/3/4.5 mm) (p142 Ch13). Ref only: Liu et al. 2023 "Age-related changes of the periocular morphology: a two- and three-dimensional anthropometry study in Caucasians" (Periorb p17 ref 25, Ch2); Lambros 2007 "Observations on periorbital and midface aging" (p17 ref 24); Schelke 2023 duplex-ultrasound malar edema (p87 ref 18); Antera 3D camera split-face study (p100 ref, Ch9).
- **Aging volume-loss (qualitative only)**: skin collagen/elastin peak ~age 30; up to 90% of skin aging from photoaging (Periorb p12, p14, Ch2). Fat compartments lose volume/shift at different rates; ligaments separate; gravitational descent of deep tissues independent of superficial; less gravitational effect where retaining ligaments exist (Periorb p15-16, Ch2). Bone: frontal bone enlarges, glabellar angle flattens; orbital + midface resorption deepens tear trough, prominence of medial brow, "appearance of enophthalmos and smaller eyes"; superomedial & inferolateral rim thin (Periorb p15 Ch2; p190 Ch19). Orbital fat involutional shrinkage → enophthalmos (Periorb p154, Ch16). Palpebral fissure height 10–11 mm → 8–10 mm in older patients (Periorb p4, Ch1).
- **Sex differences**: male skin thicker at all ages, more linear thinning; female post-menopausal rapid thinning; males more sagging lower lid, more prominent rhytids, U-pattern glabella; lid-cheek junction lower in men (Periorb p12-16, Ch2).
- **Ethnicity**: darker skin: thicker dermis + lipid content, delayed rhytids, more dyschromia; Asian: rhytid onset delayed ~10 y vs Caucasian, more periocular rhytids, thicker lid skin, higher brow, lower crease, tarsus 4 vs 10 mm (Periorb p13 Ch2; p45 Ch4; p181 Ch18).
- **Cross-region safety rules (Ch7 p83)**: understand anatomy; danger zones; aspirate; inject slowly; 0.1–0.2 mL/pass; blunt microcannulas; stop on resistance/pain; avoid overcorrection; avoid strong massage. Ischemia management: warm compress, 2% nitroglycerin paste, sildenafil, massage, intralesional hyaluronidase (intravascular not required) (Periorb p83, Ch7). Nodule/biofilm: clarithromycin + moxifloxacin or ciprofloxacin/minocycline (Periorb p84, Ch7).
- **Surgical steps with mm reference (for boundary modelling)**: Lower-lid skin resection rarely >5 mm vertical (Periorb p151, Ch15); skin pinch max 5 mm, not medial to lateral limbus (p151); subperiosteal pocket 2 cm down maxilla for fat repositioning (p150); preperiosteal midface release ≈1 cm below rim (p202 Ch20); lateral canthotomy 2 cm past commissure (p202 Ch20).

## Assessment

- **Quantitativeness**: Low for filler purposes. The only filler-specific numbers are tear-trough HA volumes (0.2–0.3 mL/eyelid; MD-codes Tt1/Tt2/Tt3 = 0.2/0.2/0.1 mL/side, may be doubled), per-pass 0.1–0.2 mL, and plane (supraperiosteal, sub-orbicularis, along the rim, cannula preferred). No G' numbers, no soft-tissue thickness in mm (except frontalis depth 2–7 mm / 3.5–5 mm and the qualitative "no subcutaneous fat" eyelid), no filler longevity in months, no 3D-volumetric outcome data, no surface-lift-per-mL.
- **Best-covered regions**: brow/upper-lid/lower-lid surface anthropometry (fissure, MRD1/2, crease heights, brow position, tarsus dimensions, LCT dimensions, nerve exit distances 17/27 mm from midline, infraorbital foramen 2–3 cm lateral / 0.7–1 cm below rim); fat-graft volumes per periorbital subregion (brow 1–3 mL, temple 2–3 mL, upper sulcus 1–2 mL, tear trough & palpebromalar 2–3 mL each); ligament/fat-pad topology (ROOF, SOOF, ORL/orbitomalar, tear-trough ligament, arcus marginalis, conjoint tendon); botulinum dose map by periocular subunit.
- **Silent**: nose, lips, marionette, chin, jawline, masseter, neck, preauricular; midface deep fat compartments (only SMAS/deep fat layer list); product rheology values; ultrasound/cadaver thickness tables.
- Useful mainly as a source for periorbital boundary definitions, ligament landmarks, negative-vector/bony criteria, and small tear-trough dosing; cross-check with a dedicated filler-anatomy text for thickness and rheology.

## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Key augmentation zone = "cross shape" between the two frontal eminences and the two supraorbital ridges; supraorbital rim projects forward creating hollow between frontal eminence and rim (ArtSci p.129).
  - Frontalis: depth **3–5 mm below skin surface**; horizontal width varies. Spiegel: bifurcates ~3.5 cm above superior orbital rim (disputed by Costin — muscle present in upper-mid forehead histologically) (p.129).
  - Fat compartments: forehead has **only superficial fat** above frontalis, **no deep fat**. Central + two lateral compartments; middle compartment connects with lateral temporal cheek fat at temple. Septa between compartments impede subcutaneous cannula/molding; supraperiosteal injection unaffected by septa (p.131).
  - SubSMAS target space: **subgalea-frontalis space** (under galeo-frontalis muscle) — indication "flat forehead"; supraorbital/supratrochlear/STA/temporal branch all lie within or above frontalis, so the space is safe (Table 3.1, p.74–75).
  - Forehead/glabella wrinkles caused by muscle action, little relation to fat compartments or retaining ligaments (p.69–70).
- **Layer thickness data**: frontalis 3–5 mm deep (p.129). No skin/fat mm data.
- **Injection plane & technique**
  - Supraperiosteal (between base of galea and periosteum), bolus technique; **23G 5 cm cannula**, entry via 23G needle; usually **3 entry points**, entry at eyebrow apex reaching forehead center (p.131). Press superomedial orbital rim to block arteries; cannula opening faces away from eye (p.113, 131).
  - Alternative needle: mark points at **1 cm intervals**, vertical injection to bone, aspirate first; more bruising (p.113).
  - Cannula method: 2–3 entry points, broadly "dissect" supraperiosteal area (Fig 4.29, p.113). Elastic bandage after heavy subgaleal dissection to limit edema (p.200).
  - All boluses should be connected into one mass; massage with fingers + wet gauze (p.131).
  - Prior forehead lift/shortening: galea–periosteum adhesion prevents even spread (p.131).
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book).
- **Product/rheology**: less viscous fillers avoid bumps but migrate under frontalis action → firmer preferred (p.131). Forehead bony curvature can push a cannula out of the deep plane (p.59).
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: (no data). General: supraperiosteal filler compressed by SMAS/ligaments; see General.
- **Danger zones**: supraorbital artery divides into superficial + deep branches **1 cm above orbital rim** → deep injection not automatically safe near brow (p.66). Supratrochlear superficial branch perforates frontalis **1.5 mm above supraorbital rim** then subcutaneous (Reece) (p.130). Deep branch of supraorbital nerve runs on periosteum within medial 2 cm of superior temporal septum (p.67). Mark middle forehead vein + STA before treatment (p.101).
- **Variation**: Asians prefer evenly rounded forehead without angular protrusions (p.129).

## Glabella

- **Anatomy**: corrugator supercilii origin **2.9 mm lateral to midline, 9.8 mm superior to nasion**; thickest between medial canthal line and mid-pupil line, **~2–3 mm thick**; inserts more laterally than expected (p.130). Procerus: nasal SMAS near nasal bone to brow skin; extends from supraorbital rim to medial canthal line/nasion level (p.130).
- SubSMAS target: **subprocerus space** (under procerus) — "glabellar depression" (Table 3.1 p.74; Fig 3.19 p.79). Ideal nasofrontal concavity 135° (p.10).
- **Injection plane**: deep under muscle; pinch skin thickly so procerus is included in the pinch, inject deep beneath it (p.80).
- **Volumes/rheology/longevity**: (no data). Radix/glabella noted as a high-external-pressure site where high-cohesion/low-G' fillers spread and fail; needs high elastic modulus (p.37, 39).
- **Danger zones**: glabella = most common site of filler blindness; supratrochlear artery course ≈ "corrugator crease" (Pessa) vs medial canthal line (Ugur); author: mostly coincides with corrugator crease, superficial branch is the thicker main branch, perforates frontalis slightly above supraorbital rim (p.130–131). Intercanthal (transcanthal) vein subcutaneous above procerus → PE risk (p.166).

## Brow (eyebrow / ROOF)

- **Anatomy**: ROOF = deep fat under orbicularis in brow region; atrophies with age → brow/lid complex sag (p.141). Ideal brow: medial end on vertical from lateral alar margin; lateral end on ala–lateral canthus line; peak at lateral limbus vertical; medial & lateral ends level (p.140). Eye ratios: eye width L=1 → straight length W ≈1.63, medial canthus→inner brow end ≈0.60 (p.140).
- **Injection plane**: submuscular into ROOF, entry at lateral brow end, **moderate-strength HA**; then soft filler dermal/subdermal to smooth. Augment **lateral to mid-pupillary line** only (medial skin firmly attached to muscle) (p.141, Fig 5.21).
- **Volumes/longevity**: (no data).
- **Danger**: supraorbital/supratrochlear medially; deep branch supraorbital artery (p.66, 138).
- **Variation**: brow ptosis less severe in Asians; brow lift with filler common in West, not Korea (p.7, 140).

## Temple

- **Anatomy/boundaries** (p.132–136): layers 1 skin, 2 subQ (lateral temporal cheek fat, may be very scant), 3 STF (=TPF/galea extension; first layer of mild resistance), 4 loose areolar = **upper temporal compartment (UTC)** / **lower temporal compartment (LTC)** split by inferior temporal septum (ITS), 5 DTF (splits into superficial + deep layers around **superficial temporal fat pad, STFP**), then deep temporal fat pad (buccal fat extension) and temporalis. Table 5.1 nomenclature p.133.
  - STFP covers **4 × 5 cm** cephalic to zygomatic arch (Matic) (p.134). DTF layers fuse ~1 cm above arch (Ramirez); Hwang: 56% attach to arch superior margin, 44% superolateral, attachment within 2 mm of arch (p.134).
  - UTC: no major vessels/nerves; LTC: triangular, contains STA, temporal branch VII, zygomaticotemporal nerve; temporal tunnel connects LTC to prezygomatic space (p.76, 134).
  - Hollowing worse with low BMI; causes: temporalis thinning, DTFP loss, STFP thinning (p.134). Typical depression is in lower temple immediately above arch (p.77).
- **Layer thickness**: middle temporal vein **~5 mm diameter**, passes **2 cm above zygomatic arch** between DTF layers (p.136, 208); sentinel vein **~2 mm** diameter, subcutaneous then pierces TPF/DTF (p.208). Skin/fat mm: (no data).
- **Injection plane & technique** (Fig 5.7 levels, p.136):
  - Level A: supraperiosteal under temporalis — "may not show change despite a large amount"; least neurovascular risk. Level B: between superficial/deep DTF layers (STFP) — efficient, feel firm deep DTF; bleeding risk from middle temporal vein. Level C: LTC — facial nerve + STA, great caution. Level D: subcutaneous — no major vessels, but bumpy/uneven; thin subQ makes it easy to mistake C for D.
  - Author preference: **between STF and DTF** (interfascial) with 23G 5 cm cannula, entry at hairline, bolus + massage to connect; entry at intersection of zygomatic arch and lateral orbital rim minimizes neurovascular risk (p.76, 136). Deep-to-temporalis injection is "inefficient" — needs larger volume, and temporalis contraction moves filler and changes its properties (p.77).
  - Pinch test: cannula in subcutaneous fat moves visibly L/R; beneath STF it does not (p.114). Needle vertical injection after marking vein/artery is an option (p.114).
- **Volumes**: (no data) — qualitatively "smaller amount" in STFP vs deep (p.77).
- **Product/rheology**: interfascial → **medium viscoelasticity**, cannula, small amount ("safest"); deep supraperiosteal → high viscosity with needle (p.208).
- **Expected response & longevity**: (no data).
- **Danger zones**: STA bifurcation above superior orbital rim in 64%, below in 36%; frontal branch runs superomedially at **60.8°** toward frontalis lateral margin, becomes subcutaneous at superolateral quadrant of brow-upper-margin × lateral-canthus-line intersection (p.135). Temporal branch VII: Pitanguy line (0.5 cm below tragus → 1.5 cm above lateral brow); rises to floor of STF **1.5–3.0 cm above arch, 0.9–1.4 cm posterior to lateral orbital rim** (Agarwal) (p.135); ITS blocks its superior progression (p.134). Temporal branch attaches under SMAS ~1 cm above zygomatic bone (p.69). Deep temporal artery encountered with bone-touch needle (p.59). PE risk via sentinel/middle temporal veins (p.208). STA embedded in STF but spatially in superficial fat (Fig 2.57 p.58).
- **Variation**: Asians with prominent arch need temple + lateral cheek fill to smooth contour (p.7–8).

## Upper eyelid & periorbital (sunken eyelid / supraorbital hollow)

- **Anatomy**: causes — genetics, orbital fat loss with age, over-resection at blepharoplasty; Asians have weak levator, more subQ fat in upper lid/brow (p.137). Target = **preseptal space** (between orbicularis and septum, below orbital retaining ligament); ROOF-plane deep fat is atrophied in sunken eyes (p.80, 138; Table 3.1 p.74).
- **Injection plane & technique**: cannula entry at intersection of lateral-canthus vertical and orbital rim; withdraw slightly after bone touch → tip in space deep to orbicularis; retrograde linear "tiny" injections, very slow, **patient upright with eyes open**; mainly medial/middle rim under brow, above supratarsal crease, below ORL (Fig 5.14 p.139). Then very soft HA subdermal to smooth/remove multiple eyelid lines (Fig 5.16 p.140). Avoid intramuscular (bleeding) and transseptal (levator injury, hematoma) (p.137).
- **Volumes**: (no data); "always safer to correct conservatively" (p.138).
- **Product**: **soft, easily moldable HA**; very soft for subdermal (p.137–140).
- **Response/longevity**: overcorrection → swollen look or inferior migration into lid (p.138).
- **Danger**: supratrochlear/supraorbital at medial rim anastomose with orbital arteries → use cannula (p.138). Contraindicated in ptosis/proptosis (p.140).

## Pre-tarsal roll ("charming roll", lower-lid fullness) — Korea-specific

- **Anatomy**: thickened tarsal orbicularis, more visible on smiling (p.142). Inferior palpebral artery (branch of supratrochlear → ICA) runs **deeper than orbicularis** and below lid margin (p.143, 145).
- **Plane**: within orbicularis or superficial to it, as close to lid margin as possible (p.143, 145).
- **Volumes**: **up to 0.2–0.4 cc per side** (p.145). Needle in 3 sections at constant depth; or 25G-type cannula retrograde from lateral entry near lashes (p.145–146).
- **Response**: Tyndall if too superficial → dissolve and redo; molding possible with cannula; patient self-mold with cotton swab first 2–3 days (p.145–146). Bruising common, bumpy surface common (p.143).
- **Longevity**: (no data).

## Tear trough & infraorbital (dark circle, nasojugal, palpebromalar, midcheek groove)

- **Anatomy/boundaries** (p.147–151):
  - Tear trough: medial canthus → mid-pupillary line, gentle curve **~3 cm** long, **2–3 mm below orbital rim**, between palpebral and orbital orbicularis; **tear trough ligament** (medial ORL, short, firmly bone-attached, medial canthal tendon → mid-pupil). Nasojugal groove is distinct (between orbital OOm and LLSAN origin), less pronounced.
  - Palpebromalar (orbitomalar) groove: mid-pupil → lateral canthus along **ORL** (looser, longer, space between bone and OOm → easier filling). Midcheek groove ("Indian band"): from tear-trough/palpebromalar junction inferolaterally along **zygomatico-cutaneous (Z-C) ligament**. Three grooves define lid-cheek, malar (malar mound/festoon), nasolabial segments (Fig 5.37).
  - Causes: TT ligament + sag, thin skin, fat differential above/below ligament (more fat and thicker skin below), infraorbital fat herniation, maxillary resorption (p.148–149).
  - SOOF (deep to OOm below eye) vs ROOF above; SOOF + deep medial cheek fat volume ↓ with age (p.151). **Medial-most tear trough has no SOOF** — tight muscle on bone, deep fat injection there is "fundamentally impossible"; inject small amount medially, sufficient laterally (p.81, 151). Gelatin cadaver: filler ends up under AND within OOm medially (p.81).
  - Malar fat (superficial, over OOm) inferior to TT ligament; its superior border = tear trough + palpebromalar groove (p.151).
  - Ristow/premaxillary space bounded above by TT ligament, below by zygomatic ligament; angular artery through its medial part, angular vein lateral boundary (p.73, 82).
- **Layer thickness**: skin under eyes "thin"; (no mm). Bony orbit depth study (El-Garem) cited only (p.94).
- **Injection plane & technique**: options deep / superficial / deep + superficial; author: **deep + superficial primary, superficial-only for touch-up** (p.152). Deep injection required if anterior cheek lacks volume; tight TT ligament needs superficial too and **ligament release before filling**. Entry 1–2 cm beyond groove end along its extension, cannula at 45–60° to bone, advance in prezygomatic space/SOOF layer to just under the trough; retrograde, small amounts medially while watching for midpoint elevation (p.152–153). Superficial: reposition cannula above OOm, **25G cannula**, low-viscosity filler, **tunnel slightly wider than the injection area first** so filler spreads laterally rather than bulging under thin skin (p.153). Sandwich (small deep + superficial) rather than large deep bolus — big deep boluses in un-released medial trough make dark circles look worse (p.156). Assess with patient gazing straight ahead (looking up = appears undercorrected, down = overcorrected) (p.152). Infraorbital block for deep injections (p.152).
  - Palpebromalar: partly improved by TT/midcheek correction; if more needed, submuscular plane; avoid inferior palpebral vein (p.153).
  - Basic-technique chapter: Indian-fold cannula entry then supraperiosteal in contact with bone; ZC ligament resistance → change path; needle vertical bone-touch when emphasizing zygoma (p.116–117).
- **Volumes**: (no explicit numbers) — "only a small amount" medially (p.153).
- **Product/rheology** (p.152): deep (prezygomatic) → avoid low viscoelasticity; thin skin/small SOOF → **medium**; thick skin/large fat → **high** viscoelastic. Superficial → **lowest viscoelasticity and minimal water retention**, molds well; high-water-retention fillers cause edema under thin skin; do superficial in two staged sessions (p.152). Periorbital = example where soft, spreading, flexible filler needed (p.55).
- **Expected response/longevity**: edema risk; nodularity if superficial to OOm with uneven/excess/high-G'/high-swelling filler (p.154). Surgery preferred age 50–60+ with severe fat protrusion (p.154).
- **Danger zones**: **>30% of Koreans have an infraorbital ("detoured") branch of a duplex facial artery**; angular vein large (esp. supine) — both pass through SOOF and DMCF then along OOm medial border → strongly recommend cannula (p.154, Fig 5.44). Infraorbital foramen ~1 cm below rim, zygomaticofacial foramen at inferior×lateral orbital rim junction (p.101, 104). Angular artery irritation causes pain when cannula near nose (p.117). Facial vein along nasojugal groove (p.158).
- **Variation**: (see Koreans above).

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (apple cheek, anteromedial/midcheek hollow, deep/superficial compartments)

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Midface has distinct superficial and deep fat compartments separated by SMAS; upper and lower face do not (p.91). Superficial compartments: large adipocytes, **increase with age**; deep compartments: small, **decrease with age**; all fat migrates downward (p.69). Deep compartments extend beyond creases → deep injection reduces folds (p.70).
  - Cheek layers (superficial→deep): skin, superficial malar fat, orbicularis, deep malar fat incl. SOOF, mimetic muscle layer (zygomaticus origins), preperiosteal fat, periosteum (p.158). **Prezygomatic space 7 layers** (Table 3.3 p.82): skin / subQ / OOm / SOOF / deep fascia origin of zygomatic muscles / preperiosteal (prezygomatic) fat / periosteum. Boundaries: floor zygomaticus origin, roof OOm, upper ORL, lower Z-C ligament; connects to lower temporal space via temporal tunnel; no important neurovascular structures (p.81–82).
  - Premaxillary space: between Z-C ligament and buccal-maxillary ligament / between angular artery and vein; same plane as Ristow's — filler migrates across the NLF into it (p.82).
  - Zygomatic ligament: strongest facial ligament with mandibular ligament; do not weaken much with age → hollows deepen with age (p.92). T-intersection of zygomatic + masseteric cutaneous ligaments = strongest support (p.71).
  - Apple cheek apex (Asian): intersection of horizontal line (alar groove base → helix root) and vertical line (lateral canthus → commissure), or slightly medial; Caucasian apex lateral/above cheekbone (p.157, Fig 5.51).
- **Layer thickness**: SMAS thickest preauricular, thins medially; medial cheek SMAS appears as fat with no white fascia (Table 3.2 p.74). Preauricular stack: skin – superficial fat + retinacula cutis superficialis – superficial fascia – deep fat + retinacula profundus – deep fascia – masticatory muscle (p.74). (No mm.)
- **Injection plane & technique** (Fig 5.53 p.161):
  1. Apple cheek → **prezygomatic space** (preperiosteal fat).
  2. Midcheek hollow/groove → **deep cheek fat pads superficial to deep facial muscles** (deep malar fat incl. SOOF, under SMAS, above zygomaticus) — most Koreans (no bone atrophy); prezygomatic/periosteal firm filler only if real bone resorption (p.158–159). Spread evenly, avoid boluses so filler moves with smile (p.158).
  3. Subdermal soft filler by tenting to smooth (p.160).
  - Entry: lateral & inferior to area; lateral midcheek at vertical of lateral orbital rim × horizontal of mid-alar groove. Techniques: retrograde fanning, cross-hatching, vertical bolus + layering; non-injecting hand holds cheek fat pad, build from apex radially (p.159–160).
  - Midcheek groove (p.156–157): superficial — tunnel Z-C ligament fibrous tissue at reticular dermis ~**10 passes with 23G cannula**, then **medium** viscoelastic filler; deep — **medium-high** filler with needle supraperiosteal, towering up to mid layer directly beneath Z-C ligament (fills lost SOOF/DMCF, lifts ligament); or cannula: fill under tear trough first then under Z-C ligament. Deep alone gives "excellent correction". Alternative for malar mound: lipolytic/HIFU above Z-C ligament.
  - Filler volumizing outside/above midcheek crease makes it look deeper; filler spreading into lower prezygomatic space when treating orbitomalar groove has same effect (p.72).
- **Volumes**: (no data).
- **Product**: Table/Fig 3.38 "choice by consistency and particle size" — firm under SMAS, soft subdermal (SSRT, p.88–91). Anterior cheek = ideal site for high-cohesion softer fillers (no strong external force) (p.37).
- **Expected response**: SSRT lifting pattern — anterior malar fill lifts nasolabial area; nasolabial fill → upper lip; anterior cheek → lower lip; prejowl → mentum; side cheek → jawline (p.89, Fig 3.36). Firm sub-SMAS filler increases density → SMAS pushed up → skin stretched/tightened (p.88). Longevity: (no data).
- **Danger zones**: infraorbital and zygomaticofacial foramina/arteries (p.158); facial vein along nasojugal groove, always lateral, deeper than deep fat near NLF (p.158–159); duplex facial artery infraorbital trunk ~30% of Koreans (p.159). Facial nerve descends from SMAS-plane to deep at anterior/lateral face boundary (p.84).
- **Variation**: Caucasians lose midface bone → periosteal fill; **Koreans do not show age-related midface bone change** — flat midface is congenital, hollowing is soft-tissue ptosis → augment soft-tissue plane (p.6, 81). Asians prefer apex medial/inside eye-tail line, circular highlight; Western oval highlight near eye tail, ogee curve (p.99).

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch (submalar / lateral cheek hollow)

- **Anatomy**: Asians have wider, more protruding arch → hollow below arch looks deeper (p.82). Hollow caused by zygomatic ligament, parotid-masseteric ligament, platysma-auricular ligaments pulling skin, not weight loss (p.83). Target: **preparotid & premasseteric space** — floor parotid + lower half masseter, roof SMAS/platysma, posterior = platysma-auricular ligament, anterior = masseteric ligaments (Table 3.4 p.84). Stenson's duct and buccal branch lie deep to parotid-masseteric fascia → space is safe (p.84). Middle vs lower premasseteric spaces separated by buccal branch (p.72).
- **Injection plane & technique**: mild — subcutaneous fat, needle/cannula, fill inferior → superior; severe (developed ZC + masseteric cutaneous ligaments) — cannula **partial tunneling** through ligaments (only weaken, not complete dissection), then retrograde fanning + layering into preparotid-masseteric space (sub-SMAS, above parotid-masseteric fascia); entry at **lateral orbital rim vertical × commissure–tragus line**; soft filler subdermal for irregularities (p.83–84, 122, 163). Subcutaneous injection alone will not correct ligament-caused hollow; wrong plane → widening or hardening (p.83). Pinch maneuver locates SMAS depth and parotid (p.122). Special molding tools (Figs 4.51–52).
- **Volumes/rheology/longevity**: (no data) — "smooth and sufficiently volumized cheek without irregular borders" (p.84).
- **Danger**: temporal branch VII 1 cm above zygoma under SMAS (p.69); facial nerve buccal branch between middle/lower premasseteric spaces (p.72).
- **Variation**: Asians need fill *inferior* to arch, not arch augmentation (p.7).

## Preauricular

- SMAS thickest here (Table 3.2 p.74); layered structure above. Entry for buccal deep injection ~4 cm anterior to tragus (p.161). Otherwise (no data in this book).

## Buccal cheek hollow (perioral cheek)

- **Anatomy**: buccal fat ~**10 cc**, extends to temple (deep temporal fat pad); increases slightly to ~50 y then decreases; superior/middle/inferior lobes, inferior = "buccal fat pad" (p.70, 85). Buccal space boundaries (Table 3.5 p.86): floor buccinator, roof mimetic muscles/SMAS, superior maxillary ligament, inferior platysma adhesion, anterior modiolus, posterior facial vein + masseteric ligaments. Parotid duct superficial to buccal space between middle and inferior lobes; marginal mandibular branch along floor below inferior lobe (p.85). Space expands with age, fat prolapses below commissure → marionette/jowl (p.85–86).
- **Plane**: subcutaneous fat or **prebuccal space** (between SMAS and buccal fat capsule); direct buccal fat injection → excessive filler use, intraoral lump, downward migration, mouth protrusion (p.86, 160). Severe: cannula through SMAS, entry **4 cm anterior to tragus, 2 cm below inferior arch border**, retrograde horizontal fanning; then subcutaneous top-up (p.161–162). Mild/moderate: subcutaneous or deep-to-subQ fat, entry inferior to region, fan inferior→superior (p.161).
- **Volumes/longevity**: (no data). Buccal branch above fat pad usually encapsulated → low injury risk unless large-bore needle (p.86).

## Nose

- **Anatomy**: 5 layers skin / superficial fat / fibromuscular (SMAS; nasalis on bridge, procerus at root) / deep fat / periosteum-perichondrium (p.79, 165). Proximal nose (root) dynamic with a mobile plane; distal (tip, scroll area) fibromuscular layer tightly adherent, subQ = dense collagen + fat → inject only sub-muscular spaces (subprocerus, subnasalis) (p.79). Tip: thick dermis, **no soft subQ fat** (p.168). Ideal: nose length 1/3 face, width 1/5 face width; nasofrontal 115–130°; nasolabial 90–95° men, 95–105° women; nasofacial 35–40° (p.164); nasal tip projection 30–45°, columello-labial 90–100° (p.10). Intercanthal distance : nose width ≈ 1 (p.8).
- **Layer thickness**: (no mm). Nose has least space between bone/cartilage and skin; dermis thicker → highest extravascular-compression necrosis risk (p.170, 204).
- **Injection plane & technique**: supraperiosteal/supraperichondrial only (p.170). Cannula: entry between tip and columella, advance touching cartilage/bone from lower lateral cartilages → upper laterals → nasal bone → radix; **upper limit = upper eyelid crease level**; retrograde linear; **21–23G 5 cm cannula** (longer bends and enters subQ); pinch with opposite hand lifting tissue over deep fat; hump → second entry below hump or needle for hump-to-radix (p.168). Needle: **25G/27G** (not insulin needle — small lumen alters rheology and gives false-negative aspiration), 3–5 midline points, serial puncture, or needle every 3–4 mm vertical (p.114, 168). Tip: between lower lateral crura, deep over cartilage, **small amount of medium-viscosity filler, droplet technique** (p.168). Columella: cannula from medial crus to anterior nasal spine, retrograde; towering for support "does not last long" — combine with cog thread; toxin to depressor septi (p.170). Molding essential; assess tissue pressure with pinch — high pressure → shape sinks within weeks; choose harder filler or accept partial correction (p.56–57, 122).
- **Volumes**: (no numbers) — "do not inject too much volume for tip"; repeat procedures increase compression risk due to fibrosis; NLF + tip in same session raises lateral nasal artery compression → split into two sessions (p.170).
- **Product/rheology**: needs shape retention + resistance to external force (glasses, brow/nasal muscle movement); high G' increases vascular compression risk (p.121). Choose **high viscoelasticity, low water retention**; formerly biphasic large-particle, now monophasic high-G' low-swelling (p.166–168). Radix = site needing high elastic modulus, not cohesion (p.37, 39). Pretreat glabellar/nasal muscles with toxin if highly active (p.122).
- **Expected response**: compression sinking in high-pressure radix; asymmetry common → mold from start (p.56, 122).
- **Danger zones**: lateral nasal artery (tip, from facial artery), dorsal nasal artery (dorsum, from ophthalmic), columellar artery (from superior labial); arteries in fibromuscular layer or between it and deep fat; often asymmetric/unilateral (p.165–166). Intercanthal vein subcutaneous above procerus → PE via supraophthalmic or angular routes (p.166). Compress dorsal nasal artery with opposite hand during radix injection (p.171). Facial artery branch diameters narrow upward → occluded at lower pressure (p.66).
- **Variation**: nose filler is the most popular filler site in Korea (p.163). Asians: paranasal + columellar fill common (p.8).

## Nasolabial fold

- **Anatomy**: three types (p.171–173): (1) paranasal/canine-fossa deep-volume deficiency — triangular paranasal depression; superficial fat almost absent below fold; (2) skin-tightness difference (upper lip skin adherent to muscle; nasolabial fat above fold **hypertrophies** with age, paranasal region atrophies); (3) muscular — LLSAN, LLS, zygomaticus minor insert into skin at fold (musculocutaneous attachment). Muscular theory of NLF slightly favored over fascial (p.71). Nasolabial fat superior to crease does not atrophy (p.177). SubSMAS target: **Ristow's (pyriform) space** = canine fossa under medial DMCF, between deep fat and periosteum; same plane as premaxillary space → filler migrates above crease unless blocked with finger (p.73, 82; Table 3.1).
- **Layer thickness**: SMAS "dim" enveloping mimetic muscles at NLF (Table 3.2 p.74); histology confirms SMAS medial to fold (p.74). (No mm.)
- **Injection plane & technique**:
  - Crease only: needle, slightly medial to crease, subcutaneous/subdermal above orbicularis, retrograde linear + fanning; fern-leaf / duck-walk superficial technique (Fig 5.73 p.174; Fig 4.39 p.116). Inserting needle in **superficial subdermal layer** avoids the superficially coursing facial artery (p.174). Top of subcutaneous fat just under dermis = safest subQ plane (p.58).
  - Fold + paranasal depression: **two layers** — soft filler superficial subcutaneous/subdermal along crease; **firm filler** into paranasal deep fat compartment (moderate) or **Ristow's space** (severe) (Fig 5.75 p.175). Needle perpendicular at center of paranasal triangle (relatively vessel-free but aspirate); touch canine fossa bone, withdraw slightly into loose space; **towering + layering**; press bulge above crease with opposite hand (p.174–176). Large subcutaneous volumes cause **lateral expansion not projection** → use Ristow's (p.176).
  - Cannula: entry **5 mm lateral to crease at inferior third of ala–commissure line**, subcutaneous to paranasal triangle, layering + fanning; withdraw along crease injecting superficially (p.176–177). Entry on commissure extension line runs parallel to facial artery (risk); some go perpendicular to fold (p.114).
  - Deep NLF filler can migrate superolaterally with expression and deepen the fold (p.59).
- **Volumes**: (no data) — "too much volume is not harmonious" (p.100).
- **Product**: soft superficial / firm deep (p.174–175). Nasolabial = "helpful" site for top-of-subcutaneous injection (p.58). Mouth-corner region needs flexible fillers not diluted with free HA (p.49).
- **Expected response**: SSRT — NLF fill lifts upper lip area (p.89). Longevity: cited RCT papers only (p.62–63), no numbers.
- **Danger zones**: facial artery runs **medial to fold in >70% of Koreans**; when lateral, within **5 mm** in >50% (p.176). In **>50% of Koreans** facial artery/SLA origin along NLF courses superficially above orbicularis oris (p.173–174). Angular artery between subcutaneous and muscular layers → supraperiosteal injection safe; compress distal artery with opposite hand (p.66). Facial artery becomes subcutaneous **1.5 cm lateral to commissure** where SLA branches (pulse palpable/visible) (p.188).
- **Variation**: paranasal augmentation popular in Asians to reduce mouth protrusion (p.8).

## Lips & perioral (lip augmentation, philtrum, smoker's lines)

- **Anatomy**: upper:lower ratio **1:1.6** ideal; upper lip vermilion 2–3 mm above upper teeth at rest; upper lip **2 mm anterior** to lower on profile; Ricketts: Western ideal lower lip 2 mm, upper 4 mm posterior; Korea: lip margins on line or 1–2 mm behind (p.9, 178, 189). Orbicularis lies posterior to lip center; superficial and deep (suborbicularis oris) fat separated by muscle; **superficial lip fat increases with age, deep decreases**, total volume conserved → flattening by inward roll, not loss (p.179). Superior labial artery mean **diameter 1 mm**, runs between mucosa and orbicularis at vermilion-border level; inferior labial between mucosa and muscle near alveolar margin (p.179–180).
- **Plane**: superficial fat layer preferred (skin-side along vermilion border, less volume) or mucosal-side into suborbicularis oris fat (safer in lower lip; more volume needed) (p.180). Retrograde, entries ~1 cm apart (p.180).
- **Volumes**: **0.3–0.4 cc per lip** typical; central tubercle **0.1–0.2 cc**; **max 1.5 cc per lip** (beyond → puffy, thickens rather than rotates) (p.180–181). Pretarsal etc. see above.
- **Expected response**: **10–20% of initial volume lost after 1 week** (p.181); foreign-body sensation transient; ice compression; lip tissue soft → low pressure, rich supply, complications less likely than nose (p.66).
- **Smoker's lines**: subdermal/intradermal, soft low-viscosity HA, **30G/31G needle** along vermilion border, small amounts (large amounts → irregularities); combine toxin **0.5 U at ~1 cm intervals** along border (p.188). Perioral wrinkle types listed p.187. Filler for dynamic perioral lines should only "slightly flatten" (p.188).
- **Danger**: facial artery superficial 1.5 cm lateral to commissure between zygomaticus major and risorius → avoid subcutaneous fat injection around commissure (p.188). Inject from mouth corner area (farther from major vessels) (p.179).
- **Variation**: Asians want central tubercle augmented; Westerners do not (p.181).

## Marionette & oral commissure (commissural line, melolabial fold, static labiomandibular fold)

- **Anatomy**: Korean modiolus **~11 mm lateral, 9 mm inferior** to mouth corner (Caucasian at/above corner) → Asians prone to downturned corners (p.181). Commissural line = depth difference between DAO (superficial) and orbicularis fibers at modiolus; lateral lower lip compartment atrophy deepens it (p.182). Superficial lower-lip compartments: two lateral + one central (p.86, 182). Marionette = melolabial fold reaching mandibular border with prejowl sulcus (p.183). Causes: maxillary/mandibular resorption, gravity, loss of deep fat under DAO, DAO compression, mandibular ligament tethering, redundant skin, jowl/buccal fat sag (Table 5.2 p.184). Labiomandibular fat decreases with age while jowl fat increases (p.70). Static line follows posterior DAO border; dynamic fold along anterior DAO border; DAO upper 1/3–1/2 myofibers only, inferior anchored by mandibular ligament → bowing (p.184). SubSMAS target: **subdepressor anguli oris space** (deep fat under DAO) (Table 3.1 p.74, p.86–87).
- **Plane/technique**: commissural/melolabial — needle, fat above muscle, linear threading + retrograde fanning, fern-leaf/duck-walk subdermal (Fig 5.91 p.185). Marionette mild–moderate — needle subcutaneous above muscle; deep/severe hollow — **cannula** into submuscular fat, entry at inferior third of commissure–mandibular border line (or medial to depressed area at lower 2/3), retrograde fanning/cross-hatching/layering into fat deep and superficial to muscle; press bulge above line with other hand; soft filler subdermal to finish (p.185).
- **Volumes/longevity**: (no data).
- **Product**: fillers at mouth corners must resist stretch/shrink — avoid products softened with free HA (p.49).
- **Danger**: facial, inferior labial, labiomental, submental, ascending mental arteries; mental artery/nerve at foramen (p.185, Fig 5.93).
- **Variation**: combination — DAO toxin, jowl fat removal, threads (p.184).

## Chin

- **Anatomy**: layers skin–subQ (superficial fat) / muscle–deep fat–bone; thin deep fat under mentalis at tip (p.190). Premental space (deep fat between mentalis and bone) — supraperiosteal bolus at tip "would require large amounts" (p.87). Fat compartments of lip/chin Fig 5.82 p.179. Ideal: philtrum-to-chin ≈1/3 face; forehead:nose:chin 1:1:1 West vs **1:1:0.8** East; midface:lower face 1:0.8–0.9 in Asians (p.8, 189). Ricketts line is a reference only — frontal naturalness prioritized (p.189–190). Lower lip → chin inverted-S (p.189).
- **Plane/technique**: supraperiosteal (bolus, needle **25/27G** vertical midline, bone touch, retract slightly, below muscle; bevel cephalad for projection, caudad for elongation); additional **medium** filler into superficial fat over muscle if needed; cannula **21/23G** retrograde fanning for wide areas, entry side of chin or just below tip (p.118, 191). Mentalis toxin **2–4 U per side**, 0.5 cm lateral to midline, 2/3 of the way from lower vermilion to chin end — prolongs maintained shape, removes cobblestone (p.192).
- **Volumes**: (no data).
- **Product**: **high viscoelastic HA or CaHA** deep; medium superficial (p.191). Chin = high-external-force site needing high G' (p.37, 39). Bone resorption in mentum by filler cited (ref 37 p.63).
- **Expected response**: post-edema irregularities palpable — massage/top-up; jawline depression at chin–body boundary after augmentation → contour (p.191, 194). Migration: toward oral cavity if too close to lower lip; downward along platysma plane if elongating too far (p.194).
- **Danger**: mental artery/nerve (foramen ~2 cm below mouth corner, slightly medial to pupil line, p.105), inferior labial; midline relatively safe; submental central branch small; abundant superficial fat → low necrosis risk (p.190, 193). Nodules if thin skin/scant fat → prefer supraperiosteal (p.193–194).
- **Variation**: Koreans have short/retracted chins; prefer slight forward + downward elongation; Western prefer distinct mandibular angle (p.7, 189).

## Jawline & prejowl

- **Anatomy**: mandibular ligament (true, strong, does not weaken much) separates labiomandibular fat from jowl; mandibular septum prevents soft tissue migrating below border (p.71–72, 92). Prejowl sulcus in older patients; in young, depressions lateral to augmented chin (p.192). Subcutaneous fat of mandibular body **thinner than chin** (p.192).
- **Technique**: needle **25/27G** vertical/oblique through DAO and platysma to supraperiosteum; cannula **21/23G 5 cm**, entries medial to chin–body boundary or below masseter anterior border (beware facial artery at entry), retrograde linear on bone, then subcutaneous pass (p.192–193). Nefertiti toxin **10–15 U per side** at 1.0–1.5 cm intervals, deeper than subcutaneous (p.193).
- **Volumes/longevity**: (no data). SSRT: prejowl fill lifts mentum; side cheek fill lifts jawline (p.89).
- **Danger**: facial artery at antegonial notch/jawline (deep at mandible crossing → superficial-fat jowl injection relatively safe, p.65); mental nerve (p.185).
- **Variation**: Asians augment mental region only, avoid angle/jawline fill (p.7).

## Masseter & mandibular angle

- Oval-face toxin/lipolytic cocktail to masseter/skin/subQ reduces lower-face volume; combined with filler T-zone (p.12, 88). Buccal fat becomes more muscular as masseter develops (p.70). Filler in angle not preferred by Asians (p.7). Otherwise (no data in this book).

## Neck

- Great auricular nerve block point 6.5 cm from external auditory canal along SCM (p.105). Platysma-auricular ligament forms posterior boundary of premasseteric space (p.84). Otherwise (no data in this book).

## General & cross-region

### Layer-stack / space model (Ch.3)
- Face = 5-layer Mendelson concept; SMAS continuous forehead→neck (galea/frontalis, procerus, nasalis, STF, orbicularis oculi, SMAS, orbicularis oris, DAO, platysma) (Fig 3.37 p.90). SMAS separates superficial fibroadipose (vertical retinacular septa → skin) from deep "lubricant" fibroadipose layer (p.88–90).
- **Table 3.1 (p.74) subSMAS adipofascial spaces → indication**: subgalea-frontalis (flat forehead); interfascial & pretemporalis / STFP (temple); subprocerus (glabella); preseptal (sunken eyelid); suborbicularis (ROOF, medial SOOF, lateral DMCF — infraorbital groove); subnasalis (flat nose); prezygomatic incl. SOOF (apple cheek); premaxillary incl. lateral DMCF (anteromedial cheek); Ristow's/pyriform incl. medial DMCF (paranasal, NLF); preparotid & premasseteric (lateral cheek); prebuccal (buccal hollow); subDAO (marionette); premental (chin). Cadaver stained-gelatin study confirmed placement in each (p.73–87).
- **Table 3.2 (p.74) SMAS thickness**: progressive thinning preauricular → medial; preauricular/cheek: skin – superficial fat (+retinacula superficialis) – superficial fascia – deep fat (+retinacula profundus) – deep fascia – masticatory muscle; parotid: skin – superficial fat – SMAS – deep fat – parotid fascia – capsule – gland; NLF: dim SMAS enveloping mimetic muscles.
- Asians: thicker, denser, heavier skin; tougher SMAS/retinacula; larger skin volume per unit area → need greater force to lift and **firmer, higher-elasticity fillers**; subdermal-only augmentation inefficient — must target subSMAS spaces (p.6, 73). Asian subcutaneous layer tighter/thicker with richer vascular plexus (Table 1.2 p.7).
- Retaining ligaments: zygomatic + mandibular strongest, do not weaken much; weaker ligaments/septa sag → grooves at strong ligaments deepen with age (p.92). Strong ligament groove: tunnel-release then strong filler; sagging-type: reinforce with filler around ligament, no aggressive tunneling (p.92–93, Fig 3.41).
- Aging fat: superficial compartments enlarge, deep shrink, all migrate down (p.69); labiomandibular fat ↓, jowl fat ↑ (p.70); buccal fat ↑ to ~50 y then ↓ (p.70); SOOF + DMCF ↓ (p.151); lip superficial ↑ deep ↓ (p.179); nasolabial fat hypertrophies (p.177). Koreans: no midface bone loss (p.6).

### Filler physics / rheology (Ch.2)
- Measurement conditions: oscillatory parallel plate, gap 1.0 mm, 25 °C, 0.1–10 Hz frequency sweep; values quoted at **1 Hz, gap 1.0 mm, Malvern Kinexus**, unit Pa (p.30, 32). Amplitude sweep for facial movement (p.30). Cross-linked HA is frequency-stable; free HA G′ rises and viscosity falls with frequency (p.29–30).
- Definitions: G′ storage, G″ loss, G* = √(G′² + G″²), tan δ = G″/G′ (p.32–35). Fig 2.33 phase angle values for 29 Korean-market fillers A–C′ at 1 Hz: 0.07, 0.09, 0.10, 0.10, 0.11, 0.12, 0.12, 0.13, 0.14, 0.14, 0.15, 0.16, 0.16, 0.16, 0.17, 0.17, 0.17, 0.19, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.31, 0.35, 0.35, 0.37, 0.44, 0.68, 0.95, 1.01 (products not named) (p.35). G′/G″/G* bar charts (Figs 2.29–2.31) have no numeric labels in the text.
- Cohesion: not a standard rheological quantity; factors Table 2.1 (p.40): HA concentration, MW, cross-link type/degree, particle size uniformity, hydration, phase angle, free-HA addition. **tan δ ≥ 0.25 → too fluid to hold shape** (diluted/free-HA-mixed fillers) (p.40, 42). Biphasic: high G′, weak cohesion, poor recovery after over-limit deformation, poor flexibility (breaks when stretched); monophasic: lower G′, higher cohesion, creep recovery, flexible bands (p.37–48). Dispersion test grades 1–5 at 15/70/95 s, 5, 10 min (monophasic hold to 95 s, all disperse by 10 min) (p.41–42).
- Allergan compression test: Restylane NASHA reached 1.2 mm inverse gap at **0.2 N**, Juvederm at **1.2 N**; ~120 s recovery; "lifting capacity = G′ × cohesivity" — author: valid only where external force is low (p.43–45).
- Creep test (two monophasic fillers, same viscoelasticity): unloaded gel heights 6.8–6.9 mm; small-particle gel pressed to 2.5 mm → 2 mm at 1 h; large-particle 2 mm → 1 mm at 1 h; recovery difference 2.6 mm; small particles better cohesion/recovery and less centrifugal spread; large particles give bigger soft volume when unloaded (p.46–48).
- Site rule (p.37, 39, 55–56): high external pressure (radix, chin, deep grooves, sub-SMAS under tough Asian tissue) → **high G′**; low-pressure wide areas (anterior cheek) → cohesive softer fillers; superficial layers with planar stretch (periorbital, perioral) → flexible, low-viscoelastic, evenly spreading fillers (Fig 2.56). Very low G′/high cohesion under thin skin → "water balloon" feel (p.40). High-G′ fillers raise compression/ischemia risk and tissue reaction (p.55, 121).
- Chapter-2 practical: intradermal fillers now low cross-link or mixed with non-cross-linked HA to avoid nodules; **"20% saturation" = ~20% volume increase by water uptake** post-injection (p.57). Fully hydrated / biphasic / highly cross-linked monophasic swell less; soft low-cross-link monophasic swell more; free-HA-mixed products lose the free HA in days (biphasic free HA absorbed in **2–3 days**) → apparent early loss at 1–2 weeks (p.57, 122). Pendant-type BDDE products swell more, persistent edema, delayed nodules (p.53, 57).
- Cross-linking: HA half-life in body 1–2 days (p.21); HA turnover half-life 24–48 h (p.212); MoD (C-MOD) typically **1–10%** (p.52); FDA residual BDDE ≤ **2 ppm** (p.51); FDA/US guidance **≤20 ml HA filler per year for a 60 kg adult** (p.53); MoE = gel strength / MoD (p.52). Filler HA MW 1.5–2.5 × 10⁶ Da (p.214).
- Non-HA: CaHA 20–45 μm particles, 30% particles / 70% CMC-glycerin gel; PCL 25–50 μm spheres, 30/70 with CMC, gel absorbed over weeks → volume dips at 2–4 weeks then collagen rebuilds; microspheres <15 μm phagocytosed, >15 μm irregular → granuloma; PLLA no immediate volume; PMMA 3:1 collagen:PMMA (p.18–19).

### Tissue response / volumetric time course (key for imaging)
- Capsule forms ~4 weeks; grid of fibroblasts/vessels/collagen replaces filler as it degrades (p.54). **Type III collagen ≈13.8% of filler volume at 4 weeks, peak ≈21.5% at ~32 weeks** (p.54). HA fillers now said to last **1–2 years or longer**, partly replaced by autologous tissue; some persists after years, shape does not fully revert (p.54).
- **Volume curve of an injected HA shape (p.55)**: volume rises by hydration for 4–8 weeks, **peak ≈1.8× at week 4**; then declines to **75% of initial (injected) volume at week 16**; **weeks 16–64 maintained ≈ initial**. **Max height falls to 2/3 within first 4 weeks** (tissue push), then held. **Width ≈1.8× at 4 weeks**, held weeks 4–32, then declines to ≈ initial by week 64. Duration varies by region hyaluronidase levels, layer, rheology, technique (p.54–55).
- Dermal half-life shorter (high hyaluronidase), deep/supraperiosteal longer — clinically well known; "more research needed" (p.212).
- Transient edema normally resolves in **2–3 days**; excessive cannula undermining worsens it (p.200). Late erythema/neovascularization more with superficial injection or where skin-to-periosteum space is small (p.201).
- Long-term fillers: PCL early volume trough at 2–4 weeks (p.19).

### Injection mechanics
- Cannula vs needle Table 4.2 (p.107). Injection force reduced by smaller syringe diameter, low viscosity, shorter needle/cannula, larger inner diameter (Table 4.3 p.119; Fig 4.48 needle length 11–17 mm). Study (J Cosmet Dermatol 2020, 4 fillers × 30/27/25G): ejection pressure exceeds vascular pressure regardless of injection force → low pressure does not prevent intravascular events (p.118–119). Aspiration: 27G/13 mm needle aspirated blood in 1 s; 25G/40 mm cannula none in 10 s (false negative) (p.211). Author: 23G cannula deep, 25–27G for dermal lines, 23–25G needle for large volumes, 30G dermal; 2 mm cannula still caused blindness cases (p.208, 212). Skin boosters: 30–34G, points 1.5–2 cm apart, needles ≤5 mm, automated devices 34G up to 720 injections/min (p.195).
- Techniques defined p.108–112: retrograde/anterograde, fan, cross-hatch, bolus/volumizing (+molding), linear/continuous threading, serial puncture, micro-puncture/droplet, Mantoux, sandwich (horizontal layers), towering (vertical, supraperiosteal up).
- Nerve blocks: supraorbital ~1 cc (+1 cm above rim), zygomaticofacial 0.5 cc, ext. nasal 0.5 cc, infraorbital 1 cc at foramen ~1 cm below rim, mental foramen 2 cm below commissure, great auricular 6.5 cm from EAC (p.103–105).

### Vascular danger summary
- Facial artery: ascends near mid-mandible / masseter anterior border (deep at bone crossing), medial to NLF >70% Koreans, superficial >50% at NLF/SLA origin, subcutaneous 1.5 cm lateral to commissure; branches narrow upward; angular artery between subQ and muscle; duplex infraorbital trunk ~30% (p.65–66, 154, 173–176, 188). Supraorbital artery splits 1 cm above rim; supratrochlear superficial branch 1.5 mm above rim (p.66, 130). Temple: STA in STF/superficial fat, middle temporal vein 5 mm at 2 cm above arch, sentinel 2 mm; VII temporal branch 1.5–3 cm above arch (p.134–136, 208). Nose: lateral nasal, dorsal nasal, columellar arteries; intercanthal vein (p.165–166). Necrosis predisposition: thick skin/small dermis-to-periosteum space (nose tip), over-injection in one space, subcutaneous plane, prior filler/fat/surgery fibrosis (p.204). Impending necrosis signs 2–3 days (reticular purple), 1–2 days if wide (p.204–205). Infection 2–3 days (p.201).
- Hyaluronidase: Korea 1500 IU vials vs US 150–200; 1 IU = 1 USP (p.214). Old rule 5–15 U per 0.1 ml (~150 U/ml); author minimum **200–300 U per ml soft filler, ≥500 U per ml volumizing**; up to 1500–3000 U at once; in vessels active only ~4–5 min → repeat 10–15 min; tissue activity ~6 h (rat: 0.2 cc filler / 600 IU, gone at 6 h); blood half-life 2 min (p.59–60, 213). In-vitro: 75 U and 375 U insufficient for 1 cc; **750 U dissolves 1 cc biphasic**; monophasic separates and needs 1 h+; PCL insoluble at 24 h (p.124–128). Guideline (p.215–218): sufficient dose, all ischemic areas, **1 cm intervals**, **repeat every 15 min**, massage, penetrate nodule capsule; 750 U at once = 3 × 250 U at 15 min in vitro; consensus 200–300 IU/day historically; 3–15 U/0.1 cc for nodules only.

### Reference proportions (Ch.1, 4)
- Thirds 1:1:1 (West); Asian midface:lower 1:0.8–0.9; fifths 1:1:1:1:1; intercanthal:nasal width ≈1 (p.8). Profile criteria Fig 1.5: glabella-root concavity 135°, tip projection 30–45°, columello-labial 90–100°; 4S lines; heart/inverted-triangle frontal ideal (p.10–11). Follow-up at 2 weeks (p.12).
- Asian-specific procedures Table 1.3 (p.8): smile-effect filler+toxin, oval-face toxin + T-zone filler, lower-lid roll, paranasal, lateral cheek + temple, tip/columella (cat-like), sunken eyelid.

## Assessment

- **Quantitative density: low-to-moderate.** This is primarily a technique/anatomy atlas. It contains almost no per-region injected-volume tables (only lips 0.3–0.4 cc/lip, max 1.5 cc; tubercle 0.1–0.2 cc; pretarsal 0.2–0.4 cc/side), no skin/fat thickness-in-mm tables, and no 3D-imaging outcome numbers.
- **Most valuable quantitative content**: (1) the generic HA volume-vs-time curve (1.8× at 4 wk, 75% at 16 wk, height → 2/3 by 4 wk, width 1.8× then normalizing by 64 wk) and collagen-III fractions (13.8%/21.5%) — usable as a default longevity/edema model; (2) "20% saturation" swelling concept; (3) the phase-angle dataset and the 0.25 tan δ threshold; (4) vessel positional stats (facial artery medial to NLF >70%, within 5 mm; duplex branch ~30%; STA bifurcation 64/36%; VII branch 1.5–3 cm above arch; middle temporal vein 5 mm at 2 cm; frontalis 3–5 mm deep; corrugator 2–3 mm thick; tear trough 2–3 mm below rim, 3 cm long); (5) hyaluronidase dosing.
- **Best-covered regions**: tear trough/infraorbital, midface compartments/spaces (prezygomatic, premaxillary, Ristow's, prebuccal, preparotid-masseteric), temple layering, nose, nasolabial fold, marionette/chin/jawline — all with explicit plane and product-stiffness rules and Asian-vs-Caucasian variation. Forehead, brow, lips covered adequately; masseter, preauricular, neck essentially absent.
- **Population caveat**: all rules are Korean-patient-centric (thicker skin, no midface bone loss, medial apple-cheek apex, medial modiolus), which matters for a region-rule engine intended for mixed ethnicities.

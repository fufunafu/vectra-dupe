IMPORTANT CAVEAT ON SCOPE: This is a cadaver dissection manual for oculofacial/plastic surgeons.
It contains NO filler volumes, NO rheology, NO ultrasound/MRI layer thicknesses, NO longevity or
3D-imaging outcome data. Its value is (a) the layer-stack model per region, (b) fat-compartment
and retaining-ligament definitions with a handful of mm/cm landmark distances, (c) vessel/nerve
course-and-plane facts usable as danger-zone rules. Every number in the book is reproduced below.

## Forehead

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Forehead ("central zone") is bounded laterally by the Temporal Line of Fusion (TLF, = superior temporal line, = conjoint tendon), where superficial temporalis fascia + deep temporalis fascia fuse and attach to periosteum; this is the origin of temporalis. It separates the temporal-fossa zone from the central forehead zone. (AnatFFN p.7–8, Fig 2.3)
  - Endoscopic brow-lift zones: (1) posterior/vertex, (2) frontal/central, (3) temporal. Posterior + central dissection plane = subperiosteal; temporal plane = deep to STF, superficial to DTF. (p.19, Fig 2.34)
  - Frontalis: paired, vertically oriented, originates from and is encased by the galea; inserts into dermis posterior to the brow; laterally terminates just medial to the TLF at the conjoint tendon where periosteum and galea are tightly adherent. (p.8, 11–12)
  - Frontalis is "the only muscle that elevates the brow". (p.28)
- **Layer stack (scalp / forehead — 5 layers, superficial → deep)** (p.5, Fig 2.1; restated p.18, p.32 Fig 3.2)
  1. Skin
  2. Subcutaneous tissue
  3. Galea aponeurosis (continuous with SMAS; splits to encompass frontalis) — in the forehead the "SMAS" = frontalis + procerus, "with the arteries passing superficially on the muscles and the nerves rising on the posterior muscle surface, more deeply within the SMAS" (p.8)
  4. Loose areolar tissue
  5. Periosteum of frontal bone
  - Galea is present on BOTH anterior and posterior surfaces of frontalis. (p.12)
  - Vertically oriented sensory nerves (CN V1: supraorbital, supratrochlear) lie in the subcutaneous plane on the frontalis surface. (p.8, Figs 2.7–2.8)
- **Layer thickness data**
  - Cranium (diploic bone) at the paracentral fixation site "is about 8.1 mm thick"; bone tunnel drilled to 4 mm depth. (p.26) — bone, not soft tissue.
  - Skin thickness (qualitative only): skin "is thicker, again in the eyebrow region" relative to lateral periorbital/temporal. (p.77)
  - Soft-tissue thickness in mm: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book). Surgical planes: subcutaneous plane "safely separates the skin from the SMAS layer" (p.8); composite lifts are subperiosteal in central zone (p.19).
- **Volumes**: (no data in this book)
- **Product / rheology**: (no data in this book)
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Supraorbital nerve emerges from its foramen/notch "at or just above the superior orbital rim"; divides into a medial superficial division and a deep lateral division (rarely separate foramina). Supratrochlear nerve → medial forehead. One or multiple foramina/notches medially at the rim. (p.13–14, 22)
  - Supratrochlear artery shown running on the frontalis surface within the galea layer (Fig 2.1, p.6). Arteries are superficial on the muscle; nerves on the posterior muscle surface. (p.8)
  - Supraorbital nerve block location = the foramen at the rim. (p.13)
- **Variation**: (no data in this book) — only "with age…forehead and brow aging" qualitative (p.5).

## Glabella

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Procerus: vertical muscle in glabella; origin fascia of inferior nasal bones; inserts dermis between eyebrows, between paired frontalis muscles. Contraction → horizontal glabellar rhytids. (p.23, 28)
  - Corrugator supercilii: two heads. Transverse head originates from medial aspect of superior orbital rim; oblique head from superior portion of medial orbital rim. Each inserts into dermis just superior to the medial third of the brow / beneath medial brow. Lies deep to frontalis. Contraction pulls brow medially and inferiorly → vertical and oblique glabellar rhytids. (p.23, 28)
  - Depressor supercilii: medial brow depressor; origin frontal process of maxilla above the medial canthal tendon; inserts dermis under medial brow; described as "medial fibers of the orbicularis oculi muscle". (p.23, 28)
  - Frontalis fibers interdigitate with orbital orbicularis oculi and centrally with procerus in the glabellar region. (p.11)
  - Corrugator + orbicularis interdigitate at the arcus marginalis of the superior orbital rim. (p.77, Fig 3.125)
  - Glabellar muscles innervated by frontal (temporal) or zygomatic branches of CN VII. (p.23)
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book). Book notes these muscles are "a frequent desired target of chemodenervation therapy". (p.24)
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Supratrochlear and supraorbital neurovascular bundles emerge at the medial superior orbital rim (p.13–14). Nasal orbital fat may be encountered in the orbital roof at endoscopic release (p.22).
  - No explicit glabellar filler danger statement; see "Nose" for the nasal-artery filler danger zone that includes the dorsal nasal / angular arteries (p.40, Fig 3.31).
- **Variation**: (no data in this book)

## Brow

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Aesthetic target: "the peak or highest curve of the arch of each eyebrow should be at the level of the lateral canthus". (p.24)
  - Frontalis inserts into dermis posterior to the brow (p.11, Fig 2.10). Brow depressors: procerus, corrugator, depressor supercilii, medial orbital orbicularis (p.10, Fig 2.9).
  - Brow ptosis in CN VII palsy (frontal branch). (p.13)
- **Layer thickness data**: skin thicker in the eyebrow region (qualitative, p.77). Otherwise (no data in this book).
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book). Surgical: supraciliary / pretrichial / mid-forehead lifts are subcutaneous-plane; endoscopic/coronal lifts move layers as a composite. (p.5, 11–12)
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Frontal branch of facial nerve reaches "a point 1.5 cm above the lateral end of the eyebrow (the Pitanguy line)"; above the arch it is in the superficial temporalis fascia (see Temple). (p.28)
  - Temporal branches seen in the STF "about 1.5 cm superior-lateral to the tail of the brow". (p.67, Fig 3.99)
- **Variation**: (no data in this book)

## Temple

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Temporal zone = lateral to the TLF/conjoint tendon; temporalis originates at the TLF. (p.7–8, 14)
  - Pitanguy line: drawn on skin from near the tragus to the lateral aspect of the eyebrow; marks where the frontal branch crosses the zygomatic arch. (p.15–16, Fig 2.26)
- **Layer stack (temporal region, superficial → deep)** (p.6–7, Fig 2.2)
  1. Skin
  2. Subcutaneous fat
  3. Superficial temporalis fascia (STF = temporoparietal fascia; continuous with galea above and SMAS below). **Superficial temporal artery runs within the superficial lamina of the multilaminar STF; frontal branch of facial nerve runs on the posterior/deep surface of the STF.** (p.6, 14)
  4. Loose areolar tissue plane (glide plane; "add[s] protection to the facial nerve" when dissecting here) (p.6, 14, 20)
  5. Deep temporalis fascia (DTF = intermediate/innominate fascia) overlying temporalis. Above the superior orbital rim level it is one layer; **it splits about 2.5 cm above the zygomatic arch** into superficial-DTF and deep-DTF enveloping the **superficial temporal fat pad (Yasargil's fat pad)** before inserting on the superior aspect of the arch. (p.7, 16, 20)
  6. Temporalis muscle
  7. Deep temporal fat pad (drawn deep to temporalis/under arch in Fig 2.2); periosteum & temporal-fossa bone. (p.7, 17)
  - Thus below the superior orbital rim there are THREE fascial layers: STF, superficial layer of DTF, deep layer of DTF. (p.7)
  - Buccal fat pad posterior lobe has a "temporal process/extension" that runs superficial to temporalis under the zygomatic arch; the lateral SOOF "abuts the deep temporal extension of the buccal fat pad". (p.72, 74–76, Figs 3.116–3.117)
- **Layer thickness data**
  - Skin "thinning somewhat in the temporal region" (qualitative). (p.77)
  - DTF split point: ~2.5 cm above zygomatic arch (p.16, 20). No mm thicknesses. (no further data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book). Surgical rule: "Superior to the zygomatic arch, surgical dissection must proceed deep to the superficial temporalis fascia to avoid injuring the facial nerve"; "maintaining temporal dissection in the plane of the superficial temporal fat [Yasargil] will prevent injury to the overlying facial nerve". (p.16, 20) The STF↔DTF loose plane is also the Gillies elevator plane for arch fractures (p.15, 20).
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Superficial temporal artery: "deep to the skin but in the superficial layer of the multi-lamella of the superficial temporalis fascia" (p.14, Fig 2.19); anterior branch of STA shown at hemicoronal incision (Fig 2.4, p.9).
  - Frontal branch of CN VII: crosses the **middle third of the zygomatic arch as 3–5 rami "in a deep, pre-periosteal plane"**, then runs superior-medially to a point 1.5 cm above the lateral end of the eyebrow, transitioning from deep at the arch to superficial in the STF above the arch. (p.15, 28, 79) Emerges "just over the periosteum" at the middle third of the arch (p.15).
  - Sentinel vein (medial zygomaticotemporal vein): traverses the deep plane to enter the STF adjacent to the frontal branch; "essential indicator of proximity" to the nerve; cautery could injure the nerve. (p.16–17, 20–21, Figs 2.27–2.28, 2.31, 2.39)
  - Deep temporal nerves and vessels (zygomaticotemporal vein) perforate the undersurface of temporalis. (p.17)
- **Variation**: (no data in this book)

## Upper eyelid & periorbital

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Orbicularis oculi: originates medial orbital margin; pretarsal / preseptal / orbital components; pretarsal firmly attached to anterior tarsus; extends to Whitnall's lateral orbital tubercle; preseptal + orbital insert into lateral palpebral raphe on the zygoma. Only the orbital portion of lower-lid orbicularis is considered midface. (p.52, Fig 3.62)
  - Orbital orbicularis originates from nasal process of maxilla and inserts into subcutaneous tissue of eyebrow. (p.28)
  - Arcus marginalis at superior orbital rim; incising it exposes post-septal orbital fat and orbital roof. Nasal orbital fat in the orbital roof. (p.22, 29)
  - Detailed eyelid anatomy deferred to companion volume (Anatomy of the Eyelid, Orbit, and Lacrimal System, 2022). (p.37, 52)
- **Layer thickness data**
  - **Eyelid skin ≈ 400 microns** — "some of the thinnest in the body"; skin "dramatically thins as the eyelid margin is approached". (p.36)
  - **No subcutaneous fat at the preseptal and pretarsal lower lid** (orbicularis directly under skin), versus "a moderate degree of fat" in the midface. (p.47, Fig 3.46)
- **Injection plane & technique / Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**: supraorbital/supratrochlear bundles at superomedial rim (p.13–14); lacrimal artery shown in Fig 3.78 (p.60). Zygomatic + buccal branches innervate orbicularis oculi (p.67).
- **Variation**: (no data in this book)

## Tear trough & infraorbital

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Nasojugal fold (tear trough): located below the medial aspect of the inferior palpebral crease of the lower lid, extends inferolaterally at **45 degrees**. (p.32–33, Fig 3.3)
  - Malar fold: originates lateral to and below the lateral canthus at the maxilla–zygoma junction, extends inferomedially at **45°** until it meets the nasojugal fold **about 15 mm below the center of the lower lid margin**. (p.33)
  - Dense subcutaneous attachments at the nasojugal fold (Fig 3.18, p.37; Fig 3.73, p.58).
  - **Orbitomalar ligament**: arises from inferior orbital rim; travels superficially through the SMAS/orbicularis, through subcutaneous fat, to insert on dermis in a "fan-like" fashion. Firmly attached deeply to periosteum of maxilla at inferior and lateral orbital rim. **Extends from 5 mm lateral to the lateral orbital rim toward the nasojugal fold overlying the anterior lacrimal crest; strongest laterally.** (p.57–58, Figs 3.73–3.75)
  - Sagittal section at lid–cheek junction (Fig 3.75, p.59), superficial → deep at rim: orbital septum / preseptal orbicularis / post-septal orbital fat / orbital orbicularis (orbital portion) / orbitomalar ligament / **SOOF** / sub-SMAS malar fat.
  - **SOOF (suborbicularis oculi fat)**: layer of fat sandwiched between the orbital portion of orbicularis oculi and the periosteum of the inferior orbital rim; located caudal to the orbitomalar ligament. Two pads: (a) **lateral SOOF** — bounded superiorly by a horizontal line from the lateral orbital wall; temporally abuts the deep temporal extension of the buccal fat pad; medially borders medial SOOF. (b) **medial SOOF** — deep fat at the inferior orbital rim; **inferior boundary of the SOOF is the tear trough**; superiorly extends over the rim toward the tarsus. (p.72, Figs 3.86, 3.114)
  - Sub-SMAS malar (deep) fat: superior edge of both its components abuts the inferior edge of the SOOF, "providing volumetric blending across the lid-cheek junction in youth. With age, separation occurs resulting in the nasojugal and malar creases." (p.72–74)
  - Levator labii superioris originates below the medial infraorbital rim, extending inferiorly from orbital orbicularis, covers the infraorbital foramen. (p.53, 63)
- **Layer thickness data**: no subcutaneous fat in preseptal/pretarsal lower lid (p.47). Otherwise (no data in this book).
- **Injection plane & technique / Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Infraorbital foramen: the infraorbital vessels and infraorbital nerve pass through the anterior lobe of the buccal fat pad (p.74, Fig 3.117); levator labii superioris covers the foramen (p.63); levator anguli oris originates ~1 cm inferior to the foramen (p.53).
  - Angular artery = continuation of facial artery at lateral side of nose (p.68, Fig 3.103); infraorbital artery and dorsal nasal artery shown in Fig 3.31 (p.41).
- **Variation (age)**: "With age, the orbitomalar ligament and skin lose elasticity causing the SOOF and sub-SMAS deep fat to descend. As a result, the orbital rim, nasojugal and malar folds become more prominent. Additionally, the orbital septum attenuates, allowing the post septal orbital fat to prolapse creating the appearance of 'bags' under the eye." (p.58) Qualitative only.

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (deep/superficial fat compartments)

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - **Midface definition**: superior border = horizontal line lateral canthus → medial canthus; inferior border = line from inferior edge of tragus → lateral oral commissure. Subdivided into anterior vs lateral midface by a line from the lateral orbital rim to the oral commissure. **Anterior midface = the "triangular" area between lower eyelid and nasolabial fold.** Lateral midface extends over the zygomatic arch and includes the zygomatic-malar and submalar regions. (p.31–32, Fig 3.1)
  - Three midface folds: nasojugal, malar, nasolabial (+ mesolabial in lower face). "In youth, the midface and lower face is full, and uniform, without evident folds or creases. However, with time, the contour of the midface and lower face deflates and descends, leading to the prominence of the aforementioned facial folds." (p.32–33)
- **Layer stack (face — 5 layers, analogous to scalp)** (p.32, Fig 3.2; and Fig 3.118 p.76)
  1. Skin (epidermis, papillary dermis, reticular dermis) — "thin and firmly adherent to the underlying superficial fat and fascia in the midface" so muscles and skin move as a unit (p.33)
  2. Subcutaneous tissue / fat (= superficial malar fat pad; connected to dermis by retinacular cutis fibers, p.36, 51)
  3. SMAS (fibromuscular sheath encasing muscles of facial expression, parotid, CN VII branches, facial artery/vein branches). **SMAS is thickest over the parotid and thinnest as it extends medially toward nasalis.** Boundaries: superotemporally joins temporoparietal fascia near the frontozygomatic suture (= superior end of facial SMAS); superomedially invests orbicularis oculi beyond the inferior orbital rim; laterally fuses with parotid fascia; inferiorly continuous with platysma at the angle of the mandible. Anchored to periosteum "at only a few locations, by true retaining ligaments". (p.50–52, Fig 3.61)
  4. Retaining ligaments + loose areolar tissue ("glide planes")
  5. Deep fascia and muscles of mastication / parotid / oral cavity (periosteum only over bone)
  - **Superficial (subcutaneous) fat compartments of the cheek** — one continuous layer "somewhat arbitrarily" divided: **nasolabial, medial, central, lateral** (malar fat pad); in the mandibular region: **superior jowl fat, inferior jowl fat** (continuous with the superficial malar fat pads). (p.47, 49, 72, Figs 3.47, 3.55, 3.113)
  - **Deep (sub-SMAS) fat compartments**: medial SOOF, lateral SOOF, **sub-SMAS (deep) medial cheek fat**, **sub-SMAS (deep) lateral cheek fat**. (Fig 3.114, p.73)
    - Sub-SMAS malar fat ("preperiosteal fat"): large pad deep to mimetic muscles, attached to both zygomatic periosteum and overlying musculature. (a) **Medial component** near the lateral border of levator labii superioris alaeque nasi at the nasolabial fold; traces inferomedially behind orbicularis oris into the upper lip. (b) **Lateral component** bounded by the bony attachment of the SMAS to the zygoma; superiorly extends beyond the inferior orbital rim to meet the SOOF; inferolaterally joins the superficial extension of the buccal fat pad. (p.72–74)
    - "The sub-SMAS or deep malar fat overlies the periosteum below the orbital rim, deep to the mimetic muscles and abuts the SOOF compartment." (p.74)
  - Subcutaneous fat adherence map: "firmly attached to the orbicularis muscle at the orbital rim and parotid area, but more loosely attached in the region of the nasojugal and nasolabial folds as well as over the mandible"; looser over levator labii superioris / LLSAN. (p.48)
  - "The malar fat pad accounts for malar prominence in youth but descends with time. Interdigitation of the superficial fat with the underlying SMAS facilitates surgical mobilization of the skin, subcutaneous fat, and superficial fascial layers as a single aesthetic unit." (p.47)
  - **Zygomatico-cutaneous ligament (zygomatic ligament / McGregor's patch)**: firm attachment of SMAS on the zygomatic bone, lateral to the origin of zygomaticus major, **approximately 45 mm anterior to the tragus**; curvilinear, extends anteriorly and medially above the origin of zygomaticus minor to the inferior border of the zygoma. **Average width ≈ 14.5 mm.** McGregor's patch = zygomatic + masseteric ligaments; signifies proximity of a perforating branch of the transverse facial artery, parotid fascia, and zygomatic branches of CN VII. Lies deep to the lateral superficial malar fat pad. (p.58–59, Figs 3.74, 3.76–3.77)
  - Glide planes: loose areolar tissue between retaining ligaments lets SMAS move over deep fascia/periosteum; "these glide planes enlarge or stretch with age due to laxity of the retaining ligaments and contribute to descent". "Blood vessels and nerves do not enter the glide planes" → safe surgical planes. (p.61)
  - Sub-SMAS plane "is quite avascular except for a perforating branch of the transverse facial artery which travels within McGregor's patch." (p.61)
  - Midfacial elevator muscles (5, within SMAS): levator labii superioris, LLSAN, levator anguli oris (origin maxilla ~1 cm inferior to infraorbital foramen → outer lip/modiolus), zygomaticus major (origin zygomatic arch → modiolus; "most laterally located muscle involved in lip elevation"; creates dimples), zygomaticus minor (origin maxillary process of zygoma → lip just lateral to LLS insertion). (p.52–54)
- **Layer thickness data**
  - Subcutaneous fat: "moderate degree" in midface vs none in lower lid (qualitative). (p.47)
  - Skin: "Observe the skin thickness in the malar region thinning in the lateral periorbital region and thinning somewhat in the temporal region. It is thicker, again in the eyebrow region… It also thins in the posterior auricular region." (qualitative, p.77)
  - SMAS thickness gradient: thickest over parotid → thinnest near nasalis (qualitative). (p.50)
  - mm values: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: (no data in this book) — book only says "Retention of midface volume is key to a youthful appearance" and that the buccal fat intermediate lobe "is the target of cosmetic fillers" (p.74; see Lateral cheek / submalar).
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Transverse facial artery perforator in McGregor's patch (p.59, 61, Fig 3.79); zygomatic branches of CN VII in same location (Fig 3.77, 3.79).
  - Facial nerve branches innervate mimetic muscles from their deep surface (except buccinator and levator anguli oris) → "surgical midface dissection can occur in the anterior subcutaneous plane without concern for disruption of innervation"; surgeon should "remain in a plane superficial to the zygomatic major and zygomatic minor muscles to avoid injury to the facial nerve branches". (p.66–67)
  - Angular artery / infraorbital artery courses (Fig 3.31 p.41; Fig 3.78 p.60).
- **Variation (age/weight)**: "Locations and sizes of fat pads in the subcutaneous tissue, and sub-SMAS or deep plane vary within individuals depending on a patient's age and weight." (p.74) Retinacular fibers attenuate with age → prolapse of subcutaneous tissue and development of malar, nasojugal, nasolabial, mesolabial folds. (p.51) Cites Gierloff et al. 2012 CT study of midfacial fat-compartment aging in Further Reading (p.85) but reproduces no numbers.

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch (incl. submalar)

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Lateral midface "extends over the zygomatic arch and includes the zygomatic malar and submalar regions". (p.31, Fig 3.1)
  - Lateral superficial cheek fat compartment (Fig 3.113); sub-SMAS lateral cheek fat bounded by SMAS-to-zygoma bony attachment (p.74).
  - **Buccal fat pad (Bichat's)**: deepest fat pad, between buccinator (medial) and masseter / zygomaticus major & minor (lateral); "acts as a gliding pad"; sits in the pre-periosteal space; three lobes: (1) **anterior lobe** — triangular/rectangular, overlies oral cavity, vertices at anterior edge of buccinator, LLSAN, orbicularis oris; extends superiorly up to the infraorbital foramen; posterior to zygomaticus major/minor; **parotid duct, anterior facial vein, infraorbital vessels and infraorbital nerve (and buccal branch CN VII) pass through it**. (2) **intermediate lobe** — between anterior and posterior lobes, overlying lateral wall of maxilla; "much more prominent in children and loses a significant amount of volume in adulthood from long standing chewing and sucking. Its loss of volume is implicated in the formation of hollow cheeks and nasolabial folds. **It is the target of cosmetic fillers.**" (3) **posterior lobe** — large, variable, extends from superficial to temporalis under the zygomatic arch, over the infraorbital foramen, down to the mandibular rim; four processes: buccal, pterygopalatine, pterygoid, temporal. (p.74–76, Figs 3.115–3.117)
  - Skin over zygoma: retinacular cutis attachments to dermis must be cut (Fig 3.14, p.36).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique**: only "intermediate lobe [of buccal fat] … is the target of cosmetic fillers" (p.74) — i.e., deep submalar plane. No technique.
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Frontal branch CN VII over the **middle third** of the zygomatic arch, deep/pre-periosteal, 3–5 rami (p.28, 79). SMAS-plication anchoring bite is placed over the **most lateral third** of the arch, "safely placed lateral to the nerve course". (p.79)
  - Transverse facial artery runs with zygomatic branches under the lateral malar fat (Fig 3.79, p.61).
  - Parotid duct crosses the masseter **approximately 2 cm inferior to the zygomatic arch**, diameter **about 2–3 mm**; buccal branches of CN VII run parallel to it (superior or inferior); duct pierces buccinator and opens lateral to the 2nd maxillary molar; accessory parotid gland sometimes present. (p.65, Figs 3.92–3.97)
- **Variation**: buccal intermediate lobe atrophy from childhood → adulthood (qualitative, p.74).

## Preauricular

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Skin anterior to tragus is thin; perichondrium immediately beneath (p.33). "The initial 2 cm flap elevation is preferably performed with a 10 blade because **the skin is densely adherent to the pre-auricular fascia with little intervening subcutaneous fat**." (p.82, Fig 3.138)
  - SMAS is thickest over the parotid; fuses with parotid fascia (p.50, 52). Parotid enclosed in parotid sheath continuous with deep cervical fascia (p.65).
  - Masseteric cutaneous ligaments: **begin approximately 42 mm anterior to the tragus and end 25 mm posterior to the superoanterior edge of the masseter**; extend superiorly almost to the zygomatic arch and inferiorly beyond the mandibular edge. (p.59–61, Figs 3.80–3.81, 3.89)
  - Facelift markings (superficial facelift): point 1 = ½ way from superior tragus to lateral corner of mouth; point 2 = 5–6 cm inferior to pinna base; point 3 midway. Cadaver extension: 8 cm anterior-inferiorly on a line from superior tragus to lateral oral commissure; 9 cm inferior to pinna base. (p.75, 77) Deep plane entry line: gonion → lateral canthus; subcutaneous dissection carried 2–4 cm beyond it; 1 cm SMAS cuff preserved at the pre-tragal incision. (p.82–83)
- **Layer thickness data**: skin thins in the posterior auricular region (p.77); "very little subcutaneous tissue" over helix cartilage (p.45); retro-auricular skin has "more subcutaneous tissue and is more mobile" (p.46). No mm.
- **Injection plane & technique / Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - Facial nerve trunk exits the stylomastoid foramen; parotid plexus → temporal, zygomatic, buccal, mandibular, cervical branches; temporal branch passes in front of the tragus to cross the arch. (p.65–67, Figs 3.98–3.99)
  - Greater auricular nerve: ascends over SCM; found **6.5 cm inferior to the external auditory canal (McKinney's point)**; vulnerable in retro-auricular skin elevation. (p.46–47, 79, 83, Figs 3.44, 3.126)
  - External jugular vein and posterior auricular nerve in the retro-auricular/stylomastoid region (p.46–47).
  - Superficial temporal artery, maxillary, posterior auricular, occipital arteries shown in Fig 3.78 (p.60).
- **Variation**: (no data in this book)

## Nose

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Nasal mimetic muscles: procerus (elevator), anomalous nasi (elevator), transverse nasalis/compressor naris, compressor narium minor, dilator naris anterior, alar nasalis (depressor), LLSAN (elevator), depressor septi nasi, orbicularis oris. (p.38–41, Fig 3.24)
  - Transverse nasalis: origin maxilla lateral to incisive fossa, sweeps to nasal bridge; "Bunny lines" Botox target. Alar nasalis: origin frontal process of maxilla just superior to lateral incisor, inserts superior to lateral crus of major alar cartilage. ## Lips & perioral

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Orbicularis oris: superior and inferior portions; fibers "firmly insert directly onto the dermis around the mouth"; composed of muscle plus fibrous membranes from other muscles; origins include buccinator fibers, nasal septum, area lateral to incisive fossa of mandible. (p.55)
  - Buccinator superior fibers → inferior orbicularis oris; inferior fibers → superior orbicularis oris (decussation). (p.69, Fig 3.108)

## Jawline & prejowl

## Masseter & mandibular angle

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Masseter: largest/strongest muscle of mastication; superficial and deep portions (Fig 3.65); moves mandible posteriorly and superiorly; lies within the deep fascia. An areolar plane overlies the masseter (sub-SMAS/sub-platysmal glide plane). Risorius originates from masseteric fascia. (p.56, 64, 61)

Lateral pterygoid: upper head from infratemporal fossa of sphenoid → TMJ capsule; lower head from lateral surface of lateral pterygoid plate. (p.63–64)

## Neck

- **Anatomy / boundaries**
  - Layers at C7 cross-section (Fig 4.2, p.89): skin → superficial fascia (with platysma) → investing layer of deep cervical fascia → (SCM, infrahyoid strap muscles) → pretracheal fascia / carotid sheath → prevertebral fascia.
  - **Platysma = the SMAS in the neck**; broad sheet within the superficial fascia, arising from fascia over pectoralis major and deltoid, crossing the clavicle; anterior fibers interlace below the mental symphysis; lateral fibers → lower border of mandibular body; posterior fibers cross mandible and lower masseter. Anterior portion is the thickest. Innervated by cervical branch CN VII. (p.94–96, Fig 4.14)
  - Deep to platysma the SMAS (superficial cervical fascia) blends with the posterior cervical fascia (p.96). A sub-platysmal loose areolar/glide plane exists (p.61).
  - Subcutaneous fat "varies in thickness" (Fig 4.13, p.94; qualitative). Submental pre-platysmal fat is the liposuction target (p.80, 94).
  - Triangles: posterior (SCM / trapezius / middle third clavicle; roof investing fascia; floor scalenes), subdivided into occipital + subclavian (omoclavicular) by the inferior belly of omohyoid; accessory nerve bisects occipital triangle. Anterior triangle (midline / anterior SCM / mandible) subdivided into carotid, submandibular (digastric), submental, muscular. Submental triangle: between anterior digastric bellies, hyoid inferiorly, roof investing fascia (SMAS), floor mylohyoid; contains small veins → anterior jugular vein, submental nodes, "a variable amount of fat". (p.89–93, 99)
  - Submandibular gland almost fills the submandibular triangle (p.98).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique / Volumes / Product / Surface response**: (no data in this book). Surgical: platysma resected elliptically and sutured to pre-hyoid tissue to sharpen the cervicomental angle and reduce banding; inferior extent of submental dissection = thyroid cartilage. (p.80–82)
- **Danger zones / vessels**
  - **External jugular vein** lies in the SMAS deep to platysma; begins posterior to the angle of the mandible, crosses SCM superficially, pierces the investing fascia **about 3 cm superior to the clavicle** to drain into the subclavian vein. (p.96–97)
  - **Great auricular nerve** ascends on SCM; McKinney's point 6.5 cm below the external auditory canal (p.47, 79, 83). Transverse cervical nerve and supraclavicular nerves emerge at posterior border of SCM (p.94, 96).
  - Marginal mandibular nerve at the submandibular gland's superior aspect; facial artery/vein obliquely over the gland's posterior portion; lingual and hypoglossal nerves superior/inferior to the deep process — all extracapsular. (p.82)
  - Carotid sheath (common/internal carotid, IJV, vagus) in carotid triangle (p.102–103).
- **Variation (age)**: "With age, the platysma separates into vertically oriented 'platysmal bands'" — treated by plication or chemodenervation. (p.95)

## Assessment

- **How quantitative**: Low. This is a surgical dissection manual; it gives roughly 20 landmark distances (cm/mm), one skin thickness (eyelid ≈ 400 µm), one bone thickness (8.1 mm calvarium), and zero soft-tissue layer thicknesses, zero filler volumes, zero rheology, zero longevity, zero imaging/volumetric outcome numbers. Nothing here should be used as a per-region volumetric response rule; it should be used to DEFINE regions and layer stacks, and to seed danger-zone constraints.
- **Best-covered regions**: (1) Temple/forehead — the clearest layer stack (STF/DTF/Yasargil split at 2.5 cm, frontal-branch depth transition, Pitanguy line, sentinel vein). (2) Midface — superficial vs deep fat-compartment nomenclature (nasolabial/medial/central/lateral + jowl; medial/lateral SOOF + deep medial/lateral cheek fat), the three true retaining ligaments with tragus-referenced distances (45 mm, 42 mm, 25 mm, width 14.5 mm, 5 mm), SMAS boundary/thickness gradient, and the buccal fat pad lobes. (3) Jawline — the two explicit filler/nerve danger zones (facial artery at mandible; marginal mandibular nerve 3 cm / 4 cm safety rule).
- **Weak/absent**: lips, chin, nasolabial (anatomy only), nose (vessel map only), neck (surgical anatomy only), upper eyelid (deferred to companion volume). No sex/ethnicity data at all; age effects are qualitative.
- **Use for the scanner project**: adopt its region boundaries (midface superior/inferior lines, anterior vs lateral midface split at lateral-orbital-rim→commissure line, lower face = tragus→commissure line to mandibular border), its superficial/deep compartment naming for the malar region, and its ligament positions as region boundary anchors (zygomatic ligament ~45 mm anterior to tragus; masseteric ligament band 42 mm anterior to tragus back to the masseter edge; orbitomalar ligament arc from 5 mm lateral of lateral orbital rim to the anterior lacrimal crest). Fetch Gierloff 2012 for actual compartment volume-change numbers.

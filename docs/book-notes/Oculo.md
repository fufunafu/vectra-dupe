## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Frontalis is the only elevator of the forehead and brow; it "does not extend lateral to the temporal fusion line, resulting in more significant brow ptosis laterally" (Oculo p. 178). At the vertical midline of the forehead "the frontalis is either nonexistent or extremely attenuated" — no need to inject there (p. 200).
  - Depressors of the brow: orbicularis oculi (medial part), corrugator supercilii, procerus (medial depressors); lateral orbicularis (lateral depressor) (p. 202).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique (botulinum toxin)**
  - Horizontal forehead lines: frontalis, "usually 6–10 injection sites", symmetrical across medial and lateral frontalis, avoiding the area directly above the eyebrows; aim needle upward to avoid toxin spread over the orbital rim onto the levator; "Injections should not be deep to the level of the periosteum" (pp. 199–200).
  - Blepharospasm protocol adds "1–3 units into frontalis muscles centrally" (p. 139).
- **Volumes/doses**
  - Frontalis BTX: men 20–25 units, women 15–20 units (p. 199).
  - "Men and older patients generally require higher doses than women and younger patients" (p. 199).
- **Product/rheology**: HA fillers listed for "forehead contouring" (p. 207); no product specifics.
- **Expected surface response & longevity**
  - BTX effect starts 2–3 days, maximal 7–10 days; touch-up at 7–10 (or 10–14) days (pp. 199, 202, 204).
  - "Less dynamic areas, such as the forehead or glabella, may last up to 6 months"; high-dynamic areas (crow's feet, lips) ~3 months (p. 204). Classic re-treatment interval 3–6 months; "Baby Botox" = smaller dose repeated ~monthly (pp. 204–205).
  - Blepharospasm-dose BTX "generally stops contractions for 4–6 months"; onset of paralysis 24–48 h, maximum at 7–10 days, "usually lasts 4–6 months" (pp. 139, 141).
  - Best BTX wrinkle response ages 30–50 (p. 198). Wrinkle grading scale 1 (faint line) to 6 (deep furrow) (Fig. 2, p. 199).
- **Danger zones / nerves (surface landmarks, from the anaesthesia chapter)**
  - Supratrochlear nerve ≈ **1 cm** lateral to forehead midline; supraorbital nerve ≈ **2 cm** lateral to midline, on the supraorbital ridge (p. 19, Fig. 1).
  - Supraorbital nerve exits the notch/foramen "at 0.5–0.7 cm above the supraorbital margin" (p. 19).
  - Supratrochlear block point: 1 cm medial to the supraorbital notch/foramen on the upper orbital margin (p. 19). Combined block: infiltrate medial two-thirds of eyebrow, 4–5 mL LA (p. 19).
  - Supraorbital + supratrochlear arteries accompany the nerves (neurovascular bundle) (p. 227, Corneal Neurotization chapter).
  - Temporal branch of facial nerve at risk in endoscopic/coronal/pretrichial lifts (p. 180). Frontalis-flap chapter: "The frontal or temporal branch of the facial nerve passes approximately 1.5 cm at the lateral brow to enter the undersurface of the frontalis muscle" (p. 118).
- **Variation**: sex (BTX dose above; male brow flatter/lower, female arched/higher, p. 178).

## Glabella

- **Anatomy**: corrugators are the principal frown muscle; procerus always co-treated; nasalis for bunny lines (p. 200).
- **Injection plane & technique (BTX)**: "inverted triangle with the apex at the root of the nose. Usually 5 sites are injected with a total amount of 10–15 units" (p. 200, Fig. 6). Corrugator/procerus injections are deeper than the superficial orbicularis injections (p. 139). Blepharospasm protocol: 5–10 U corrugator, 1–2 U procerus (p. 139).
- **Fillers**: "Correction of glabellar lines" listed as an upper-face HA indication; ideal combination = BTX first then filler for residual static lines, "Glabellar lines is a good example" (p. 207). Glabella named among regions "of superficial location of the artery" with higher risk of vascular occlusion (cheeks, glabella, nasal ala, temples) (p. 205).
- **Danger zones**: vascular occlusion → skin necrosis / vision loss; "The use of blunt microcannulas has minimized the risk of ecchymosis and virtually eliminated the risk of intravascular injection" (p. 207).
- **Longevity**: BTX up to 6 months (p. 204).
- **Layer thickness / volumes / variation**: (no data in this book)

## Brow

- **Anatomy/boundaries — ideal brow position**: "completely above the superior orbital rim, with the medial end on the same vertical line with the ala of the nose, the lateral end at the extension of a line from the ala to the lateral canthus, and the apex on a vertical line directly above the lateral limbus" (p. 178, Fig. 1). Male brow flatter and lower; female arched and higher (p. 178).
- **Aging mechanism**: "The etiology of involutional brow descent is multi-factorial": gravity loosening attachments of brow to supraorbital rim; frontalis absent lateral to temporal fusion line → lateral brow ptosis; "Volume loss through fat deflation also results in brow descent. Reinflation of the brow with dermal fillers or autogenous fat is effective in those cases of volume loss" (p. 178).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no data)
- **Injection plane & technique**
  - Chemical brow lift: differential BTX in depressors vs elevators; lower a too-high brow by focally weakening frontalis above it; "Jack Nicholson" brow prevented with small BTX into lateral frontalis; treating only medial+lateral frontalis gives central arching (pp. 202–203, Fig. 9).
  - Surgical: direct browplasty "gives the greatest amount of lift per millimeter of tissue resected"; external browpexy incision 1–2 cm supraciliary; internal browpexy via bleph incision, "elevation obtained from a browpexy is modest at best" (pp. 179–180).
- **Volumes**: HA fillers indicated for "superior sulcus deformity" (p. 207). No mL.
- **Expected surface response**: upper blepharoplasty itself lowers the brow: "Excessive skin removal may result in descent of the brow" (p. 177).
- **Danger zones**: supraorbital nerve at medial superior orbital rim (p. 179); see Forehead.
- **Variation**: sex (above).

## Temple

- Listed HA indication: "temporal fossa hollowing" (p. 207). Temples named as a region where the artery is superficial → higher occlusion risk (p. 205).
- Zygomaticotemporal nerve: exits zygomaticotemporal foramen, runs between deep and superficial layers of deep temporal fascia; innervates an area "3 cm lateral to lateral canthus and of 3 cm diameter"; block at lateral orbital edge at frontozygomatic suture level, or palpate lateral orbital edge 1 cm superiorly then 0.5–1 cm posteriorly into a groove; deep+superficial 5 mL LA (p. 22).
- All else: (no data in this book)

## Upper eyelid & periorbital

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Upper lid fat: medial fat pad and central preaponeurotic fat (behind septum, anterior to levator) (pp. 177–178); lacrimal gland lateral (prolapse repositioned to fossa behind superior rim, p. 178).
  - Eyelid skin "is the thinnest in the body and is devoid of subcutaneous fat" (p. 181). Orbicularis: pretarsal, preseptal, preorbital (orbital) parts (p. 181).
  - Orbicularis oculi extent (for BTX planning): "extends 1.4 cm above the rim, 1.2 cm below the rim, and 2.5 cm laterally" (p. 139).
  - East Asian lid: absent/low crease "due to more distal insertion of the orbital septum and more inferior extension of extraconal orbital fat" (p. 123).
- **Eyelid dimensions (measurements)**
  - Lid crease height (cosmetic bleph chapter): female 8–10 mm, male 6–8 mm (p. 177).
  - Lid crease height (adult ptosis chapter): non-Asian male 6–9 mm, female 8–11 mm; Asian male 4–6 mm, female 6–8 mm (p. 129).
  - Skin to retain between inferior brow cilia and lashes after bleph: ≈18–20 mm, equal both sides (p. 177). Alternative marking: 10–15 mm between upper lid margin and lower border of eyebrow (p. 129–130).
  - MRD1 (upper lid margin to corneal reflex): mean ≈5 mm in whites, 2–4 mm in Asians (p. 123). Ptosis severity: mild 2 mm, moderate 3 mm, severe 4 mm (p. 85).
  - Levator function (lid excursion down- to up-gaze, brow fixed): >13 mm excellent, 10–13 very good, 8–10 good, 5–7 fair, ≤4 poor (p. 123); congenital chapter: excellent 13–15, very good 10–13, good 8–10, fair 5–7, poor ≤4 mm (p. 85).
  - Normal horizontal palpebral fissure 25–30 mm; blepharophimosis 18–22 mm (p. 106).
  - Upper tarsus: keep a minimum of 4 mm of tarsus in the upper lid during reconstruction (p. 155–156). Lower tarsus "generally less than 5 mm" (p. 182).
  - Local anaesthetic infiltration ≤1.5 mL per upper eyelid (p. 130); anaesthesia chapter: limit LA volume to <1 mL in the upper lid to avoid levator paralysis confounding height adjustment (p. 18).
  - Levator resection table (Congenital Ptosis, Table 1, p. 86): for ptosis 2/3/4 mm — LF 12–15 mm → resect 10/12/14 mm; LF 9–11 mm → 12/14/16 mm; LF 5–8 mm → 14/16/18 mm. Adult chapter rule: 4 mm resection per 1 mm ptosis, 6 mm for 2 mm, 9–10 mm for 3 mm; >10 mm avoided (p. 128).
  - Hertel exophthalmometry: Asians average 18 mm (p. 445); Centurion syndrome example 7 mm OU (p. 80); "negative vector (shallow orbit)" flagged as risk for lower-lid retraction after reconstruction (p. 151).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no numeric data; qualitative: thinnest skin, no subcutaneous fat, p. 181)
- **Injection plane & technique**
  - Fillers: "lighter, more temporary materials are generally preferred in regions with thinner skin, such as the eyelids"; lower lids and lips "should be treated conservatively" because HA sequesters water (p. 206). Tyndall effect if too superficial in thin skin (p. 207).
  - BTX for orbicularis hypertrophy ("sausage roll" pretarsal roll): 2.5–5 units, avoiding the medial lid (p. 201, Fig. 8). Blepharospasm: 2.5 U subcutaneous to upper and lower lid preseptal orbicularis, ≥5 mm from puncta (p. 139). Apraxia of lid opening: 1–2 U at 2 sites medially and laterally 5 mm from lid margin (p. 141). Upper lid retraction: 5–15 U into levator subconjunctivally 5–6 mm above tarsus (p. 141). Crocodile tears: 2.5–10 U into lacrimal gland (p. 141).
  - BTX dilution: 1–4 cc saline → 10.0, 5.0 or 2.5 U/0.1 mL (p. 141).
- **Volumes**: (no filler mL data)
- **Expected surface response & longevity**: BTX-induced ptosis (levator diffusion) reversed partially by apraclonidine; example ptosis "returned to normal 4 weeks later" (pp. 204, 146). BTX complications (ptosis, diplopia, lagophthalmos, ectropion) <10%, transient 2–4 weeks, avoided by superficial injection outside the orbital rim and avoiding central upper/lower lid (p. 139).
- **Danger zones**: retrobulbar hemorrhage after bleph "potentially blinding" (p. 178); post-bleph orbital hemorrhage with visual loss ≈1 in 40,000 (p. 191). Infratrochlear nerve block point 0.5–1 cm above the medial canthus at nasal base/orbit junction, 0.5–1 mL (p. 21).
- **Variation (age/sex/ethnicity)**: crease and MRD1 ethnic differences above; senile aponeurotic ptosis raises the crease (p. 123).

## Tear trough & infraorbital

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Tear trough "is located over the infero-medial orbital rim. It becomes depressed with age causing hollowness. This occurs mainly due to loss of subcutaneous fat and thinning of overlying skin" (Periorbital Dermatology, p. 417).
  - Lower-lid/cheek layer stack (Lower Blepharoplasty, pp. 181–182): skin (thinnest in body, no subcutaneous fat) → orbicularis oculi (pretarsal / preseptal / preorbital) → orbital septum (thinner than upper lid, "in an older patient may be very thin") → 3 fat pads (nasal, central, lateral; inferior oblique between nasal and central; fibrous strands of retractors separate central and lateral) → capsulopalpebral fascia (lower lid retractors) → conjunctiva. At the rim/cheek transition: **SOOF deep to the orbital orbicularis over the inferior orbital rim; inferior to the rim SMAS overlies SOOF; malar fat pad superficial to SMAS**. "Correct position of both the SOOF and the malar fat pad lead to a high, smooth cheek characteristic of the youthful midface."
  - Aging: "With descent of the SOOF and malar fat pads, hollows can form along the lower orbital rim and medially, contributing to a prominent tear trough… characteristic 'double convexity' seen in the aged face. The reason for tear trough deformity formation is considered to be the thinning of subcutaneous tissue and skin at the top of midface descent" (p. 184).
  - Injectables chapter: "Tear trough deformities results from a combination of orbital fat herniation in the lower lid and skin tethering to the orbitomalar ligament" (p. 208).
  - Lower lid: MRD2 and scleral show recorded in mm pre-op (p. 184).
- **Layer thickness data**: (no numeric data; qualitative above)
- **Injection plane & technique (filler)**
  - "Adding volume, combined with horizontal cutting (subcision) of the orbitomalar ligament skin adhesions with the injecting needle is performed. Injection is typically in a **pre-periosteal plane** and results in reversal of the double convexity deformity of the tear trough" (p. 208, Fig. 13).
  - Fine lower-lid lines: parallel subcision with a sharp 27 G needle along the lines, then thin HA through a finer needle (p. 208).
  - "It is always better to undertreat, as HA fillers tend to expand as they imbibe water overnight"; lower lids at risk of visible edema → conservative (p. 206).
  - HA also used for periorbital hyperpigmentation (vascular and structural types) — Table 2, p. 418: Pigmented (brown) → bleaching/peels/lasers/PRP; Vascular (blue/pink/purple, deepens on stretch) → filler, autologous fat, lasers, PRP; Structural (skin-coloured shadows from contour ± bags, ptosis, "loss of fat with bony prominence"; improves on stretch) → surgery, filler.
  - Surgical alternative: transconjunctival fat repositioning into a pre-periosteal (or subperiosteal) pocket over the rim; nasal pedicle placed "slightly inferior and medial to the tear trough" (pp. 190–191, Figs. 7–8).
  - Deoxycholic acid (off-label) for lower-lid fat pads in young patients: 2–4 mL of 10 mg/mL into the infraorbital fat pads, 2–3 sessions one month apart, mixed with triamcinolone + lidocaine (p. 209).
- **Volumes**: (no HA mL data). Local anaesthesia for lower bleph: 2 mL transconjunctival, 2 mL infraorbital block, 2 mL lateral canthal block per side, +1 mL subciliary (p. 185).
- **Product/rheology**: thin/low-viscosity HA for eyelids; see p. 206.
- **Expected surface response**: filler gives "immediate improvement… tear trough contour improvement and under eye dark circle improved" (p. 420). Post-op skin tightening after transconjunctival or transcutaneous bleph "up to 2 mm because of the healing process" (pp. 186, 189).
- **Lower-lid laxity metrics**: distraction test >6 mm from globe = laxity (p. 184); malpositions chapter uses >8 mm (p. 67). Snap-back graded instant / with blink / not repositioned (p. 184).
- **Danger zones**
  - Infraorbital foramen: "2 cm below the midline of orbit", on a vertical line through supraorbital notch and pupil in neutral gaze (p. 23). Pediatric distance from midline: **21 mm + 0.5 × age (years)**; foramen migrates inferotemporally in first 3 years and at 10–12 years, finalised ≈ age 20; more inferotemporal in males (p. 23). Infraorbital block 1–2 mL subcutaneous; deeper beneath muscle if prominent quadratus labii superioris (p. 23).
  - Zygomaticofacial nerve/foramen: 2 cm lateral and 2 cm inferior to the lateral canthus (p. 22); innervates skin over zygoma, inferior temple, lateral lower lid.
  - Inferior oblique between nasal and central fat pads (pp. 182, 189).
- **Variation**: (no data beyond sex difference in foramen position)

## Midface: malar & medial cheek

- **Anatomy**: SOOF / SMAS / malar fat pad stack as above (p. 182). Facial-nerve-palsy chapter: mid-facial descent treated with subperiosteal cheek lift + posterior lamellar graft (2:1 graft-to-retraction ratio, e.g., 2 mm retraction → 4 mm hard-palate graft) (pp. 171–172).
- **Craniofacial growth**: orbital growth essentially complete by age 7, then 1–2 %/year; "maxilla and malar bone reaches skeletal maturity in late adolescence, with the growth of the malar complex proceeding laterally and posteriorly" (p. 325).
- **Filler**: HA indications "midface lift… cheek augmentation" (p. 207); "for volume augmentation of the malar area or nasolabial fold, more viscous agents are preferred" (p. 206). Cheeks named as superficial-artery risk region (p. 205).
- **BTX**: gummy smile — levator labii 2.5 units each side of the nares (p. 203).
- Layer thickness / volumes / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch

- Zygomaticofacial foramen ≈ 2 cm lateral + 2 cm inferior to lateral canthus (p. 22). Zygomaticotemporal nerve territory 3 cm lateral to lateral canthus, 3 cm diameter (p. 22).
- Crow's feet BTX: ≥3 injections on a vertical line outside the lateral orbital margin, 5–10 units; needle aimed toward the ear to avoid lateral rectus (pp. 199, 201, Fig. 7). Blepharospasm: ~5 U at each of 3 lateral points, 1 cm lateral to the lateral orbital rim (p. 139).
- Crow's-feet BTX longevity ≈3 months (p. 204).
- Else: (no data)

## Preauricular

- Facial nerve blocks: O'Brien at mandibular neck anterior to tragus, max depth 1.7 cm, ~5 mL; Atkinson 2–5 mL subcutaneous at midpoint of line between lower zygoma edge and TMJ (p. 25). Maxillary nerve block: lateral pterygoid plate at ~4 cm depth (p. 25). No filler data.

## Chin

## General & cross-region

- **Aging model (qualitative)**: intrinsic factors = reduced tissue elasticity, collagen loss, soft-tissue atrophy, persistent muscle use, gravity; extrinsic = photodamage, smoking; "typical reversal of V pattern of facial contour" young vs aged (Fig. 1, p. 198). "With increasing age, the body's natural potential to produce hyaluronic acid as well as its inherent hygroscopy decreases" (p. 205). Fillers "support and lift the fat pads and ligaments" (p. 205).
- **Filler classification (Table 1, p. 206, adapted from Smith 2008)**
  - Source: autologous (fat); biological (collagen, HA); synthetic (hydroxyapatite, silicone oil, PMMA microspheres, polyacrylamide hydrogel, HEMA/EMA, PLLA).
  - Duration of cosmetic benefit: temporary short = saline; short = bovine collagen; reversible medium-to-long = HA; non-reversible long = hydroxyapatite, polyacrylamide hydrogel, porcine collagen; non-reversible very long = silicone oil, PMMA, HEMA/EMA, PLLA, fat; non-reversible variable = fat.
  - Duration classes: short <3 months, medium 3–12 months, long 12–24 months, very long >24 months (p. 205).
  - Risk profile: low = saline, HA; medium = collagen, hydroxylapatite, PMMA, PLLA, fat; high = hydroxylapatite, PMMA, PLLA, fat, silicone, polyacrylamide, HEMA/EMA.
  - Skill required: low = saline; medium = HA, collagen; high = the rest.
- **HA product variables**: source, concentration, particle size, cross-linking (type/agent), monophasic vs biphasic, added anaesthetic; "Based on the amount of crosslinking, size and the concentration of HA molecule, HA fillers can be injected at different layers"; less viscous for superficial fine wrinkles, more viscous for malar/NLF volume; lighter products for thin-skin regions (eyelids) (p. 206). First FDA HA = Restylane 2003; bovine collagen first FDA filler 1980s (p. 205). Reversible with diluted hyaluronidase; nodules → massage, then hyaluronidase (pp. 206–207).
- **Injection techniques**: topical 1 % lignocaine cream 30 min prior; serial puncture (small boluses close together, acne scars), linear threading (inject on withdrawal; linear rhytides, NLF), cross-hatching (two perpendicular linear series), fanning (single entry, multiple radiating threads); microcannula recommended (p. 208, Fig. 14).
- **Vascular safety**: "The main end artery for the face is the facial artery"; "Higher risks of complications are entailed in regions of superficial location of the artery" — cheeks, glabella, nasal ala, temples (p. 205, Fig. 12 facial blood vessels — no depths given). Blunt microcannulas "virtually eliminated the risk of intravascular injection" (p. 207).
- **Adverse effects**: pain, bruising, edema, nodules, intravascular injection, granuloma, scarring; Tyndall bluish hue if superficial in thin skin (p. 207). HA imbibes water overnight → undertreat (p. 206).
- **BTX general**: FDA-approved for blepharospasm, hemifacial spasm, strabismus, glabellar lines, periocular rhytides; inject the muscle causing the wrinkle, not the wrinkle; 32 G needles; topical anaesthetic/cooling may reduce duration of effect; onset 2–3 d, peak 7–10 d; post-care no heat 12 h, no exercise/massage 24 h; move injected muscles immediately after; areas: crow's feet/lips ~3 months, forehead/glabella up to 6 months (pp. 198–204).
- **PRP**: used for dark circles, tear trough, wrinkles; also around lacrimal gland for dry eye (p. 208, 420).
- **Wound healing timeline**: remodeling starts after 3 weeks, continues 6–12 months (p. 2). Skin graft adheres by fibrin, breaks down within 48 h (p. 7).
- **Imaging accuracy / 3D**: (no data — book discusses CT/MRI of the orbit only; "Orbital volume changes during growth… assessed" cited as a reference, p. 332.) Orbital cavernous venous malformation grows ≈0.2 cm³/year → ≈2 mm proptosis per year (p. 519) — a rough orbital volume-to-projection ratio (0.2 mL ≈ 2 mm globe displacement) that may be a useful sanity check but is not soft-tissue data.
- **Nerve-block volume notes**: supraorbital 1–2 mL; infratrochlear 0.5–1 mL; all three (supraorbital/supratrochlear/infratrochlear) with 2–3 mL from mid-eyebrow to glabella (pp. 19–21).

## Assessment

Quantitativeness: LOW for filler planning. The only injectables chapter (pp. 197–210, 14 pages, half of it botulinum toxin) gives unit doses per muscle and BTX durations but zero filler volumes, no layer thicknesses, no rheology numbers, and no outcome/volumetric studies. The lower-blepharoplasty chapter gives the best anatomical layer description (skin → orbicularis → septum → 3 fat pads → capsulopalpebral fascia; SOOF/SMAS/malar fat stack at the rim) and the mechanism of tear-trough/double-convexity aging, all qualitative. The genuinely useful numbers are: eyelid morphometrics (crease height by sex/ethnicity, MRD1 norms, levator function grades, lid-laxity thresholds, 18–20 mm brow-to-lash skin), orbicularis extent (1.4 cm above / 1.2 cm below rim / 2.5 cm lateral), nerve/foramen surface coordinates (supratrochlear 1 cm, supraorbital 2 cm from midline; infraorbital foramen 2 cm below orbit midline; zygomaticofacial 2 cm lateral + 2 cm inferior to lateral canthus; pediatric IOF formula 21 mm + 0.5×age), and Hertel norms. Regions covered best: upper eyelid/brow and lower eyelid/tear trough (anatomy + surgical planes); forehead/glabella/crow's feet only via BTX dosing. All other regions are essentially silent. Use this book for periorbital landmark definitions and eyelid measurement conventions, not for per-region filler rule sets.

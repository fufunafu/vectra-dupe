# USProto — Malherbe K, Roberts S. *Ultrasound Protocol for Facial Aesthetics: A practical clinical guide* (Springer, 2024, ISBN 978-3-031-75949-9)

Citation convention: `USProto p.N` = printed page number (text-file page = printed + 15; e.g. printed p.1 is form-feed page 16). Chapter/section numbers given where useful.
Book structure (TOC, p.v–vii): 1 Introduction (p.1) · 2 Sonographic Principles & Techniques (p.5) · 3 Anatomical Principles of the Face (p.25) · 4 Clinical Indications (p.37) · 5 Complications (p.41) · 6 Ultrasound Protocol (p.53: 6.1 Lower face, 6.2 Midface, 6.3 Upper face) · Appendix flowcharts (p.79) · Glossary of Imaging (p.85–112; image-only in the extracted text, no recoverable numbers).

**Headline caveat:** this book is a *probe-positioning protocol* (where to put the transducer, what muscle/vessel appears next), NOT a thickness atlas. It contains almost no mm layer-thickness values, no volumes per region, no rheology, no longevity data. Its quantitative content is: one temporoparietal-fascia thickness (2–3 mm), one Doppler velocity/RI table for 9 facial arteries (n=40), the 10-layer temporal stack with the layer index each vessel occupies, a handful of cm offsets for landmarks (facial artery 1.5–2 cm lateral to the oral commissure; DAO 2 cm anterior to masseter; probe 1 cm above mandibular border), filler echogenicity by product class with two dated examples (CaHA at 4/12 and 3/12), and three complication case studies with hyaluronidase doses. Everything below is exhaustive for what the book actually provides.

## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries:** Frontalis covers forehead/skull; conjoint with occipitofrontalis; two bellies (frontalis, occipitalis) encased in galea aponeurotica. Origin posteriorly from galea (corresponds with hairline on surface); inserts inferomedially interdigitating with procerus, inferolaterally attaching to orbicularis oculi and corrugator. **No bony attachments.** SMAS continues above zygomatic arch and includes temporoparietal fascia (blending into galea) and frontalis "as part of the SMAS". Corrugator is deep to frontalis and orbicularis with bony origin from medial orbital rim. (USProto §3.3, p.32)
- **Forehead line morphology (4 types, prevalence):** (1) full straight lines across whole forehead **45%**; (2) gull-wing lines, central depression + lateral elevation **30%**; (3) short central horizontal lines, few/none laterally **10%**; (4) lateral straight lines as two columns, no central lines **15%**. (USProto p.32)
- **Layer thickness data:** (no data in this book) — only the sonographic stack "frontalis visible just below the SMAS and above the frontal bone" (§6.3.1, p.73).
- **Injection plane & technique:** Book lists forehead among "regions of high vascular compromise risk, such as the temple, forehead, and nose" for which US should be used to determine injection depth (§4.1, p.37). Otherwise (no data).
- **Volumes / Product-rheology / Surface response & longevity:** (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones / vessels:** Supratrochlear + supraorbital arteries (ophthalmic → internal carotid) supply the muscle medially from the inferior margin after exiting the orbit; supraorbital exits supraorbital notch/foramen, supratrochlear exits more medially; frontal branch of superficial temporal artery supplies laterally; all anastomose into a "highly vascularized network". Superficial arteries supply muscle/galea/skin; "the deeper layer supplies the periosteum". (p.32–33)
- **Nerve:** Temporal branch of CN VII crosses zygomatic arch, passes through areolar tissue on the surface of the temporal fascia, enters frontalis on its deep surface; anterior/middle/posterior temporal branches innervate orbicularis oculi, frontalis, corrugator. (p.32)
- **US protocol:** transducer transverse, "one finger width cranial to the eyebrow"; glide superior/lateral from glabella toward the metopian line. (§6.3.1, p.73–74)
- **Variation:** (no data in this book)

## Glabella

- **Anatomy:** Procerus = pyramidal muscle arising from fascia of superior nasal region near junction of nasal bones and superolateral nasal cartilage; fibers run superiorly and merge with frontalis; inserts skin of glabella. Blood supply: angular and lateral nasal branches of facial artery. (§3.3, p.33)
- **Corrugator supercilii:** origin medial part of superciliary arch; insertion skin above middle part of eyebrow; blood supply branches of superficial temporal and ophthalmic arteries. Sonographically "noted deep to the depressor supercilii muscle with its lateral border deep to the orbicularis oculi"; **supraorbital foramen lies deep to the corrugator along the cortical ridge**; a branch of the supraorbital artery runs near the corrugator along its medial border. Probe at 45° along the edge of the medial eyebrow. (§6.3.3, p.73)
- **Procerus on US:** transverse on the nasion just proximal to nasalis; procerus "over the frontal bone and deep to the SMAS". (§6.3.2, p.73)
- **Supratrochlear notch on US:** oblique longitudinal plane in the deep superior-medial ridge of orbital rim; angle toward glabella until a cortical break is seen; power Doppler shows supratrochlear artery traversing the foramen; supratrochlear nerve ("salt and pepper") just lateral to the artery. (§6.3.5, p.77)
- **Layer thickness data:** (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones:** Explicit: "avoiding high-risk areas such as the glabella and nose" (§5.1.4, p.45). Anastomoses between dorsal nasal + supratrochlear branches of ophthalmic (ICA side) and angular branch of facial + medial branches of infraorbital (ECA side) — pathway for retrograde embolisation → retinal artery occlusion/blindness (p.35, p.41). In ~**70%** of ICA occlusion cases there is a significant angular artery pulse (collateral); this finding is "typical in 1 out of 10 persons over 55 who have had chronic hypertension" (p.35).
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response / Variation:** (no data in this book)

## Brow

- Frontalis is "the sole elevator muscle of the upper face" — neurotoxin caution to avoid brow heaviness/drop (§6.3.1, p.73). Procerus "depresses medial end of eyebrow"; corrugator "pulls skin of eyebrow downward and medially" (p.33).
- Supraorbital foramen on US: transverse, midpoint aligned to midpoint of eyebrow/superior orbital rim (mid-pupillary line); glide below the eyebrow ridge and tilt up until cortical break; power Doppler shows supraorbital artery in the foramen (§6.3.5, p.73, Fig 6.50 p.76).
- Appendix upper-face flowchart (Fig A.2, p.80) nodes: Glabella → Corrugator supercilii (deep to CSM / lateral to DSM) → Depressor supercilii → Supraorbital foramen → "Deep to orbicularis oculi muscle" → **Retro-Orbicularis Oculi Fat (ROOF)** "superior and overlying" → orbicularis oculi → temporal fusion line → 10 layers of temporal fossa.
- Thickness / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Temple

- **Anatomy / layer stack (two versions given):**
  - Ch.3 table (§3.2, p.31): skin → subcutaneous tissue → temporoparietal fascia (superficial temporal fascia) → innominate fascia → deep temporal fascia (divides into deep and superficial layer) → temporalis muscle → pericranium → cranium. **Temporoparietal fascia ≈ 2–3 mm thick** (only mm value in the book). TPF is continuous with SMAS below the arch and platysma in the neck ("unified fascia layer from the scalp to the clavicle"); joins orbicularis oculi and frontalis anteriorly, occipitalis posteriorly. Deep temporal fascia splits into deep and superficial layers before inserting on the superior aspect of the zygomatic arch; **superficial temporal fat pad lies between these two layers**.
  - Ch.6 "ten layers" on ultrasound (§6.2.6, p.68, Fig 6.36 p.70), superficial → deep:
    1. Skin
    2. Subcutaneous fat
    3. Superficial temporal fascia
    4. Loose alveolar [areolar] tissue
    5. Superficial lamina of the deep temporal fascia
    6. Superficial temporal fat
    7. Deep lamina of the deep temporal fascia
    8. Temporal extension of the Bichat fat (deep temporal fat pad / buccal fat extension)
    9. Temporalis muscle
    10. Temporal bone
- **Vessel-to-layer map (§6.2.7, p.70):**
  | Vessel | Layer position |
  |---|---|
  | Superficial temporal artery | between layers 2–4 (protocol text: "identified in layers 2–3") |
  | Middle temporal vein | between layers 5–7 (figure text: "between layers 5–6") |
  | Anterior + posterior branches of deep temporal artery | between layers 9–10 (i.e., on bone beneath/within temporalis) |
  | Temporal branch of facial nerve | "near the superficial temporal artery", cross-section = oval ring with salt-and-pepper centre (Fig 6.41 p.72; caption calls it auriculotemporal nerve posterior to the artery) |
- **Temporalis:** origin temporal plane below superior temporal line + deep surface of temporal fascia; inserts coronoid process; blood supply deep temporal branches of maxillary artery + middle temporal branches of superficial temporal artery (p.31).
- **US protocol:** "K-point" = heel of probe fixed on lateral edge of brow / lateral orbital rim, swivel from 11 o'clock to 6 o'clock (Fig 6.37 p.71). Temporal vessels are **easily compressed** — light touch + gel standoff pad required; colour box steered 60°, increased colour gain; power Doppler if STA not found. For deep temporal artery: transducer transverse on zygomatic arch, increase depth to see bone, slide inferior→superior. (p.70)
- **Layer thickness data (mm):** only TPF 2–3 mm (p.31). No total soft-tissue depth given.
- **Injection plane & technique:** Temple listed as a "key target point of injection" alongside piriform recess and masseter region (p.25) and as a high-vascular-risk region where US should set injection depth (p.37); post-treatment US "to assess the correct depth and placement of fillers in key target areas such as the temple, tear trough and piriform recess" (§4.2, p.37). No plane recommendation stated.
- **Volumes / Product / Surface response & longevity:** (no data in this book)
- **Danger zones:** Case study 2 (Fig 5.2, p.43): 43-year-old female, vascular occlusion of superficial temporal artery within 24 h of filler; ecchymosis followed STA course; treated with **hyaluronidase 10 500 IU divided into three sessions**; persistent tingling/burning afterwards. Doppler reference for STA: PSV 42.09 cm/s, RI 0.79 (see General table).
- **Variation:** (no data in this book)

## Upper eyelid & periorbital

- Orbicularis oculi: three parts (orbital, palpebral, lacrimal — lacrimal "lies most deeply"); origin nasal part of frontal bone, frontal process of maxilla, medial palpebral ligament; inserts lateral palpebral raphe and tarsal plates; supplied by branches of superficial temporal, maxillary, facial and ophthalmic arteries (§3.2, p.30).
- On US: probe along superior or inferior orbital rim transverse; "thin hypoechoic band noted just below the thin layer of subcutaneous soft tissue is the orbicularis oculi muscle" (§6.3.4, p.73). ROOF: appendix flowchart places Retro-Orbicularis Oculi Fat "superior and overlying" relative to the orbicularis oculi node (Fig A.2 p.80).
- Orbicularis retaining ligament forms superior and inferior boundaries of the orbital compartments of upper and lower lid and of the prezygomatic space (p.31).
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Tear trough & infraorbital

- **Anatomy (retaining ligaments, §3.2, p.31):** Orbicularis retaining ligaments bound the prezygomatic space; overlap with superficial fat compartment boundaries (nasolabial + medial compartments together = malar fat pad). As orbital septum attenuates with age, periorbital fat bulges anteriorly against the septum and inferiorly against the ORL; combined with upper-cheek deflation this accentuates the ORL at the lid-cheek junction. **Nasojugal groove** and its lateral extension the **palpebromalar groove** are notable aging signs. Medial limb of zygomatic cutaneous ligaments forms the **mid-cheek groove**; bulging/ptosis of this space forms **malar bags and festoons**.
- **SOOF:** "The SubOrbicularis oculi fat (SOOF) is noted deep to the orbicularis oculi muscle in the infra orbital area" (§6.3.4, p.73). LLSAN "lying just above the infraorbital fat pad" seen when probe tilted medially to show nasal cartilage in profile (§6.2.4, p.64).
- **Infraorbital foramen on US (§6.2.8, p.72):** transducer transverse along inferior orbital rim, midpoint at mid-pupil; power/colour Doppler; "a small cortical break in the maxilla" with an arterial vessel traversing longitudinally into it. Muscles above this level: levator anguli oris and levator labii superioris. Doppler reference infraorbital artery: PSV 15.55 cm/s, RI 0.75 (lowest PSV of the 9 measured).
- **Angular vein/artery in tear trough:** heel-toe sweep from medial to lateral "in the tear trough to determine the presence of the angular artery and vein" (§2.1, p.6). Angular vein "lies in the nasojugal fold"; angular artery is medial to the angular vein (§6.2.3, p.64). Light pressure essential — vein compresses. Confirm vein with spectral venous waveform.
- **Injection plane:** Tear trough named as key target for post-treatment depth/placement check (§4.2, p.37); heel-toe method recommended for the curved tear trough (p.6). No plane/depth specified.
- **Thickness data / volumes / product / surface response / variation:** (no data in this book)

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (deep/superficial fat compartments)

- **Fat compartments & ligaments (§3.2 "Retaining ligaments of the midface", p.31):**
  - Superficial: nasolabial + medial cheek compartments → **malar fat pad**; zygomatic ligaments overlap the superficial superior cheek septum separating **lateral orbital** compartment from **middle** compartment; masseteric cutaneous ligaments overlap the septum separating **middle** and **medial** compartments; platysma-auricular ligament = plane between **lateral temporal cheek** compartment and **postauricular** compartment.
  - Aging model quoted: "soft tissue descent and volumetric deflation"; two theories — ligament laxity → descent, vs. ligaments stay strong while unsupported tissue descends → bulges/grooves ("stigmata").
  - No deep-compartment (deep medial cheek fat / SOOF volumes) data beyond SOOF being deep to orbicularis oculi.
- **Muscles (§3.2, p.29–30):** Zygomaticus major and minor: origin lateral surface of zygomatic bone; major → modiolus, minor → skin and lateral muscles of upper lip ("deepens and elevates nasolabial sulcus"); supplied by superior labial artery. LLSAN: origin frontal process of maxilla; inserts skin of ala and upper lip; supplied by facial artery + infraorbital artery.
- **US protocol (§6.2.1–6.2.4, p.64):** zygomaticus major = first thin hypoechoic band at the superior end of masseter, best seen in oblique plane; probe transverse on zygomatic arch with midpoint at lateral canthus. Zygomaticus minor: foot of probe on lateral orbital rim, heel toward nasolabial fold; "the thicker muscle belly situated beneath the orbicularis oculi muscle corresponds to the zygomaticus minor"; the muscle overlying the zygomatic bone is zygomaticus minor. Position 2 from medial canthus turning heel lateral/transverse: lateral→medial the bellies are levator anguli oris, levator labii superioris, LLSAN (most medial).
- **Angular artery (§6.2.3, p.64):** foot of probe on medial canthus aligned along the nasolabial fold just lateral to nasalis; colour box angled 60°; angular artery lies in close relation to LLSAN; artery medial to the vein; lateral nasal branches appear when colour gain increased. Doppler: angular PSV 20.73 cm/s, RI 0.65 (lowest RI of the 9).
- **Complication case 1 (Fig 5.1, p.42):** 50-year-old, delayed-onset nodule 13 days post "hyaluronidase (low density) filler treatment" [sic]; two nodules — first in the **prezygomatic space**, second **2 cm posterior**; US: oval mixed-echogenic fluid collection with echogenic rim/capsule, surrounding cellulitis-like "cobblestone" tissue; serial imaging over **6 weeks** suggested to confirm resolution; US-guided injection of hyperechoic fluid bolus into the capsule.
- **Layer thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation:** (no data in this book)

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch

- Zygomatic arch is a named surface landmark (Fig 3.2, p.26) and protocol waypoint (point 8) reached by gliding superior from the masseter (Fig 6.27, p.65). Transverse facial artery runs anteriorly between zygoma and parotid duct, supplying parotid duct, gland, masseter (p.27). Doppler: transverse facial PSV 21.38 cm/s, RI 0.74.
- Superficial temporal fat pad sits between the two laminae of the deep temporal fascia as they insert on the superior aspect of the arch (p.31).
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Preauricular

- Parotid gland (§3.1, p.27): retromandibular fossa; bordered superiorly by zygomatic arch, anteriorly by masseter, posteriorly by SCM; superficial lobe extends anteriorly over mandibular ramus and posterior masseter; superficial/deep lobes divided by facial nerve and posterior facial (retromandibular) vein; surrounded by parotid sheath; Stensen's duct runs over masseter to its anterior border then pierces buccinator, opening opposite maxillary 2nd molar. Palpable anterior and inferior to lower half of ear, extends to lower border of mandible.
- On US (§6.1.2, p.55): "diffuse hyperechoic appearance"; first hyperechoic structure just posterior to masseter; assess lymphadenopathy with colour Doppler before filler; masseter and parotid "may be injected with neurotoxin for facial reshaping".
- **Complication case 3 (Fig 5.3, p.47):** sterile abscess 3 months post HA filler, preauricular region inferior to zygomatic bone; hardened nodule; **0.7 ml aspirated** (polymorphs, culture negative); treated with **3 ml saline + 2 ml Xylocaine 2% + 3000 IU hyaluronidase**.
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Nose

- Nasalis (§3.2, p.30): transverse part (compressor naris) from maxilla superolateral to incisive fossa → aponeurosis at nasal bridge; alar part (dilator naris posterior) from frontal process of maxilla above lateral incisor → skin of ala above lateral crus. Supplied by facial artery + infraorbital branch of maxillary. Procerus: from fascia over nasal bones / superolateral nasal cartilage (p.33).
- US: oblique coronal plane along the nasal cartilage, glide medially from LLSAN (§6.2.5, p.68). Columellar arteries branch superiorly from the superior labial artery within the philtral columns (§6.1.8, p.61).
- **Danger zone:** nose explicitly a high-risk area (avoid; p.45); "cases of nasal tip necrosis after rhinoplasty or dermal filler injections have been documented" (p.41); depth determination by US advised for nose (p.37). Lateral nasal branch of facial artery is one of four principal facial branches (p.34).
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Nasolabial fold

- **Piriform recess** is a named "key target point of injection" (p.25) and post-treatment depth-check area (p.37). Fig 2.17 example 2 (p.22): recently placed crosslinked HA "just lateral to the nasolabial fold within the piriform recess" appears "similar to a simple cyst, with clear hypoechoic contents and well circumscribed oval border" (longitudinal probe, split screen bilateral).
- Facial artery course flowchart (Fig A.4, p.82): anterior border of masseter → **2 cm lateral to oral commissure** ("naked facial artery") → **lateral nasolabial fold** → piriform recess → start of angular artery → imaginary line piriform recess→medial canthus; then move medial to alar base in two steps (angular artery) or lateral to mid-pupillary line in two steps (angular vein).
- Heel-toe method recommended for the nasolabial fold (p.6); position 1 heel on medial canthus, toe along NLF; position 2 toe rotated lateral toward maxilla (Fig 2.5, p.7).
- Facial artery "might go over the levator labii superioris in the usual version or through it" (p.33). Between risorius and buccinator the facial artery gives off the superior labial artery (p.28).
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book). (Ref cited: Arlette & Trotter 2008, histologic location of HA in NLF — not summarised in text.)

## Lips & perioral

- **Orbicularis oris** (§3.1, p.29): multilayered, no bony/tendinous origin, attaches via thin SMAS to lip dermis; blood supply superior + inferior labial (facial), mental + infraorbital (maxillary), transverse facial (STA).
- **Labial arteries on US (§6.1.8, p.61):** probe transverse with midpoint at the oral commissure; **facial artery located 1.5–2 cm lateral to the oral commissure** ("naked facial artery"). Trace from lateral edge of lips onto outer border of superior vermilion. Colour Doppler: "a tortuous, large pulsating vessel is noted within the **subcutaneous layer both within and below the orbicularis oris muscle belly**". Superior labial artery seen in length with probe along the vermilion border; **"the depth and entire length may differ amongst patients"**; small columellar branch extends from the superior labial artery. Inferior labial artery lies **deep and lateral to DAO**, then traverses to lower lip; inferior labial artery = demarcation between DAO (superficial) and DLI (deep) (§6.1.5, p.59).
- **Doppler reference (Fig 3.5, p.35):** superior labial PSV 27.38 cm/s RI 0.72; inferior labial PSV 27.99 cm/s RI 0.77.
- Risorius (p.28): superficial fascia of cheek, overlying masseter and buccopharyngeal fascia; converges to modiolus; facial artery passes deep to risorius and superficial to buccinator; on US a thin hypoechoic band between anterior border of masseter and posterior DAO fibers, more superficial than buccinator (§6.1.4, p.57). Toxin into risorius → shortened smile.
- Thickness (mm) / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Marionette & oral commissure

- **DAO** (§3.1, p.28): origin oblique line + mental tubercle of mandible; inserts modiolus; some fibers cross midline forming transversus menti ("mental sling"); supplied by inferior labial (facial) + mental (maxillary) arteries.
- **DLI:** quadrilateral, from oblique line between symphysis and mental foramen, superomedial to lower-lip integument; blends with orbicularis oris, contralateral DLI, platysma.
- **US (§6.1.5–6.1.6, p.59):** probe transverse starting on anterior border of masseter ~**1 cm superior to the mandible**, glide anterior; DAO = first belly close to masseter (point 4 = **2 cm anterior to the masseter**, Fig 6.14 p.60); DAO is "the thicker diamond shaped muscle (also known as triangularis)", DLI is "the thinner hypoechoic muscle belly medial and deep to the DAO"; DLI "typically smaller compared to the DAO". Oblique view: heel on modiolus lateral to commissure, toe longitudinal, sweep laterally to trace DAO insertion→origin. Overactive DLI → droopy lower lip / asymmetric smile.
- Modiolus = dense mobile fibromuscular mass formed by risorius, zygomaticus major, DAO, orbicularis oris (p.28).
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Chin

- **Mentalis** (§3.1, p.29): paired central muscle; origin mental protuberance near midline; inserts skin of chin; "pouting muscle". US (§6.1.7, p.61): probe transverse, midpoint at midpoint of lower chin, ~**1 cm superior to the mandibular body**; "two thin hypoechoic bands seen on either side of the midpoint represent the two bellies"; variants Type A-1, A-2, B (Fig 6.21, p.62), most common = bilateral bands either side of midline. Curved pogonion → tilt probe perpendicular to avoid anisotropy; heel-toe recommended for "the curved mentum" (p.6).
- Mental artery (maxillary) supplies DAO/DLI; Doppler reference mental artery PSV 23.05 cm/s RI 0.78; submental PSV 21.07 RI 0.79 (p.35).
- Filler imaging example (Fig 2.17 ex. 4, p.22): CaHA in the **mentum**, aged **3/12 (3 months)**: "degrading and integration of old filler is noted with small microcalcifications noted within the fluid bolus", with posterior acoustic shadow.
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Jawline & prejowl

- Ligaments (§3.2, p.31): **mandibular ligament** forms anterior border of the inferior jowl fat compartment; **mandibular septum** separates superior and inferior jowl fat compartments. Jowls form by descent of the **premasseteric space** over the lower masseter; superior and inferior jowl compartments descend and the mandibular ligament limits anterior extension. Mandibular ligament has a subcutaneous extension causing skin indentation with age (missed → dissatisfaction after facelift).
- Facial artery crosses mandible at the masseter's anterior-inferior border where its pulse is palpable against bone (p.33, p.35); marginal mandibular nerve usually superficial to it (artery runs deep to the nerve, p.34). Bleeding control: compress at the mandibular angle (p.34).
- Thickness / plane / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book)

## Masseter & mandibular angle

- **Anatomy (§3.1, p.26):** paired rectangular muscle, superficial + deep parts. Superficial origin: maxillary process of zygomatic bone + inferior border of zygomatic arch (**anterior 2/3**); deep origin: deep/inferior surface of arch (**posterior 1/3**). Insertion lateral ramus and angle. Masseteric artery.
- **US protocol (§6.1.1, p.53):** probe **1 cm superior to the mandibular body**, transverse, at the **midpoint between mentum and mandibular angle**; confirm by clenching; place superficial head at image centre. **Measure masseter in the anterior-posterior plane at rest and during contraction** as pre-neurotoxin reference; repeat same plane/orientation post-treatment (no reference values given). Split-screen rest vs contracted; "reduced width of muscle during relaxed phase in comparison to contraction" (Fig 6.3, p.54). Dynamic imaging shows superficial, intermediate and deep bellies.
- **Deep internal tendon (DIT) variants (Fig 6.4, p.55):** Type A — DIT in anterior third between superficial and deep groups (figure caption: within mid-region of belly); Type B — DIT in posterior third (caption: mid to anterior border); Type C — DIT covers predominantly the most inferior part / extends across entire anterior and posterior borders with multiple myotendinous divisions. Relevant to toxin dosing/placement (ref Lee et al. 2021).
- **Filler imaging examples (Fig 2.17, p.22):** ex.1 CaHA in the **subcutaneous soft tissue of the masseter region, 4/12 (4 months) old** — posterior acoustic shadowing from calcium; ex.3 crosslinked HA "placed within the superficial muscle belly" of masseter — degrading/integrating, contents now "complex with mixed echogenic internal foci" (vs simple-cyst look when fresh).
- Facial artery/vein anterior to masseter (§6.1.3, p.55): colour box along anterior border; "the vessel visualised closest to the masseter muscle belly is the facial vein, thereafter the facial artery"; **facial vein lies posterior to the facial artery** at this point; veins larger and compressible, arteries tortuous and pulsatile. Doppler: facial artery PSV 45.31 cm/s RI 0.81 (highest PSV of the 9).
- Asymmetric masseter: dynamic US of the bellies to choose toxin targets (§4.1, p.37); follow-up dynamic contraction to judge effect (§4.2, p.38).
- Thickness (mm) / volumes / product / longevity / variation: (no data in this book) — book says measure but gives no normative numbers.

## Neck

- Platysma continuity: TPF–SMAS–platysma form "a unified fascia layer from the scalp to the clavicle" (p.31). Facial artery origin in carotid triangle (omohyoid superior belly, SCM, posterior digastric); runs deep to posterior digastric/stylohyoid, along submandibular gland; cervical branches: tonsillar, glandular, submental, ascending palatine (p.33–34). Hypoglossal nerve deep to proximal facial artery. Submental artery Doppler: PSV 21.07 cm/s, RI 0.79.
- ECA branch mnemonic "SALFOPMS" (Fig A.5, p.83): superior thyroid, ascending pharyngeal, lingual, facial, occipital, posterior auricular, maxillary (largest terminal), superficial temporal (terminal). CCA divides at carotid bulb; ECA smaller diameter and more anterior than ICA.
- Everything else: (no data in this book)

## General & cross-region

### Face partitioning used by the book
- Three horizontal thirds: upper / mid / lower (Fig 3.1, p.25). Surface landmarks (Fig 3.2, p.26): metopian line, midsagittal plane, glabella, mentum, temporal rim, orbital rim, mandibular angle, zygomatic arches.
- Protocol waypoints ("point system"): **Lower face** (Fig 6.1 p.54, Fig A.1 p.79): 1 masseter → 2 parotid (posterior, toward EAM) → 3 facial artery/vein (anterior border masseter) → 4 DAO (2 cm anterior) → 5 DLI/mentalis (mid-chin) → 6 risorius (superior) → orbicularis oris/labial arteries along vermilion; buccinator deep. **Midface** (Fig 6.26 p.63, Fig A.3 p.81): masseter → 8 zygomatic arch → 9 zygomaticus major → 10 zygomaticus minor (toe to lateral canthus) → 11 orbicularis oculi on inferior orbital rim → infraorbital foramen (mid-pupillary) → SOOF deep to orbicularis oculi → piriform recess → angular artery/vein/LLSAN (medial canthus–NLF line, points A→C). **Upper face** (Fig 6.44 p.73, Fig A.2 p.80): glabella G → frontalis (superior-lateral toward metopian line) → procerus → corrugator (deep to CSM, lateral to depressor supercilii) → supraorbital foramen → ROOF → orbicularis oculi → temporal fusion line → 10-layer temporal fossa.
- Facial artery point system (Fig 3.3 p.34; Fig A.4 p.82): anterior border of masseter → facial artery & vein → naked facial artery (1.5–2 cm / "2 cm" lateral to oral commissure) → superior labial artery → inferior labial artery → submental branches; superiorly → lateral NLF → piriform recess → angular artery → medial canthus. Four principal facial branches: inferior labial, superior labial, lateral nasal, angular (terminal) (p.34).

### Filler appearance on ultrasound (§2.5.2, p.19–21; Fig 2.17, p.22)
| Product class | Fresh appearance | Over time | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyaluronic acid (crosslinked) | hypoechoic/anechoic, "similar to a simple cyst", well-circumscribed oval border, homogeneous | degrades/integrates: contents become "complex with mixed echogenic internal foci"; **echogenicity rises with edema/inflammation or vascular compromise** | Juvéderm Ultra/Voluma, Restylane-L/Lyft, Belotero Balance |
| Calcium hydroxyapatite | moderate–high echogenicity, hyperechoic bright echoes, **posterior acoustic shadowing** | at 3/12 and 4/12: still shadowing; degrading with "small microcalcifications within the fluid bolus" | Radiesse, "Harmonica" |
| PLLA | initially hyperechoic (microspheres) | echogenicity decreases as collagen forms → more homogeneous/hypoechoic | Sculptra |
| PMMA | hyperechoic, may have posterior shadowing | persistent | Bellafill |
| Collagen | hypoechoic, varies with concentration/additives | — | Cosmoderm, Zyderm |
| Autologous fat | hypoechoic, resembles native fat | echogenicity may increase (fibrosis, cystic degeneration) | — |
- "Filler aging relates to the echogenicity of the filler bolus site from anechoic to a more heterogeneous appearance" (§4.1, p.37). B-mode + shear-wave elastography can discern filler types (p.37) but "differentiation of various types of filler products using ultrasound is limiting; MRI advised" (§4.3, p.38).
- No spread/plane/persistence measurements (mm or months) are given for any product beyond the 3/12 and 4/12 CaHA examples.

### Imaging technique constants (for any US-validation arm of the project)
- Linear transducer **≥15 MHz** for facial aesthetics (p.5); elsewhere "high-frequency transducers (e.g., 10–15 MHz) are preferred" (p.8). Depth-vs-resolution trade-off per region (p.8).
- Transducer must be perpendicular; anisotropy is "the most common pitfall" (p.6). Face = series of curved "O-gee" surfaces → rock/tilt probe; gel standoff pad for curved surfaces (p.6–7). Heel-toe method for curved mentum, tear trough, NLF (p.6).
- Doppler: optimal angle **60° or less** (p.12); PW sample box = **2/3 of target vessel lumen diameter** (p.12); raise colour gain until blooming then back off (p.11); PRF low for veins/high for arteries, aliasing example at PRF 1.4 kHz on CCA (p.12); power Doppler angle-independent, best for small/low-flow facial vessels (p.11). Colour box steered 60° in temple (p.70).
- Modes: B-mode, colour, power, PW spectral; presets with topographic maps + probe-orientation indicators recommended for reproducibility across sessions (p.9).
- Artifacts to know: acoustic shadowing (CaHA/PMMA), reverberation, mirror, edge shadowing, side/lobe, clutter, comet tail, twinkling, colour blooming (p.17–19).

### Complications & doses (Ch.5)
- Case 1 (p.42): delayed nodule day 13, prezygomatic space + 2 cm posterior; capsule with mixed echogenic fluid; 6-week serial imaging; US-guided injection.
- Case 2 (p.43): STA occlusion <24 h; hyaluronidase **10 500 IU / 3 sessions**.
- Case 3 (p.47): sterile abscess 3 months post HA, preauricular; 0.7 ml aspirated; 3 ml saline + 2 ml 2% Xylocaine + 3000 IU hyaluronidase.
- Prevention (p.44–45): aspiration considered limiting (Goodman et al.); rely on continuous movement, slow injection, low extrusion force, small volumes, knowledge of safer planes; real-time colour-Doppler-guided injection "potentially the most effective way" to avoid intravascular injection. Emergency kit: hyaluronidase, nitroglycerin cream, oxygen; HBOT for severe cases.
- Retinal/ophthalmic artery occlusion → blindness via anastomoses (p.41); embolism to distant sites reported (p.41).

## Assessment

- **Quantitative density: low.** Across 121 pages the only hard soft-tissue number is TPF thickness 2–3 mm. There are no per-region skin/fat/SMAS thicknesses, no vessel depths in mm (only layer indices in the temple and "subcutaneous, within and below orbicularis oris" for labial arteries), no filler volumes, no rheology, no longevity curves, no volumetric-outcome data. Pages 85–112 (Glossary of Imaging / case atlas) are image-only and yielded nothing.
- **What it is good for:** (1) a standardized, landmark-based *zone/waypoint definition* of the face for scanning (masseter-anchored lower face, zygomatic-arch/medial-canthus midface, glabella-anchored upper face) that could be reused as region boundaries; (2) the **temporal 10-layer stack with vessel-by-layer positions**; (3) the **9-artery PSV/RI reference table**; (4) a clear qualitative description of how HA vs CaHA vs PLLA/PMMA fillers look on US and how HA integrates over months (fresh = anechoic cyst-like, later = heterogeneous), useful if the project ever uses US as ground truth for filler location; (5) the cm offsets: facial artery 1.5–2 cm lateral to oral commissure, DAO 2 cm anterior to masseter, probe 1 cm above mandibular border.
- **Regions best covered:** temple (layer stack + vessels), lower face/perioral (muscle order and labial artery relations), masseter (measurement protocol, DIT variants). **Weakest:** forehead, brow, nose, chin, jawline, neck — anatomy prose only.
- Treat this book as a source for *region definitions, vessel/layer topology and US-appearance rules*, not for thickness or volume parameters; those must come from the other books in the set.

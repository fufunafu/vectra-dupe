## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Frontalis: origin deep aspect of galea superiorly; insertion by interdigitation with orbital orbicularis and into brow dermis. Medial extent = paramedian line of forehead; lateral extent = temporal fusion line. Zone between lateral edge of frontalis and temporal fusion line = "zone of fixation". — PSCurr p.1150 (Ch. 99)
  - Frontalis is "considered a continuation of the same layer of scalp and forehead as the galea"; relates inferiorly to procerus (medial), corrugator (medial brow), orbital orbicularis (lateral). — PSCurr p.1150 (Ch. 99)
  - Supraorbital nerve exits foramen/notch "about 2.7 cm from the midline"; supratrochlear "about 1.7 cm from the midline"; corrugator occupies the space between them. A vertical line through the medial pupil (eye at rest) passes through both supraorbital and infraorbital foramina. — PSCurr p.1150 (Ch. 99); repeated p.1152 (Codner video notes)
  - Normal brow-to-frontal-hairline distance: **men 6–8 cm, women 5–6 cm**. — PSCurr p.1150 (Ch. 99)
  - Brow-lift dissection plane detail (gives a layer landmark): open approach is subgaleal/supraperiosteal until "about 3–4 cm superior to the superior orbital rim", then subperiosteal to protect the deep branch of supraorbital nerve and release the superior orbital retaining ligament. — PSCurr p.1141 (Ch. 98 case); p.1152 says "2–3 cm superior to the superior orbital rim" for the same transition (Ch. 99).
  - Structures released in any brow lift: zone of fixation just medial to temporal fusion line (galea+periosteum to bone); periosteal attachments at supraorbital rim; superior and lateral orbital retaining ligament. — PSCurr p.1151 (Ch. 99)
- **Layer thickness data:** (no data in this book)
- **Injection plane & technique:** Neurotoxin only: forehead 10–20 units (Botox). Fat "can be injected" for mild vertical radix/glabellar creases — "probably an uncommon modality with the advent of Botox". — PSCurr p.1113, p.1151
  - Case-conference diagram (Fig. 95.2, Botulinum dosage map, p.1118): forehead dots labelled 2–3 units each across forehead, 2.5 at lateral brow, glabella cluster 3/2.5/2/4; crow's feet 2/2/2/2 per side; masseter 10/10 per side; perioral/DAO 2.5, chin 2.5, 1.5/1. (Numbers are per-injection-point units; the figure is a hand-drawn map.)
- **Volumes:** (no filler volume data in this book)
- **Product/rheology:** (no data)
- **Expected surface response & longevity:** Neurotoxin: clinical effect evident at 24–72 h, duration of action 3–4 months; patients told effect "requires 2–3 weeks". Diffusion: avoid bending over for 4–6 h post-injection. — PSCurr p.1112, p.1118, p.1112
- **Danger zones:** Frontal branch of VII: "along a line drawn from 0.5 cm below the tragus to a point 1.5 cm lateral to the lateral brow"; lies on deep surface of temporoparietal fascia. Sentinel vein "1.0 cm lateral and superior to the lateral canthus; the frontal branch is 1 cm lateral to the sentinel vein." Supraorbital artery: local anesthetic bolus injected into supraorbital artery can travel retrograde into ophthalmic artery then antegrade into central retinal artery (blindness). — PSCurr p.1187 (Ch. 103), p.1152 (Ch. 99), p.1129 (Ch. 97)
- **Variation:** Transverse forehead lines = frontalis hypertonicity; deep transverse furrows indicate chronic frontalis overactivity compensating for brow ptosis/lid ptosis. Asian forehead described as "more flat in the visible central portion… rather than convex and rounded"; suggested soft-tissue enhancement with HA filler (temporary) or autologous fat. — PSCurr p.1151, p.1145–1146 (Ch. 98)

## Glabella

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Muscle layers superficial→deep in the medial brow: procerus/orbital orbicularis; depressor supercilii; oblique and transverse heads of corrugator. — PSCurr p.1150 (Ch. 99)
  - Corrugator: oblique head medial, originates medial superior orbital rim, deep to procerus, inserts dermis beneath medial eyebrow; transverse head originates similarly, inserts more laterally in middle third of eyebrow; transverse head "appears to play a larger role in moving the eyebrow medially and producing the glabellar vertical creases". — PSCurr p.1150
  - Procerus: origin nasal bones + nasal process of frontal bone, inserts dermis → transverse creases at nasal root; "probably a minimal, if at all, brow depressor". — PSCurr p.1150
  - Vertical glabellar lines: transverse head of corrugator primarily + depressor supercilii. Oblique glabellar lines: oblique head of corrugator + depressor supercilii + medial orbital orbicularis. Transverse dorsal-nasal lines: procerus. — PSCurr p.1151
  - Transpalpebral corrugator resection: oblique head lies between supratrochlear and supraorbital nerves; "perhaps a piece of fat placed into the dead space"; complication = medial forehead anesthesia/paresthesia (supratrochlear injury). — PSCurr p.1151–1152
- **Layer thickness:** (no data)
- **Injection plane & technique:** Neurotoxin 20 units glabella (Botox). — PSCurr p.1113
- **Volumes:** (no filler data)
- **Danger zones:** Blindness after filler: "injections in the nasal dorsum, glabella, or nasolabial folds" — retrograde injection from external carotid to internal carotid system, e.g. angular artery under pressure into supratrochlear then retrograde into ophthalmic and retinal arteries. Mitigation: cannula instead of needle, low-pressure injection, small bolus, aspiration before injection. — PSCurr p.1115 (Ch. 95)
- **Mobility:** Glabellar complex adducts brow medially & depresses medial brow → vertical/oblique rhytides. Post-injection ptosis from toxin migration to levator; lasts ~1 month; treated with apraclonidine tid or oxymetazoline drops (stimulate Muller's). — PSCurr p.1111–1113

## Brow

- **Anatomy/aesthetics**
  - Female brow: medial end curves slightly down, just inferior to medial supraorbital rim; apex at "approximately two-thirds laterally" (p.1141) / "junction of the middle and lateral one-third" (p.1150); vertical from apex passes just lateral to lateral limbus; vertical from medial brow passes through caruncle and lateral ala; diagonal from lateral brow end intersects at alar base — Westmore's triangle (Fig. 99.1). Lateral end of brow "1 or 2 mm higher than the medial brow". — PSCurr p.1141 (Ch. 98), p.1150 (Ch. 99)
  - Male: brows heavier, more likely ptotic; conservative lateral skin resection. — PSCurr p.1133
  - Zone of fixation = galea+periosteal fixation to bone just medial to temporal fusion line; temporal fusion line is at "the junction of the middle and lateral one-third of the eyebrow". — PSCurr p.1151
- **Aging mechanism:** Brow ptosis more marked laterally because frontalis does not extend beyond temporal fusion line; unopposed depressors (orbital orbicularis laterally; corrugator transverse head + depressor supercilii medially) + laxity of frontalis + "gravity and attenuation of the zone of adhesion lateral to the frontalis". — PSCurr p.1130 (Ch. 97), p.1151 (Ch. 99), p.1191 (Ch. 103)
  - Table 99.1 "Typical Features of the Aged Upper Face": vertical glabellar skin lines; oblique glabellar skin lines; transverse dorsal nasal skin lines; lateral eyebrow ptosis; pseudoexcess upper eyelid skin (ptotic eyebrow skin); transverse forehead skin lines. — PSCurr p.1151
- **Injection:** Mild/moderate brow ptosis "could be initially addressed with non-surgical methods, neuromodulators": inject medial inferior forehead to neutralize corrugator + depressor supercilii, and lateral orbicularis (a brow depressor). — PSCurr p.1141
- **Surgical numbers (context for layer geometry):** coronal incision 5 cm posterior to hairline; endoscopic: three short scalp incisions 5 cm behind hairline; temporal scalp incision 2–3 cm; fixation: drill hole / transosseous screw / Endotine. — PSCurr p.1152
- **Layer thickness / Volumes / Rheology:** (no data)
- **Variation:** Men brow-hairline 6–8 cm vs women 5–6 cm (see Forehead). Upper-lid blepharoplasty can unmask/cause brow ptosis (frontalis relaxation; excessive lateral skin excision pulls brow down); Hering's law for contralateral brow/lid. — PSCurr p.1130, p.1141, p.1151

## Temple

- **Anatomy/layers** (from trauma module Ch. 42 and facelift Ch. 103; no aesthetic-injection data)
  - Deep temporal fascia is single layer over temporalis superiorly; splits at "about the level of the superior orbital rim or to 4 cm superior to the palpated zygomatic arch"; the superficial temporal fat pad lies between superficial and deep layers of the deep temporal fascia, directly superior to and overlying the zygomatic arch. The middle temporal artery "can lie within the superficial temporal fat pad". Frontal branch of VII is superficial to the superficial layer of deep temporal fascia (on deep surface of temporoparietal fascia). — PSCurr p.423–424 (Ch. 42)
  - Facelift version: superior edge of superficial fat pad is "about 3–4 cm superior to the zygomatic arch". — PSCurr p.1187 (Ch. 103)
  - SMAS is NOT contiguous with temporoparietal fascia (attached at the zygomatic arch) and is NOT contiguous with parotidomasseteric fascia (that is deep cervical fascia, deep to SMAS). — PSCurr p.1185–1186
  - Superficial facial fat pads include a "forehead-temporal" pad that "actually consists of two separate pads, extending into the pre parotid and upper neck". — PSCurr p.1113 (Ch. 95)
- **Injection plane & technique:** Case plan: "Sculptra® to the temples and sunken area lateral to the temples." Fat grafting: "temporal hollowing, can be addressed". — PSCurr p.1118 (Ch. 95), p.1072 (Ch. 90)
- **Layer thickness / Volumes / Rheology / Surface response:** (no data)
- **Danger zones:** frontal branch line 0.5 cm below tragus → 1.5 cm lateral to lateral brow; sentinel vein 1.0 cm lateral+superior to lateral canthus. — PSCurr p.1187, p.1152

## Upper eyelid & periorbital

- **Anatomy/topography (young adult)** — PSCurr p.1127–1128 (Ch. 97)
  - Palpebral aperture **10 mm**; horizontal lid length **30 mm**.
  - Upper lid crease **10–12 mm** above lash margin (levator aponeurosis fibers into dermis/orbicularis); Asian palpebral fold, if present, sits "1–2 mm superior to the lash margin" (p.1146) and Asian aperture "less than 11 mm" (p.1145).
  - Lower lid crease **2–3 mm** from lash margin medially, **5–6 mm** laterally.
  - Superior tarsus height **8–10 mm**; inferior tarsus **4–5 mm**.
  - Upper lid margin sits **1–1.5 mm below the superior limbus**; lower lid margin at the inferior limbus. Lateral canthus "about 2 mm higher than the medial canthus" (varies with age/race; some Asian groups higher than 2 mm).
  - Whitnall's ligament runs horizontally "about 8–10 mm superior to the levator aponeurosis" (p.1129).
  - Lamellae: upper lid anterior = skin, orbicularis, septum; posterior = levator aponeurosis, Muller's, conjunctiva, tarsus. Lower lid anterior = skin + orbicularis; middle = septum; posterior = capsulopalpebral fascia + conjunctiva (+tarsus).
  - Upper lid fat pads lie posterior to septum; nasal (medial) pad whitish-yellow vs central/preaponeurotic more yellow; lateral pad "adds little to the prominence" and is near lacrimal gland. Retro-orbicularis fat centrally; medially fat descent "blocked by the corrugator". SOOF may extend into the upper lid in Asians. — PSCurr p.1129, p.1134
- **Ptosis grading / measurements** — PSCurr p.1130–1131 (Ch. 97)
  - Iris vertical diameter assumed **12 mm**; pupil **4 mm**. Normal upper lid at mid-pupil "at most halfway between the superior limbus and the superior edge of the 4 mm pupil, perhaps even 1 mm less". Lash margin at superior pupil edge = **2 mm ptosis** (mild–moderate); at mid-pupil = **5–6 mm** (severe). Severity: mild 1–2 mm, moderate 2–3 mm, severe ≥4 mm. MRD = mid-pupillary light reflex to upper lid margin; normal MRD "about 4 mm" (p.1190). Case p.1143: 4–5 mm = "threshold of severe ptosis".
  - Levator function: normal **15–18 mm** excursion, good 10–14 mm, <10 mm requires direct ptosis procedure (frontalis blocked with thumb). Case p.1143: upward gaze lash margin 6 mm from inferior limbus = compromised; function "7–8 mm or fair".
  - Lagophthalmos target after upper blepharoplasty: **2 mm** (p.1134) / "2–3 mm only" (p.1133).
- **Surgical geometry (useful as tissue-envelope numbers)** — PSCurr p.1133–1134, 1141, 1146 (Ch. 98)
  - Lower incision at 7–8 mm (men), 8–9 mm (women) from lash margin; superior incision ≥10 mm from inferior brow; closure ~6 mm above lash margin medially/laterally; skin excision width "approximately 10–12 mm centrally". Asian fold creation: suture through levator aponeurosis at 7–8 mm above lash margin (12 o'clock), inferior incision ~7 mm; 2–3 mm strip of orbicularis excised. Rohrich video: inferior incision "low 7 mm for added 'fracto' fat".
- **Injection plane & technique:** Periorbital filler difficult: "complex regional anatomy and thin skin that easily shows contour irregularities, color issues and inflammation, possibility of the remote but severe complication of vision loss." Plane: **suborbicularis**, 30-gauge needle or cannula, **microboluses 0.3–0.5 cc**, fan-shaped area **0.3–1 cc** total "to augment periorbital hollows". — PSCurr p.1114 (Ch. 95)
  - Supra-palpebral (sulcus) hollowing in involutional ptosis: "fat could be added"; case p.1144: autologous fat injections harvested abdominally or medial thigh. — PSCurr p.1138, p.1144
  - Nanofat used "in specific areas, such as periorbital and perioral, to achieve improvement by a more youthful appearance of the skin, independent of volume correction." — PSCurr p.1072 (Ch. 90)
- **Volumes:** 0.3–1 cc periorbital hollows (above). Hyaluronidase to dissolve HA nodule: MCQ options 25 / 15–30 / 50–100 / 100–150 units (answer not printed). — PSCurr p.1119
- **Product/rheology:** (no periorbital-specific data)
- **Surface response / Tyndall:** Filler "injected too superficially in the dermis may also create a blue discoloration via the Tyndall effect"; treat with hyaluronidase (Hylenex). — PSCurr p.1115, p.1118
- **Danger zones:** Retrobulbar hematoma: almost all within 24 h, half within 6 h; medial upper and lower fat pads most prone (infra-/supramedial palpebral vessels). Bleeding from medial fat pad = superior ophthalmic vein branches; profuse = superior branch of medial palpebral artery. Eyelid venous drainage → cavernous sinus. Ethmoidal arteries are ophthalmic (ICA) branches. — PSCurr p.1149–1150, p.1134, p.1129
- **Variation (Asian):** No dermal insertions of levator; septum inserts inferiorly/anteriorly on aponeurosis → preaponeurotic fat low in lid; SOOF may extend into upper lid; palpebral fold closer to lash margin; epicanthal folds; central fifth (intercanthal) "about 40% greater"; increased bizygomatic and bigonial width, flatter forehead, low radix. — PSCurr p.1128, p.1134, p.1145–1146
- **Aging changes list (periorbital):** brow ptosis (lateral > medial); upper lid hooding; upper lid skin laxity; prominence of medial upper fat pad; superiorly displaced palpebral fold with mild ptosis; lower lid skin laxity; lower lid fat prominence; capsuloligamentous laxity with scleral show; skeletonization of tear trough/nasojugal groove. — PSCurr p.1130 (Ch. 97)

## Tear trough & infraorbital

- **Anatomy/boundaries** — PSCurr p.1127–1128, p.1135–1136 (Ch. 97–98)
  - Arcus marginalis = fibroelastic tissue from anterior border of orbital rim/periosteum continuing into septum ("fusion of the orbital septum with the periosteum of the orbit rim"); distinct from the **orbital/orbicularis retaining ligament (ORL)**, which lies inferior: fibrous attachment of deep surface of orbicularis to periosteum of orbital rim at junction of preseptal and pre-orbital orbicularis; arises from periosteum, inserts on deep surface of orbicularis and dermis. Both limit spread of "raccoon eyes" blood into cheek.
  - Tear trough (medial) / nasojugal groove (lateral): skinfold masked in youth by overlap of inferior orbital orbicularis and superior lip of malar fat pad. Full definition: fusion of septum to inferior orbital rim at arcus marginalis + ORL running anterior and transversely from inferior orbital rim to deep orbicularis and dermis. Deformity from: inferior descent of malar fat pad, laxity of orbicularis, anterior prominence of orbital fat pads from septal laxity. — p.1128
  - "Tear trough triad": protrusion of orbital fat, skeletonization of the eyelid, accentuated by retrusive orbital rim / negative vector; increased visibility of soft-tissue attachments to rim + postseptal fat herniating over the attachment; laxity of preseptal/preorbital orbicularis contributory. Severity progresses medial→lateral with time. — p.1135
  - Lower lid septum: attaches directly to inferior tarsal border and fuses with capsulopalpebral fascia "about 4 mm" (p.1128) / "3–4 mm" (p.1128 Q7) inferior to tarsus; conjoined fascia insertion is "2–3 mm" on inferior tarsal border (p.1136). Septum immediately deep to orbicularis from infraorbital rim to tarsus.
  - Lower lid fat compartments: medial/central separated by inferior oblique fascia; central/lateral by arcuate expansion of inferior oblique; lateral compartment "usually extends considerably further laterally than anticipated" and is most likely under-dissected. — p.1135
  - SOOF (suborbicularis orbital fat): in the lower lid, posterior to orbital orbicularis but anterior to septum and bony rim (vs postseptal fat posterior to septum). — p.1129
  - Deep fat compartments per Fig. 95.1: medial and lateral sub-orbicularis oculi fat (SOOF), medial and lateral parts of deep medial cheek fat. Superficial: infraorbital fat, lateral orbital fat. — p.1114
  - Lower lid/midface aging concept: "increasing hollowness and 'skeletonization' of the lid–orbital interface with apparent increase in vertical height of the lid with a conversion of a convex to a concave configuration." Skeletal: "increase in vertical height of the orbit because of recession of the infraorbital rim, recession of the pyriform aperture, and adjacent maxilla." — p.1135, p.1188
- **Vector / globe position** — PSCurr p.1130, p.1137
  - Positive vs negative vector = most anterior globe vs lower lid margin/infraorbital rim/malar eminence; Hertel exophthalmometry **≤14 mm** ≈ positive vector (lateral canthal procedures push lid up; move fixation inferiorly); **≥18 mm** prominent globe/negative vector (lid "clothes-lines" inferiorly; fixation upward, canthoplasty rather than canthopexy).
  - Snap-back test: lid pulled **>3–4 mm** from globe with lag → lateral canthal procedure (p.1131, p.1136); **>5–6 mm** laxity → canthoplasty rather than canthopexy (p.1136). MCQ: "6 mm of tarsal laxity" is an indication (p.1147).
  - Scleral show: normal lid at limbus or "perhaps 1 mm superiorly". Post-op 2 mm scleral show: observe 7–10 days, massage at 2 weeks, release if persistent at 4–6 months. Diplopia clears in 3–6 months. — p.1131, p.1150
  - Canthopexy fixation: 2 mm posterior and 2 mm superior to a transverse line through the inferior edge of the pupil; Whitnall's tubercle landmark. Lower lid skin/muscle excision "rarely more than 3–4 mm". — p.1136, p.1135
- **Layer thickness data:** (no numeric thickness; qualitative "thin skin")
- **Injection plane & technique (filler):** Suborbicularis plane, 30-G needle or cannula, microboluses 0.3–0.5 cc; total 0.3–1 cc fan for periorbital hollows. Case: "Periorbital area—Voluma® or Restylane Lyft® in the upper cheeks laterally infraorbital region and should be placed on the bone." Sequencing: malar contouring FIRST — lifting/volumization "may also decrease the need to treat the tear trough." — PSCurr p.1114, p.1118, p.1115 (Ch. 95)
- **Fat grafting to tear trough:** "Adding a small volume, **1–2 cc**, of processed autologous fat grafts to the medial nasojugal groove may be of value" (with transconjunctival septal reset, ORL release). "Micro fat grafting of the tear trough and nasojugal groove." Micro free orbital fat grafts to tear trough (Miranda & Codner 2017) referenced. Principal facial fat-graft areas: "inferior orbital rim or nasojugal region and anterior cheek". — PSCurr p.1192 (Ch. 103), p.1141 (Ch. 98), p.1133 bibliography, p.1195 (Ch. 104)
- **Surgical release equivalents:** transconjunctival preseptal approach → release septum from arcus marginalis, divide ORL along length of infraorbital rim, septal reset (pedicled septal-orbital fat flap over rim) or free fat over rim; subperiosteal dissection into premaxillary space for severe malar mounds. Septal reset concern: vertical lid shortening from middle-lamella contracture. — PSCurr p.1135–1137, p.1192
- **Volumes:** 0.3–1 cc filler; 1–2 cc fat medial nasojugal groove.
- **Product/rheology:** Voluma / Restylane Lyft on bone laterally at infraorbital region (case). Calcium hydroxyapatite NOT for lips (perioral only stated). — p.1118, p.1115
- **Surface response & longevity:** (no numeric data) — Tyndall effect risk if superficial; nodules "usually require incision and drainage" (p.1115).
- **Danger zones:** vision loss (see Glabella); angular artery; infraorbital foramen on medial-pupil vertical line (p.1150).
- **Variation:** Asian lower lid "smooth with little or no subtarsal fold, and no tear trough is evident" at age 35 (case). — p.1145

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (deep/superficial fat compartments)

- **Anatomy/boundaries**
  - Five facial layers: (1) skin, (2) subcutaneous, (3) musculoaponeurotic, (4) subaponeurotic containing ligaments and soft-tissue spaces, (5) deep fascia = periosteum on the facial skeleton and deep muscle fasciae (deep temporal, parotid-masseteric) where bone is overlaid by masticatory structures. — PSCurr p.1113 (Ch. 95)
  - **Superficial fat pads** (Ch. 95 Q5): nasolabial, cheek, forehead-temporal, infraorbital. "The nasolabial is a fat pad that parallels the nasolabial fold, the infraorbital is located as the name implies, the cheek fat pad in the central cheek area and the forehead-temporal actually consists of two separate pads, extending into the pre parotid and upper neck." — p.1113
  - **Deep fat compartments** (Ch. 95 Q6): medial and lateral suborbicularis, medial and lateral deep cheek. — p.1113
  - **Fig. 95.1** (Cotofana, expert2expert) legend — Superficial: 1 nasolabial fat; 2 jowl fat; 3 infraorbital fat; 4 medial fat; 5 middle fat; 6 lateral orbital fat; 7 lateral temporal cheek fat. Deep: 8 medial SOOF; 9 lateral SOOF; 10 medial part of deep medial cheek fat; 11 lateral part of deep medial cheek fat; 12 facial artery; 13 transverse facial artery. "The fat compartments of the midface are distributed at two depths and are closely interrelated with many other anatomical structures … that influence facial appearance at rest and during movement." — p.1114
  - **Fig. 104.1** (Rohrich lift-and-fill 2014): High superficial malar compartment; Middle superficial malar compartment; Deep malar compartment; Deep nasolabial malar compartment. — p.1195
  - Zygomatico-cutaneous ligaments: arise from surface of zygoma/malar eminence, insert dermis. Retaining ligaments = fusion of SMAS (superficial) and parotido-masseteric fascia (deep). Safe release plane: superficial to SMAS, directly over malar eminence ("relatively free of branches of the facial nerve"). — p.1186–1187 (Ch. 103)
  - SMAS invests zygomaticus major/minor on deep and superficial surfaces; sub-SMAS dissection must go superficial at lateral border of zygomaticus major (buccal branch risk). — p.1186–1187
  - Cross-section at vertical from lateral orbital rim × horizontal from philtrum: skin, subcutaneous fat, SMAS, mimetic muscles, parotido-masseteric fascia, CN VII branches, masseter/buccal fat pad (Figs. 103.1, 103.2 — Baker/Stuzin). — p.1186
  - Mendelson video: anterior face = mobile (retaining ligaments concentrated circum-orbital and circumoral); lateral face = fixed by ligaments; "fourth space" contains vertically oriented retaining ligaments and VII; malar bulge lies between fixed ORL and the more anterior attachment at the nasolabial fold; pre-zygomatic and pre-masseter spaces. — p.1197–1198
- **Aging / volume-loss theory (qualitative)** — PSCurr p.1113, p.1188, p.1191
  - Two schools: (1) bone resorption of maxilla below infraorbital rim, pyriform aperture widening (deepens NLF), mandibular resorption (jowls); (2) deflation — "loss of the tension of the facial skin envelope secondary to some diminution of the contents of the superficial fat compartments as well as a shift in anterior–posterior volume secondary to atrophic skeletal changes"; descent theory — malar fat pad descends inferior/medial from attenuation of zygomatico-cutaneous and parotido-masseteric ligaments → loss of superior cheek prominence, accentuated nasojugal/palpebral-malar grooves, deeper NLF, oval→square face, illusion of longer face. "In all likelihood… a combination".
  - Because resorption occurs "directly beneath the periosteum, the ideal non-surgical correction is subperiosteal placement of filler materials… In fact, the non-surgical correction is the immediate supraperiosteal placement of filler materials." — p.1113
  - Combined surgery+filler concept: address deflation with soft-tissue augmentation and use SMAS manipulation "as a bridge between the superficial and deep fat compartments to improve contour." — p.1113
  - Youthful face: fullness/convexity of malar and pre-parotid regions, submalar concavity, smooth mandibular border; oval/egg shape small end down; profile = series of convexities forehead, cheek, chin — "the result of support of the fat compartments by the retaining ligaments". — p.1188
  - Fat grafting "addresses… deflation of the facial fat compartments as a result of changes in the underlying skeleton as well as perhaps fat atrophy." — p.1195
- **Layer thickness data:** (no numeric data)
- **Injection plane & technique**
  - Filler: "Cheek filler Voluma®, Restylane Lyft® — Filler goes deep onto the bone" (Fig. 95.3). Malar contouring done first in a malar/tear-trough/NLF sequence. — p.1119, p.1115
  - Fat grafting targets: "Primarily, the deep malar and the adjacent or medial deep nasolabial malar compartments"; additional lateral grafting in "the superior and middle superficial malar compartments". Anticipated volume: **"2–3 ccs at each compartment."** — p.1195 (Ch. 104)
  - Ch. 90: deep medial cheek "augmented with macro- and micrografting to enhance anterior cheek projection." — p.1072
  - Stuzin video: lipofilling "into the nasolabial folds and the deep and superficial malar fat pads and high malar fat pads" (last 4 min of Video 3). — p.1197
  - Coleman facial technique: 19 G or 17 G cannula, "injects small volumes, 0.1 cc in a 1 cc syringe"; max syringe 10 cc; multiple passes in different planes (transverse and superficial→deep) to avoid coalescence into a larger fat glob. — p.1072 (Ch. 90)
  - Fat for fine wrinkles placed "directly beneath the dermis." — p.1195
- **Volumes:** filler — no per-region mL given for cheek; fat — 2–3 cc per deep compartment (deep malar; deep nasolabial malar).
- **Product/rheology:** MCQ: patient with NLF + loss of cheek volume — "A high G prime filler would be most appropriate because: (a) Longevity (b) Product stiffness (c) Swelling factor (d) Tissue dispersion (e) Injection viscosity" (answer not printed; stiffness implied). HA products distinguished by "degree of cross-linking that results in change of viscosity, stiffness, and cohesiveness"; variables: cross-linking, concentration, particle size, viscosity. — p.1120, p.1114, p.1113
- **Expected surface response & longevity:** malar volumization "will result in lifting and volumization that may decrease the appearance of the nasolabial folds and may also decrease the need to treat the tear trough." No mm-per-mL data. Fat: undercorrection, contour irregularities, oil cyst, lumps are routine counselling items; undercorrection → repeat grafting; overcorrection → micro-liposuction. — p.1115, p.1196
- **Danger zones:** facial artery (12) and transverse facial artery (13) drawn in Fig. 95.1; zygomatic/buccal VII branches triangle: malar eminence → posterior mandibular angle → oral commissure; branches lie between superficial and deep parotid lobes and superficial to masseter but deep to parotido-masseteric fascia. Vascular arcades: medial (angular, labial, dorsal nasal), middle (infraorbital, mental), lateral (zygomatico-orbital, transverse facial). — p.1114, p.1186–1187
- **Variation:** Asian: increased bizygomatic distance/transverse width, square vs oval. Female cheeks "more prominent" (Ch. 108, qualitative). — p.1145, p.1242

## Nose

## Nasolabial fold

- **Anatomy:** Nasolabial superficial fat pad parallels the fold (1 in Fig. 95.1); deep nasolabial malar compartment (Fig. 104.1). Pyriform aperture widening/recession "leads to deepening of the nasolabial folds". SMAS attachment to mimetic muscles tethers effacement — SMAS advancement "improves the jowls but does not achieve improvement in the nasal labial fold from the modiolus to the alar base." Lateral SMASectomy vector is "parallel to that of the nasolabial fold". — PSCurr p.1114, p.1195, p.1113, p.1194

Case: "Filler into the pyriform aperture **0.1–0.2 cc** Voluma® or Restylane Lyft®" (Fig. 95.3: "Filler pyriform aperture, 0.1–0.2 cc"). Malar first — may reduce NLF need. Fat: lipofilling into NLFs (Stuzin video). — PSCurr p.1114–1115, p.1118–1119, p.1197
- **Volumes:** 0.1–0.2 cc pyriform aperture (deep bolus). NLF itself: no mL given.
- **Product/rheology:** high-G′ filler MCQ for NLF + cheek volume loss (p.1120). Sculptra nodules "usually resolve with massage". — p.1115
- **Surface response & longevity:** (no numeric data)
- **Danger zones:** blindness from NLF injection (angular artery → supratrochlear → ophthalmic/retinal); facial vessels cross mandible 2 cm lateral to commissure then ascend as facial/angular artery. — p.1115, p.1187

## Chin

"Witch's chin" from submental crease incisions adhering to mandible; submental incision 1–1.5 cm posterior to submental crease. ## Jawline & prejowl

- **Anatomy:** Mandibular-cutaneous ligaments arise from anterior body of mandible, insert dermis; attenuation → jowls "from the descent of the soft tissue overlying the anterior mandible". Jowls = all three aging components: skin/subcutaneous fat change, SMAS/platysma laxity, attenuation of mandibulo-cutaneous and masseteric ligaments. Bone: "Reduction of mandibular proportions by bone resorption results in jowling." Jowl fat = superficial compartment 2 (Fig. 95.1). — PSCurr p.1186, p.1188, p.1196, p.1113, p.1114
- **Injection plane & technique:** "Bone resorption in the angle of the mandible can also be addressed. The filler commonly employed is calcium hydroxyapatite placed supraperiosteally." — p.1115
- **Volumes / thickness / longevity:** (no data)
- **Danger zones:** Marginal mandibular nerve: in cadavers "consistently at or superior to a point one cm inferior to the angle"; in vivo more variable; parallels mandibular body until crossing facial vessels at mid-body (2 cm lateral to oral commissure) then courses superiorly. Platysma transection/myotomy start point: "1 cm posterior and 2 cm inferior to the angle of the mandible" (p.1187) / "2 cm inferior and 1 cm posterior to the angle" (p.1197). Release of mandibular-cutaneous ligaments done posterior to the vessel crossing. Facelift complication rates (sub-SMAS, nonsmoker): hematoma 1–3%, marginal mandibular paresis 1–3%, skin slough 1–3%. — p.1187, p.1194, p.1197

## Neck

## General & cross-region

### Layer-stack models
- Five layers of the face: skin / subcutaneous / musculoaponeurotic / subaponeurotic (ligaments + spaces) / deep fascia (= periosteum over skeleton; deep temporal & parotido-masseteric fascia over masticatory structures). — PSCurr p.1113 (Ch. 95)
- Lateral face cross-section (Stuzin/Baker Figs. 103.1–103.2): skin, subcutaneous fat, SMAS, mimetic muscles, parotido-masseteric fascia, CN VII, masseter/buccal fat. MCQ answer sequence: "Skin, SMAS, mimetic muscles, parotidomasseteric fascia, facial nerve." — p.1186, p.1192
- Mendelson: superficial fascia, "fourth space" with vertically oriented retaining ligaments and VII; anterior (mobile) vs lateral (fixed) face. — p.1197–1198
- Retaining ligaments (three sets): zygomatico-cutaneous (zygoma/malar eminence → dermis); mandibular-cutaneous (anterior mandibular body → dermis); parotido-masseteric-derived ligaments (condensation with superficial fascia); ORL at orbit; superior/lateral orbital retaining ligament and zone of fixation at brow. — p.1186, p.1128, p.1151
- Fat compartment nomenclature (Fig. 95.1 & Fig. 104.1 reproduced above under Midface).

### Aging-change quantitative data
- Skin histology: decreased dermal thickness, loss/fragmentation of elastin; photoaging = elastin degradation; wrinkles: animation creases (mimetic insertion), fine shallow (elastin network disruption), deep (actinic + atrophy). Also "decrease in elastin… diminished and disorganization of collagen content, and flattening of the papillary dermis… facial musculature tends to become attenuated." — p.1187–1188, p.1118
- MCQ "factors that contribute most significantly to facial aging changes": (a) mimetic tone loss (b) **deflation of facial fat compartments with attenuation of the retaining ligaments** (c) elastin loss (d) bone resorption — answer not printed; text favours combination with (b). — p.1192
- Skeletal changes: orbit vertical height increases (infraorbital rim recession); pyriform aperture recession/widening; maxillary recession; mandibular resorption. — p.1113, p.1188
- No numeric volume-loss figures (mL, %) for any compartment in this book.
- Patient age stratification: 40s = "type I or ideal candidate" (less advanced change, enhanced outcomes) vs 60s. — p.1188

### Filler product data (Ch. 95)
- Compositions: Restylane = cross-linked HA; Juvederm = "increased cross-linking and higher concentration of hyaluronic acid than" Restylane; Radiesse = calcium hydroxyapatite microspheres in gel (gel absorbed, stimulates elastin/collagen); Sculptra = poly-L-lactic acid (fibroplasia, gradual dermal volume over weeks, multiple treatments, nodules resolve with massage); Artefill/Bellafill = PMMA microspheres in gel (nonbiodegradable; inflammatory neocollagenesis; several injections); collagen = bovine, skin test, short duration. — p.1113–1114
- Physical-property variables: degree of cross-linking, concentration, particle size, viscosity → viscosity/stiffness/cohesiveness. HA mode of action: hydrophilic → increased soft-tissue volume; may induce collagen genesis. Durations: HA "intermediate", CaHA "perhaps longer", PLLA multiple treatments. — p.1113–1114
- Products named per region in case (Fig. 95.3): cheek deep on bone — Voluma, Restylane Lyft; lateral cheek subcutaneous cannula — Vollure, Contour, Versa; pyriform 0.1–0.2 cc — Voluma/Lyft; marionette subcutaneous cannula — Versa, Vollure, Lyft; lips — Versa, Juvederm Ultra, Restylane; central chin on periosteum — Voluma, Lyft; temples — Sculptra. — p.1118–1119
- G′: only the MCQ on p.1120 (high G′ for NLF + cheek volume loss). No G′ values, no lifting-capacity tables.
- Technique rules: local blocks distort anatomy; steady plunger pressure while withdrawing (else lumps); 3-D fanning, linear threading (antero/retrograde), serial puncture, cross-hatching. Sequence: malar → tear trough → NLF. — p.1114–1115
- Complications: nodules/granulomas any filler; Tyndall (superficial dermis) → I&D or hyaluronidase; intra-arterial (blanching, pain) → hyaluronidase ± heparin; blindness (dorsum/glabella/NLF). Hyaluronidase dose MCQ options 25 / 15–30 / 50–100 / 100–150 units. — p.1115, p.1119

### Fat grafting — biology and retention (Ch. 90 pp.1069–1073; Ch. 104 p.1195–1196)
- Components: adipocytes + stromal vascular fraction (SVF; site of ASCs). Steps: harvest, processing, recipient-site preparation, delivery.
- Donor site: "None" superior; abdomen and medial thigh used most. Lidocaine effect on retention: "None." Transfer as rapidly as possible.
- Harvest: adipocytes shear-sensitive; 10 cc hand syringe < vacuum or 60 cc syringe stress; high-volume suction at one-half to two-thirds of 760 mmHg. Cannula 2.7–3.0 mm or 12-gauge (2.0 mm) blunt needle for large volume.
- Processing: centrifugation (1200 rpm vs 3000 rpm — lower "may" cause less damage, unproven), Telfa rolling (least traumatic, slowest), closed washing/filtration, decantation; "conclusive evidence to dictate a preferred choice is lacking."
- Recipient site: external volume expansion (worn ~24 h/day for 2–4 weeks) increases capacity/reduces interstitial pressure; microneedling ("internal fasciotomies"). Fat-volume-to-capacity ratio: do NOT overfill to compensate for expected resorption — "Not advisable. To do so will only unfavorably affect the volume-capacity ratio." Compliance of recipient (lax, non-fibrosed, non-irradiated) determines volume per session and number of sessions.
- Survival physiology: imbibition first 24–48 h, then inosculation and vascularization. Three zones: peripheral (ASCs + adipocytes survive), regenerative (ASCs survive, adipocytes die, regenerate), central necrosis (fibrosis). **Maximal injected-fat radius ≈ 1.6 mm (graft diameter 3.2 mm)** for complete revascularization; larger → central necrosis. 16 G cannula ID 1.2 mm and 14 G 1.6 mm with 1 cc injection deliver within limits. Coleman: 19 G/17 G cannula, 0.1 cc aliquots in 1 cc syringe for face; max 10 cc syringe; multiple passes in different planes. Coleman tenets: gentle harvest, centrifugation, small transplant volumes. Rationale for small volumes: enhanced vascularization; large volumes → central necrosis.
- **Facial usable fraction: "Approximately 20%" of harvested fat can be utilized for facial fat grafting.** — p.1195
- Enrichment: PRP (labour-intensive, mixed results); SVF via collagenase (higher graft retention in animals; FDA trial-only; oncologic concern); ASCs "well-defined potential to enhance fat graft retention"; nanofat (mechanical emulsification, high SVF, little fat, no volume effect — skin quality only).
- Retention percentages: MCQ "odds of fat grafting survival" to flap-reconstructed non-irradiated breast: options 20% / 40% / 60% / 85% — answer NOT printed. No facial retention percentage stated. Breast surveillance: one-fourth had imaging, 8% biopsy (breast context).
- Facial fat-graft complications: undercorrection, contour irregularities, oil cyst, lumps; corrections listed. — p.1196
- Two objectives: structural filler for deflation; ADSC transplantation for skin texture/elasticity. — p.1195

## Assessment

**How quantitative:** Low-to-moderate. PSCurr is a residency study guide (Q&A + case conferences + MCQs), so numbers appear only where a board-style question demands one. It contains NO ultrasound/cadaver/MRI soft-tissue thickness data, NO per-region filler mL tables, NO G′/cohesivity values, NO longevity curves, NO fat-compartment volume-loss data, and NO 3D-imaging outcome data. Its value is (a) a clean, citable set of anatomical definitions (five-layer model, retaining ligaments, fat-compartment naming with two figure legends — Cotofana Fig. 95.1 and Rohrich Fig. 104.1), (b) a compact set of nerve/vessel landmark distances (cm) for danger-zone maps, (c) a handful of injection-plane rules per region (bone/supraperiosteal for cheek, pyriform, chin, angle; subcutaneous cannula for lateral cheek and marionette; suborbicularis micro-boluses for periorbital), (d) the few volumes it does give (periorbital 0.3–0.5 cc boluses / 0.3–1 cc total; pyriform 0.1–0.2 cc; fat 2–3 cc per deep malar compartment; 1–2 cc fat to medial nasojugal groove; ~20% of harvested fat usable on the face; 1.6 mm max graft radius), and (e) a rich set of eyelid millimetre measurements.

**Regions covered best:** Upper eyelid/periorbital and tear trough (Ch. 97–98: dozens of mm values — aperture, creases, tarsal heights, ptosis grades, levator excursion, snap-back thresholds, Hertel cut-offs, canthopexy geometry); Brow/forehead/glabella (muscle anatomy, nerve distances, brow-hairline distances, toxin doses); Midface fat compartments (nomenclature and fat-graft targets/volumes); Neck (platysma decussation %, incision geometry, deep fat). Weakest: lips, nose, chin, jawline, masseter, temple, preauricular (product names and plane only, no numbers).

**Best use for the project:** treat PSCurr as the source of (1) region boundary definitions and ligament anchors, (2) default injection depth per region, (3) danger-zone landmark coordinates, and (4) the eyelid metric set; look elsewhere for thickness-in-mm, mL-per-region, rheology and longevity. Its bibliographies (Cotofana 2015 midface; Braz 2015 lower face; Wong & Mendelson 2015; Ramanadham & Rohrich 2015 fat compartments; Rohrich 2014 lift-and-fill; Marten & Elyassnia 2018 "Facial fat grafting: why, where, how, and how much"; Strong/Rohrich 2024 fat grafting review; Trévidic 2022 "smiling cadavers" midface multilayering) are the papers to mine for the missing quantitative rule-set data.

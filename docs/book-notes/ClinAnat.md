## General & cross-region

### Five-layer model and fat classification (ClinAnat pp.4–5)
- Five layers: (1) skin, (2) subcutaneous layer, (3) SMAS, (4) retaining ligaments and spaces, (5) periosteum and deep fascia. (p.4)
- Superficial fat compartments named: forehead, lateral orbital, malar, nasolabial, jowl, buccal fat pad (superficial) (Fig 1.3, p.6). Deep fat: ROOF, galeal fat extension, SOOF, deep medial cheek fat, buccal fat pad (Fig 1.4, p.6).
- SMAS has three sublayers: fascial layer superficial to muscles; layer intimately associated with mimetic muscles; deep layer extensively attached to periosteum (p.5, p.22). "Innominate fascia" in temple = subSMAS fat; subSMAS fat is in the same layer as ROOF and continues anatomically to ROOF (p.5, Fig 1.6 US 18 MHz).
- **Superficial fat thickness ordering (thin → thick): radix & dorsum < supraorbital < forehead < temple < cheek < infraorbital < perioral.** Thinnest at radix/dorsum "about 1.6 mm", thickest in perioral region "5–8 mm"; thickest overall at lateral aspect of nasolabial fold and jowl (attributed to sagging). Subcutaneous thickness "could be greatly changed rapidly" at the marionette line and nasolabial fold — injector must expect abrupt depth changes. (p.4)

### Subcutaneous tissue organisation (SAT/DAT) and how filler behaves per plane (ClinAnat pp.51–53)
- Layer list superficial → deep: epidermis; dermis; superficial adipose tissue (SAT) with retinaculum cutis superficialis; superficial fascia (= galea / temporoparietal fascia / SMAS depending on region); deep adipose tissue (DAT) with retinaculum cutis profundus; deep fascia; muscle. (p.51)
- SAT: cuboid lobules, honeycomb vertical septa anchored to dermis → immobile, solid. Filler placed in SAT "can be localized and cannot spread easily", "enhance the skin lifting effect" — best plane for shaping/lifting *linear* targets (glabellar frown lines, nasolabial crease, superficial forehead crease, narrow nasal dorsum). (p.53)
- DAT / loose areolar / subSMAS plane: flat lobules, oblique loose septa, "shock absorb" gliding layer. Filler "can spread easily along the obliquely oriented loose septal structure ... separated and dissected with minimal force of compression and massage" — best for wide smooth surface lifting without lumps (mid-forehead depression, temporal hollow, cheek). (p.53)
- **Implication for volumetric modelling: SAT injection → localized projection; DAT/supraperiosteal injection → lateral spread with even surface.** (p.53)

### Aging (qualitative only) (ClinAnat pp.48–51)
- Bone: orbital rim enlarges, maxilla shortens, mandible length and height reduce; maxilla resorbs faster than mandible so chin can look protruded in old age; alveolar bone loss with tooth loss. (pp.49, 51)
- Superficial fat: droops with gravity (jowl, deepened NLF). Deep fat: relocation + atrophy (hollow cheek). Fig 1.66 marks "remarkable volume loss" areas (brown) vs protrusion/drooping (purple) — no mL numbers given. (pp.49–50)
- Retaining ligaments have two-sided ageing effect: they deepen creases at compartment borders (tear trough via ORL) while still elastic; once lax, fat protrudes (palpebromalar groove, festoon). (p.49)
- Periorbital ageing sequence: tear trough (young, ORL medial) → septal fat protrudes above ORL → nasojugal groove → crow's feet → orbital rim resorption + ORL laxity → festoon. Perioral sequence: NLF at border of upper lip & superficial malar fat → malar fat droops, cheek hollows → labiomandibular crease at DAO medial border → superficial buccal fat droops, mid-chin pit at mandibular ligament → jowl. (p.50)

### Asian vs Caucasian craniofacial (ClinAnat pp.46–48)
- Asians brachycephalic, flat face, anterolaterally protruding zygoma, pronounced mandibular angle, flat nose, shallow facial depth. Korean female head/face dims ≈96% of male; Caucasian female ≈90% of male. Max head length/height ratio Asians 80%. Epicanthal fold ~57%. Lower face ratio in Asians 1:1:0.8 vs Western 1:1:1 (p.167). Ideal nose angles: nasofrontal 115–130°, nasofacial 35–40°, nasolabial 90–110° (p.143); nasofrontal should not exceed "natural range of 140°" (p.153).

### Filler physics / rheology (sparse; no G' tables) 
- Soft, low-hydrophilic HA for pretarsal roll; hydrophilic fillers → excess volume; rigid fillers → lumpy (p.114). Tear trough: HA with "low water retention capacity" (high-water-retention HA → persistent swelling **1–2 weeks** post injection); "very low viscoelasticity" to prevent lump; no CaHA/collagen derivatives in thin skin (p.126). Nose: biphasic large-particle HA preferred over monophasic to limit intravascular mass; monophasic gel lasts longer but water absorption widens dorsum; CaHA not correctable (p.149). Chin: "hardest filler" of high viscoelasticity, 2–4 cc (p.167). Lips: soft (beginners) to moderate-hard (contour, experts), HA only (p.158). Perioral wrinkles: soft HA; alternatives CaHA hyperdilution (1:1<), PLLA, PDLLA via cannula (p.169). Earlobe: moderate filler, 0.5–1 mL/side (p.172).
- Longevity: only one number — pretarsal roll (subdermal/intramuscular) "Effect could last over 3 years with proper injection" and lasts longer than other areas (p.114). Chin filler "may lose its rigidness or migrate by the action of mentalis m. over time"; hyperactive mentalis "induces migration of filler material into superior direction, and duration of filler is prone to be short" (p.165, 167). Perioral: repeated sessions needed because area is "very dynamic" (p.169).
- Vascular safety rules (p.175–176): small volume per site; slow gentle injection; retrograde; aspirate (unreliable with high-viscoelastic filler, small needle, filler in needle lumen, small vessel); cannula larger than vessel diameter, ≥25 G general (p.147), ≥27 G NLF (p.138); Doppler US mapping pre-procedure. High-risk blindness regions: **glabella, nasolabial fold, nose** (p.173). Vulnerable vessels list: supratrochlear (glabella), supraorbital (forehead), lateral nasal (nose tip), dorsal nasal (nose), facial a. (NLF) (p.173). External compression more common than intravascular; more likely with thick, firm, immobile skin, large superficial volumes, post-surgical fibrosis (p.172).

### Facial artery global pattern (ClinAnat pp.36–41)
- Facial a. continues to angular a. in only **36.3%** of 91 Asian hemifaces (French 4%, Japanese 12%, Turkish 22%, British 68%). Symmetry only **30%**. Angular a. is terminal branch of facial a. in 51% (Fig 1.51 patterns 23/18/32/26%). (pp.37, 39)
- Branching types: Type I nasolabial 51.8% (Ia 37.0, Ib 14.8); Type II nasolabial with infraorbital trunk 29.6% (IIa 16.7, IIb 12.9); Type III forehead pattern 18.6% (IIIa 16.7, IIIb 1.9). (p.39, Fig 1.52 p.41)
- **Exposed segment** of facial a. (no muscle cover, coiled) ~**15 mm lateral to cheilion**, present in most cases (p.37, p.137–138). Facial a. deeper than platysma/DAO in lower face; between risorius and ZMj at buccal fat pad (p.137).
- Facial vein: at midface located deeper than facial artery and more lateral, less tortuous; lacks valves; connects to superior ophthalmic v. and cavernous sinus (pp.40, 44).
- Superficial temporal a.: transverse facial branch ~2 cm below zygomatic arch; frontal/parietal bifurcation ~**37 mm superior and 18 mm anterior to tragus**; frontal branch single 94.8%, double 5.2% (p.40).
- Ophthalmic a. emerging point: **14 mm above and 4 mm medial to medial canthus, 5 mm below skin surface** (p.35, Fig 1.46 US). Supraorbital a. deep branches pierce frontalis to become superficial **25.0–32.5 mm above orbital rim** (p.35). Supratrochlear a.: superficial branch in every case, deep branch in 55% (p.36).
- Midline forehead vessels: superficial subcutaneous vessels at forehead midline in 34–50% of specimens (p.36); arterial branches within subcutaneous layer at midline 52–60%; veins in subcutaneous layer at glabella 96.7%; midline vessels within 1.5 cm of midline (p.104).
- Intercanthal vein: present at glabella/radix in 71% (p.40); "observed in every case" per p.154; located subcutaneous, superficial to procerus; Type I midsagittal 29.3%, IIa above intercanthal line 63.4%, IIb below 7.3% (p.155); above/below ICL combined 70.7% (p.154).

## Forehead

- **Anatomy/boundaries:** hairline (trichion) to eyebrows; layers skin – subcutaneous – frontalis + galea aponeurosis – loose connective tissue – periosteum – frontal bone (Fig 3.3 p.101). Natural wide concavity between frontal eminence and superciliary arch; metopion = midpoint between frontal eminences (p.100). Frontalis lateral margin beyond superior temporal line in ~84%, ~1 cm lateral to it (p.10). Aponeurotic (thin, tough) part of frontalis occupies upper one-third of forehead (p.106). Forehead fat compartment (Fig 1.3). Superior temporal septum → temporal ligamentous adhesion at lateral third of eyebrow, 10 mm above supraorbital margin, 20 mm high × 15 mm wide (p.23).
- **Layer thickness:** skin 1.70 ± 0.71 mm; superficial fat 1.99 ± 1.21 mm (Table 1.1 p.6). Overall soft tissue thickness of forehead **4.3–5.3 mm, mean 4.7 ± 0.3 mm**; frontalis lies **2.0–3.3 mm** beneath skin (p.10). "Covering soft tissue is thin ... (<2 mm)" in the filler chapter (p.101). Subcutaneous fat "relatively lower than other parts of the face" (p.101).
- **Injection plane & technique:** supraperiosteal / loose areolar plane deep to frontalis "strongly recommended" — vessels (supraorbital, supratrochlear, frontal br. STA) run through/above frontalis; plane allows easy spread and smooth contour; fewer vertical septa (p.102). Cannula (flexible 25 G) with nerve block (SON/STN; ZTN for lateral, often incomplete → local infiltration), entry at forehead–temple border, or >1 cm from midline; entry made directly on periosteum, advance along supraperiosteal layer (p.102–104). Must track cannula tip (not shaft) as forehead curvature shifts tip into muscle/subcutaneous layer — plan multiple entries (p.108). Needle alternative: touch bone, small bolus per site, compress and massage, no nerve block needed (p.106). Cover transition mid-forehead → upper forehead → hairline to avoid step deformity (p.106, 109).
- **Volumes:** (no data in this book).
- **Product:** (no data).
- **Surface response:** deep plane → smooth wide spread; thick soft frontalis at mid-forehead "may swell with filler" while aponeurotic upper third stays flat → demarcation (p.106). Large lateral volumes → engorged superficial temporal veins (p.107).
- **Danger zones:** supraorbital a./n. at foramen; supratrochlear a. more superficial than supraorbital at same height (p.107); midline subcutaneous vessels 52–60% (p.104); frontal branch STA crosses lateral frontalis border ~2 cm above lateral eyebrow (p.103); deep branch of supraorbital n. runs just superficial to periosteum from foramen to scalp (p.102). Skin necrosis from intravascular or extravascular compression (p.107).
- **Variation:** Asians brachycephalic → desire convex forehead (p.100). Asian frontalis dosing lower; narrow forehead <4 cm vs >6 cm (toxin) (p.66).

## Glabella

- **Anatomy:** frown lines just medial to medial eyebrow, **1–1.5 cm superior to frontal notch** (p.102). Procerus deeper at glabella than sellion (**3.8 vs 2.9 mm** below skin); width sellion 11.0 mm → glabella 14.5 mm; thickness 1.6 → 1.1 mm (p.12). Corrugator (Koreans) arises 16 mm above intercanthal plane, 4–14 mm from midline; inserts into frontalis/dermis 30 mm above HL, 16–35 mm from midline; max vertical length 15 mm (Korean) vs 21 mm (Caucasian); oblique belly narrow-vertical 63% / broad-triangular 37% (p.67). Frowning pattern: general 81%, upward (frontalis) 19% (p.66).
- **Layer thickness:** skin "exceptionally thin" (p.6); supraorbital skin 1.67 ± 0.83, fat 1.82 ± 1.22 mm (Table 1.1).
- **Injection plane:** general depression → supraperiosteal, >1 cm from orbital rim, needle tip on periosteum (p.102). Deep static lines → subdermal superficial fat with multiple small aliquots along the wrinkle, slow (p.107, 109); fine wrinkles intradermal only (p.109). Cannula "highly recommended" due to blindness risk; conventional midline entry discouraged (p.103–104).
- **Volumes:** (no data).
- **Danger zones:** supratrochlear branches already superficial (through/above muscle) at glabellar depression (p.102); at glabella centre vessel runs superficially so deep supraperiosteal plane is safe (p.102); intercanthal vein subcutaneous 96.7% veins (p.104); ophthalmic a. emerging point 14 mm above/4 mm medial to medial canthus, 5 mm deep (p.35). Glabella = top blindness risk region (p.173).

## Brow / Upper eyelid & periorbital (sunken eye, pretarsal roll)

- **Anatomy:** upper eyelid skin thinnest of face **0.38–0.80 mm** (p.5). ROOF is deep fat continuous with temple subSMAS fat (p.5, 115). Asian upper lid: septal fat extends to mid/lower tarsus, often no levator–skin connection (p.109). Sunken eye from congenital or senile orbital fat atrophy + orbital enlargement (p.110).
- **Injection (sunken eye):** plane **above orbital septum, under orbicularis oculi** (submuscular), entry at lateral end of sunken area, 27–30 G cannula, slow retrograde, small amounts, **under-correct** (over-correction → puffy) (p.111). Cadaver validation Fig 3.20.
- **Injection (pretarsal roll):** intramuscular OOc, not on tarsus, not subdermal (Tyndall); needle 30–33 G, lower lid divided into five segments, shape with rod; keep near lash line; low-hydrophilic soft HA (p.111–114). Inferior medial palpebral a. (ophthalmic branch) present "in nearly all the cases", **~1 mm diameter at medial canthus** → intravascular risk if deep at tarsus (p.111).
- **Longevity:** pretarsal roll "could last over 3 years" (p.114).
- **Danger:** ophthalmic a. emergence at medial septal fat, vessels "may be thick", communicates with central retinal a. (p.114).
- **OOc thickness (from toxin chapter):** lateral OOc ~**1.2 mm** thick at lateral canthus level (US); lateral canthus → lateral OOc edge **3.1 cm** in Asians; lateral/medial muscular bands present 54%/66% of Asians (p.12, 62).

## Temple

- **Anatomy/boundaries:** temporal fossa; clinically the "temple" sits slightly in front of pterion (p.115). Layers: skin – subcutaneous – superficial temporal fascia (STF/temporoparietal, carries STA & STV, continuous with SMAS) – subSMAS fat (innominate; contains temporal branches of facial n.; continuous with ROOF; "no important vascular structures") – deep temporal fascia (DTF; splits at superior orbital rim level into superficial & deep layers enclosing temporal fat pad and middle temporal vein) – temporalis – periosteum (p.115–116). Superficial DTF crosses zygomatic arch to ZMj origin and continues as parotideomasseteric fascia (p.22, 116, Fig 1.28 US).
- **Layer thickness:** skin 1.65 ± 0.91; superficial fat 2.58 ± 1.68 mm (Table 1.1). "Thickness from the skin to the temporal bone is quite variable" (p.121). Temporalis: upper half above arch is muscle belly only; lower half (two/three finger widths) is tendon; highest tendon point 45 mm above zygomatic arch (p.11, 94).
- **Injection planes — four options (Fig 3.33 p.118):** (1) between STF and DTF (subSMAS fat) — cannula, avoid STA/STV and frontal br. facial n.; (2) between superficial and deep DTF (temporal fat pad) — contains middle temporal vein ~**20 mm** ("one finger width") / "30–35 mm" above zygomatic arch (p.117 says 30–35 mm; p.121 says ~20 mm), aspirate; (3) between DTF and temporalis — relatively avascular potential space, cannula parallel to muscle, massage; (4) between temporalis and periosteum — needle to bone, bevel-down, 45° oblique, 3–4 points, press after each; **author's preferred plane** (p.118–121). Deep plane needs **larger volume** than superficial because tight DTF resists lifting (p.120). No safe zone at eyebrow level (DTAs anteroposterior within muscle); DTA absent 1 cm behind zygomatic tubercle (p.117, Fig 3.32 vascular-free zone).
- **Volumes:** (no data).
- **Danger:** STA pulse in front of auricle; STV; middle temporal v. (drains sentinel, inferior palpebral, periorbital vv. → cavernous sinus backflow risk) (p.119, 121); deep temporal a./v. within muscle; thin temporal bone — do not press (p.121). Reported blindness cases (p.118). Undulations common due to thin skin → massage (p.121).
- **Toxin data:** temporalis 20–40 U in 4–6 points, upper half; ≥45 mm above arch (p.93–94).

## Tear trough & infraorbital (tear trough, nasojugal groove, palpebromalar groove)

- **Anatomy/boundaries:** tear trough = medial third below eye, border between thin lower-lid skin and thick midface skin; 2–3 ORL fibres pinch OOc to bone; ORL medial part firm to deep OOc, lateral part loose/elongated septum; unclear dermal attachment (p.124–125). Nasojugal groove = medial canthus → anterior zygoma, may extend to midcheek furrow (Indian band); palpebromalar groove along lower lateral orbital rim (p.128, 132). Anterior zygomatic layers: skin – superficial malar fat – OOc – SOOF – LLS/Zmi – periosteum with infraorbital n. (p.128, Fig 4.9). OOc separates malar fat into superficial and deep (SOOF); zygomatic cutaneous ligaments along OOc lower margin support upper SOOF; malar fat divided into upper/lower by nasojugal groove (p.128). Infraorbital foramen: upper third of line infraorbital margin→ala, medial to pupil–mental foramen line (p.45). LLS originates 8–10 mm below infraorbital margin (p.16).
- **Layer thickness:** infraorbital skin 1.97 ± 0.84 mm; superficial fat **4.93 ± 2.98 mm** (Table 1.1). Tear trough etiologies include skin thickness difference above vs below rim and different subcutaneous quantity (p.3).
- **Injection plane:** two-layer principle: (1) deep into SOOF / prezygomatic space above LLS & Zmi (not just supraperiosteal) for volume; (2) subdermal above OOc for finish — tear trough is the exception where superficial-to-OOc filler gives better results (p.126, 129). Cannula: entry 1 cm from end of tear trough; 23–27 G for SOOF, 30 G for superficial (p.126). Nasojugal: entry ~1 cm below groove end, at intersection of lateral-canthus vertical and alar-base horizontal; fill central deepest point first then above/below (p.129). Palpebromalar: entry 0.5 cm from lateral canthus, 30 G cannula, deep to internal aspect of orbital septum first, then just above OOc if insufficient (p.133). Intraoral alternative for nasojugal: 23 G cannula from vestibule at first premolar, supraperiosteal to lower infraorbital space, **0.3–0.5 cc HA** retrograde, antibiotics (p.136).
- **Volumes:** 0.3–0.5 cc (intraoral nasojugal) (p.136); otherwise no data.
- **Product:** HA only, low water retention, very low viscoelasticity; no CaHA/collagen (p.126, 129).
- **Surface response:** high-water-retention HA → persistent swelling 1–2 weeks (p.126).
- **Danger:** inferior palpebral v. and angular v. near trough (p.124); **detoured infraorbital trunk of facial a. (Type II) in ~30% of Asians** runs along nasojugal groove along inferomedial border of medial muscular band of OOc then turns medially over infraorbital area (Fig 4.20 with V1/V2/HL grid; distances only in figure) (p.129, 135).

## Midface: malar & medial cheek (hollow cheek, deep compartments)

- **Anatomy:** cheek layers skin – subcutaneous fat – facial mm./SMAS – buccal fat pad – buccinator – mucosa (p.139, Fig 4.27 coronal/transverse). Buccal fat pad (Bichat) = central body + extensions (pterygopalatine, temporal, buccal lobes); "central body and its extensions ... make up **50% of the total cheek fat**"; atrophies with age; supported by risorius (p.139). Deep medial cheek fat listed in Fig 1.4; NLF deepening involves "atrophy of deep medial cheek fat" (p.133). Zygomatic ligament (McGregor's patch) posterior to Zmi origin, arch→skin (p.23). Zygomatic cutaneous ligament from zygoma periosteum along OOc lower margin to skin — malar mound/baggy lower eyelid when lax (p.23).
- **Layer thickness:** cheek skin 1.85 ± 1.03; superficial fat 4.54 ± 2.71 mm (Table 1.1). Infraorbital muscle depth increases medial→lateral, upper→lower (p.20).
- **Injection plane (hollow cheek):** two possible layers: subcutaneous fat, or buccal fat pad; buccal-fat injection "requires a large volume", "not usually recommended except in cases of the severe sunken cheek", and may aggravate jowl due to dense compact fat (p.139). Target area: NLF → masseter, lower zygomatic arch → sagging point; cannula entry 1 cm lateral and 2 cm below cheilion (p.142). Cadaver Fig 5.25.
- **Volumes:** (no data). **Product:** degradable HA advised; CaHA possible (p.142).
- **Danger:** facial vein in subcutaneous plane with buccal fat (Fig 4.27); exposed facial a. (see NLF).
- **Variation:** Asians favor plump cheeks; hollow cheek accentuates zygomatic arch (p.139).

## Lateral cheek & zygomatic arch (subzygomatic depression)

- **Anatomy:** masseteric cutaneous ligament (false) from inferior zygomatic arch to anterior masseter border, attaches to SMAS/skin; laxity → SMAS sag over masseter (p.24, 142). Zygomatic arch skin is thick (>2 mm at zygion) (p.5). Midcheek furrow correlates with premasseteric retaining ligaments (p.3). Superficial DTF crossing arch (p.22).
- **Injection plane:** subcutaneous fat above masseter; region bounded by arch (upper), middle ½ masseter (lower), masseter anterior/posterior borders; entry at intersection of lateral-canthus vertical and cheilion–tragus line; gradation from deepest point below arch to mid-masseter (p.142–143). Volume increase "difficult, due to the dense connective tissue"; massive filler → undulations/bulging on animation; option to release fascia with sharp needle (bruising, parotid duct risk) (p.143).
- **Volumes:** (no data). **Product:** HA safest; CaHA possible (p.143).
- **Danger:** parotid duct; transverse facial a. ~2 cm below arch (p.40).

## Preauricular

- Preauricular lines defined (p.4); platysma-auricular fascia below lobule where lateral temporal-cheek fat and postauricular fat merge (p.24). Earlobe augmentation: HA, 27–30 G needle, 2–3 points, **0.5–1 mL per side**, moderate filler, aspirate; posterior auricular a. supply (p.171–172). Otherwise (no data).

## Nose

- **Anatomy:** layers skin – superficial fatty layer – fibromuscular layer (SMAS) – deep fatty layer – perichondrium/periosteum (Fig 4.34, 4.42 p.146, 151). Rhinion thinnest dorsum skin; ala/tip skin ~2.0 mm (p.5, 143). Radix/dorsum superficial fat thinnest of face ~1.6 mm; Table 1.1 skin 1.51 ± 0.55, fat 1.61 ± 1.07 mm (p.4, 6). Asians: thicker skin, abundant subcutaneous tissue (masks undulations but raises intravascular risk in subcutaneous layer) (p.143). LLSAN 100%, LLS 90%, Zmi 28% attach to ala (p.13, 145). Radix in Asians sits on intercanthal line (p.146).
- **Vessel depth (key):** at upper/bony dorsum dorsal nasal a. runs **superficial fatty layer, superficial to procerus/fibromuscular layer** (few branches supraperiosteal); at lower/cartilaginous dorsum it runs **deep, beneath fibromuscular layer / deep fatty layer** (p.36, 53, 145). Communicating branches between bilateral dorsal nasal aa. 78%; one-side dorsal nasal a. crossing midline 21.6% of Asians; thick tortuous ophthalmic branch along lateral dorsum sometimes (p.145). Lateral nasal a. (facial) supplies tip; columellar branches of superior labial a. enter via columellolabial junction at deep fatty layer near medial crus, then branch into both superficial and deep fat toward tip (p.145–146). Intercanthal vein subcutaneous at radix (p.154).
- **Injection plane:** dorsum: cannula (≥25 G) supraperichondrial/supraperiosteal from tip entry, retrograde column radix → supratip break, then flanking columns; **relocate radix 3–5 mm superior**; tip only if augmenting tip (p.146–147). Needle allowed only supraperiosteal at radix (30 G), with aspiration (p.147, 153). Severe hump: dual entry (tip cannula + glabellar needle) (p.154). Columella: deep subcutaneous, entry infratip, **0.2–0.5 cc**; nasolabial angle: deep between medial crus and anterior nasal spine, target >90° (p.151, 153). Ch.1 adds: DAT beneath nasal muscles = safe high-volume plane; subdermal SAT = sharp defined outline plane, but at bony dorsum superficial vessels contraindicate superficial injection (p.53).
- **Volumes:** columella 0.2–0.5 cc (p.153); otherwise none.
- **Product:** biphasic large-particle HA; avoid monophasic (widening) and CaHA (p.149).
- **Danger:** blindness via dorsal nasal/lateral nasal → ophthalmic; many Korean cases after nose filler, arterial injection most common cause (p.173); intercanthal vein puncture → filler to cavernous sinus via angular/superior ophthalmic v. (cadaver case) (p.173–174).

## Nasolabial fold

- **Anatomy/boundaries:** alar groove/alar-facial crease down lateral upper lip; medial boundary of malar fat pad fixed by cutaneous insertions of LLSAN, LLS, Zmi and ZMj (p.133). NLF is a "natural component", deepens with malar fat sag, deep medial cheek fat atrophy (p.133). Modiolus in Asians **11.0 ± 2.6 mm lateral, 8.9 ± 2.8 mm inferior to cheilion**, below intercheilion line (58.4%) vs Caucasian on/above (p.17, 80).
- **Layer thickness:** superficial fat thickest at lateral aspect of NLF (perioral 5.14 ± 3.31 mm); abrupt thickness change across the fold (p.4, 6).
- **Facial artery relative to NLF (Asians):** runs close to NLF in **93.3%**; **3.2 ± 4.5 mm lateral to ala**; **13.5 ± 5.4 mm from cheilion**; nasal division medial to NLF 42.9%, lateral 23.2%, crossing 33.9% (Fig 4.19: 42.9/23.2/19.6/14.3%); within 5 mm of NLF in 43%; traverses slightly medial to NLF in ~77% (p.133, 135). In ~56% (nasolabial 37% + forehead 18.6%) superior labial/lateral nasal a. exposed over OOr at medial NLF (p.135). Superior labial a. branches within a **1.5 cm square superolateral to cheilion**, runs under OOr with **minimum depth 3 mm** superior to vermilion border (p.138, 162).
- **Injection plane:** think isosceles triangle (base ala; fill the lower sunken side). (1) canine fossa supraperiosteal volume first; (2) retrograde from entry 1–5 mm medial of fold; (3) medial NLF: superficial to OOr; (4) dermal for fine wrinkles (p.136, Fig 4.22b: deep + superficial planes). Ch.1: SAT plane localizes filler for crease (p.53). Cannula ≥27 G, entry 1 cm from NLF end or 1 cm lateral + 1 cm below cheilion, tip first to upper medial near nose then retrograde; cannula 23–27 G by product (p.138). Avoid lateral to cheilion (exposed segment) (p.136).
- **Volumes:** (no data). **Product:** (no data).
- **Danger:** two zones — furcation of superior labial a. (green box) and exposed facial a. segment (purple) (Fig 4.25 p.139); hematoma "acorn or chestnut" sign; NLF = blindness-risk region (p.137, 173).
- **Toxin note:** LLSAN 1 cm lateral to ala for gummy smile (p.73).

## Lips & perioral

- **Anatomy:** vermilion border, Cupid's bow, philtrum, cheilion; zones cutaneous / intermediate (red) / dry mucosa / wet mucosa (p.158). **Lip thickness at vermilion border (Asians): upper 9.4 ± 0.4 mm, lower 10.9 ± 0.7 mm** (p.158). OOr pars peripheralis + pars marginalis (p.14, Fig 1.17 US). Labial arteries in **wet mucosal layer** in upper 35–57% / lower 28–55%, deeper than OOr (p.158). Superior labial a. under OOr, min depth 3 mm above VB, then below VB near Cupid's bow; columellar branches from it (p.138, 162). Inferior labial a.: 1–3 branches, rarely parallel to VB, branches ~1.5 cm inferior to lower lip, passes middle ½ of DAO, runs along mental crease between DAO and DLI (p.160, 163); rarely from submental a. (p.166).
- **Layer thickness:** perioral skin 1.82 ± 0.83; superficial fat **5.14 ± 3.31 mm** (thickest region, 5–8 mm) (p.4, 6). Risorius 20–50 mm lateral to cheilion, 0–15 mm below intercheilion line (p.16, 91).
- **Injection plane:** wrinkles → small soft filler into dry mucosa; VB/philtrum definition → dermal/subdermal along VB and philtrum (cadaver Fig 5.6 subdermal), 1–3 sessions; volume → **submucosal or intramuscular below mucous portion**, dry–wet junction increases volume and everts; avoid deep muscle from wet mucosa (labial aa.); approach from dry not wet mucosa; corner lift = volume slightly medial of upper corner + lower corner + upper marionette; cannula for volume (entry near commissure), needle for points (p.158–160). Deep vertical lines → dermal per wrinkle ± toxin (p.159). Perioral wrinkles (skin): hydrolifting subdermal multipoint 1 cm apart, small needle or cannula; large volumes make jowl more noticeable (p.168–169).
- **Volumes:** (no data). **Product:** soft HA (beginners), moderate-hard for contour; HA only (p.158); perioral soft HA / CaHA hyperdilution / PLLA / PDLLA (p.169).
- **Longevity/mobility:** perioral very dynamic → repeated sessions (p.169); toxin combination (p.159).
- **Danger:** superior labial a. if VB injected too deep; 1.5 cm radius around facial→superior labial division; exposed facial a. at lateral OOr border; subdermal capillary bruising (p.160, 169).
- **Variation:** Asian modiolus lower → "Mona Lisa" smile (p.80); Asian lower face ratio 0.8 (p.167).

## Marionette & oral commissure

- **Anatomy:** labiomandibular crease modiolus → mandibular border, DAO defines borders; marionette line = static vertical line from cheilion caused by "abrupt decrease in subcutaneous tissue medial to the DAO" + alveolar bone loss (p.3). Jowl forms as superficial buccal fat + buccal fat pad droop while mandibular ligament holds → prejowl sulcus; DAO lateral/posterior borders become pronounced; deep buccal fat drooping identifiable on US (p.169). DAO inferior width 3.6 cm avg, origin 1.5 cm lateral to symphysis; DAO "thinnest muscle" (p.77, 82). Mandibular retaining ligament: mandible periosteum under DAO origin → skin (p.24).
- **Layer thickness:** subcutaneous thickness changes abruptly at marionette line (p.4); jowl among thickest superficial fat (p.4).
- **Injection plane:** medial portion of wrinkle "across multiple layers", sufficient volume deep → superficial, cross-hatching, needle or cannula; + DAO toxin (p.169). Jowl: fill prejowl sulcus and behind jowl for smooth jawline, **minimum volume** ("too much volume ... can aggravate sagging"); severe → lifting/fat removal/fat-dissolving under US (p.171).
- **Volumes:** minimal; no numbers. **Product:** (no data).
- **Danger:** facial a./v., inferior labial a./v., mental a./v.; avoid antegonial notch (facial a. branch point) and mental foramen (2 cm below commissure on pupil line, p.45) for cannula entry (p.171).

## Chin

- **Anatomy:** layers skin – subcutaneous – mentalis – mandible; "not strictly aligned"; medial mentalis fibres cross to form dome with fat/ligament-filled vacant space between fibres (p.164). Mentalis lies **6.7–10.6 mm** below skin (mean depths) (p.83); Asian mentalis length 1.8 cm, width 1.1 cm; origin 2 cm below intercheilion line, 0.45–1.3 cm (avg 0.98) lateral to midline; insertion width ~0.97 cm; dome-type 86.4% (merging 47.7, separate 38.6), flat 13.6% (p.84). Chin skin ~2.0 mm, >2 mm at pogonion (p.5). Ricketts line: Asian ideal aligns nose–lower lip VB (p.167).
- **Injection plane:** supraperiosteal (least vascular; hard filler; "copious amounts"), intramuscular, or subcutaneous; needle usual, cannula for wide area, centre→lateral or lateral→centre; 2–3 sessions if tissue tension high (tissue expansion) (p.164–165). Mild retrusion with ample central superficial fat → small volume effective; severe retrusion often lacks superficial fat → subcutaneous injection gives rugged surface and leaks from entry → deep supraperiosteal large volume hard filler; lateral chin thin fat → rugged risk (p.165). Below chin tip → leaks onto platysma via mentalis spaces; aim needle to anterior mandible instead (p.165–166). Extend to mandibular angle to avoid prejowl dent (p.165).
- **Volumes:** **2–4 cc** of hardest filler (p.167); split over 2–3 sessions if space insufficient (p.167).
- **Product:** hard, high viscoelasticity HA (p.164, 167).
- **Surface response/longevity:** hyperactive mentalis migrates filler superiorly and shortens duration; filler loses rigidity/migrates over time; combine toxin; can migrate along loose neck tissue (p.165, 167–168). Massage for symmetry (p.167).
- **Danger:** mental a. (foramen), inferior labial a.; "relatively safe"; needle bruising (p.166).
- **Toxin:** mentalis 3–4 U/side 1 cm lateral to midline at lower border + 1–2 U 1–2 cm above (p.85).

## Jawline & prejowl

- Chin augmentation may dent prejowl; inject chin tip and extend toward mandibular angle (cannula, Fig 5.16) (p.165). Prejowl sulcus anterior border marks mandibular retaining ligament (p.4). Minimum volume for prejowl/jawline (p.171). Facial v. runs superficial on mandible unlike facial a. (p.40). Platysma posterior fibres 17 mm from mandibular angle; platysma insertion height at alare level (p.21). Otherwise (no data).

## Masseter & mandibular angle (toxin chapter, CT data)

- Masseter three layers (superficial largest; thickest at lower portion); CT (65 Chinese): hypertrophy thickness **1.3 cm** vs normal **0.8 cm**; 3D-CT 28 Korean women: lower-border depth **34.7 ± 3.2 mm (25.0–40.0)**, thickness **14.9 ± 2.2 mm (10.6–20.1)** (p.88). **Skin-to-mandible depth in masseteric region 15–25 mm**; deep inferior tendons 2–5 mm from mandible; DIT divides superficial part transversely 35%, longitudinally 38.3%; upper masseter deep tendons continuous ~70%, capsule 45.5%, interdigitation 9.1% (p.88, 90). Risorius over masseter surface (20–50 mm lateral to cheilion); toxin 10–40 U/side, 20 U for 3–5 cm width, injection points below lobule–cheilion line, within 1 cm of mandibular border (p.89, 91). Parotid 30 U/side; submandibular 20–30 U (p.96). Transverse facial a. ~2 cm below arch (p.40).

## Neck

- Platysma: subcutaneous, no fibres in superficial mid-neck; decussation in Asians 85% (43% >20 mm below chin); non-decussation Caucasian 39% vs Asian 15% → fewer "gobbler necks" (p.21, 87). Toxin 2 U/point, 1.5–2 cm apart, 10–20 U per band, ≤50 U (p.87). Filler: chin filler can migrate along loose neck tissue (p.168). Otherwise (no data).

## Assessment

- **Quantitativeness:** moderate. Strong on *anatomy metrics* (Table 1.1 skin/superficial fat by region; forehead soft tissue 4.7 mm; frontalis depth 2.0–3.3 mm; procerus depth 2.9/3.8 mm; mentalis depth 6.7–10.6 mm; masseter skin-to-bone 15–25 mm; lip thickness 9.4/10.9 mm; artery distances/percentages for facial, angular, superior labial, ophthalmic, STA; vessel *layer* per region). **Essentially no data on injection volumes per region (only chin 2–4 cc, columella 0.2–0.5 cc, intraoral nasojugal 0.3–0.5 cc, earlobe 0.5–1 mL), no rheology tables (G'), no longevity curves (one qualitative "over 3 years" for pretarsal roll), no fat-compartment volume-loss numbers, no 3D imaging outcome data.**
- **Best-covered regions:** forehead/glabella (planes + vessel depths), temple (four-layer plane model), tear trough/nasojugal (two-layer SOOF/subdermal rule, detoured facial a. 30%), nasolabial fold (facial a. topography in mm and %), nose (vessel depth changes bony vs cartilaginous dorsum), lips (thickness, artery layer), chin (mentalis depth, hard filler 2–4 cc, migration behaviour), masseter (CT thickness).
- **Most useful for the scanner project:** Table 1.1 layer thicknesses; SAT-vs-DAT spread rule (localized projection vs lateral spread); per-region injection plane (supraperiosteal for forehead/glabella/temple deep/chin/canine fossa; SOOF for infraorbital; subcutaneous for hollow cheek and subzygoma; submucosal/intramuscular for lips); mobility warnings (mentalis migration, perioral dynamics); Asian-specific vessel and modiolus positions.
